Part Two · The Internal Battle
IX
Like Hot Sugarcane Juice

Our spiritual society, as my supreme preceptor Śrīla Prabhupāda taught us, is not a place of blind dogmas or silent, passive followings. It is a “hearing and questioning society.” An intelligent class of human beings must hear, and from that hearing, they must ask deep questions. In the religions of the world, you go and the priest speaks, and you leave without any questions—no questions are allowed. But our process is built on perfect questions and perfect answers.

I remember a beautiful, sacred session where many sincere souls gathered and laid their cards on the table, asking their deepest doubts, their struggles, and their pain. Let us enter into those questions directly, without any cosmetics, and let the truth speak.

✦

Valuing the Rare Gift of Devotion

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness is undoubtedly very difficult, why would anyone willingly accept it?

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness were cheap, I would not follow it. I tell you this straight. Why? Because anything which is cheap has no value, and anyone can follow it.

Look at the material world. If you go to the market, the vegetable shop is crowded, packed with people shouting and pushing. But if you walk down the street to the diamond shop, how many people do you see inside? Very few. Why? Because a diamond is costly. The value, the quality, is of a completely different grade.

Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the ultimate diamond; it can never be a cheap process. It is difficult because when you achieve perfection in this path, your material existence is terminated for eternity, and you return back home, back to Godhead, never to return to this world of misery again. Because we have been deviant and have misbehaved in millions of ways since time immemorial, we have a massive mountain of dirt to clean. And that purification process is not cheap—it is difficult.

So when we say Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has made the process “easy,” what do we mean? We mean that if you compare bhakti-yoga to other paths like Ashtanga Yoga, bhakti is a walk in the park. Do you have any idea what Ashtanga Yoga requires? You have to go to the forest, tie your leg to a tree, and hang upside down with your head on the ground for years and years. How many of us can do that? None of us! Compared to that brutal, agonizing physical torture, chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa is wonderfully sweet and easy.

But make no mistake: to chant the Holy Name with pure, undivided attention is not a simple, mechanical trick. If it were easy, we would not be standing here pretending. We would have tears pouring from our eyes every single morning.

When pure devotees saw the Deities on the altar, they did not just stand there politely checking their watches. When Śrīnivāsācārya saw Lord Govinda, he fainted on the spot, completely unconscious in ecstatic love. When Sañjaya saw the peacock fan of Kṛṣṇa, he collapsed.

Think of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī: when Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī gave him a copy of his book Lalita-mādhava, Raghunātha dāsa took the book and cried every single day just by holding it and reading its pastimes! When Rāmānanda Rāya went to read the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, he couldn't even read the letters because his eyes were so full of tears of ecstatic love that the water literally wiped out every single syllable on the page! The tears from his eyes dipped the book inside water, and he had to go and look for a different copy.

Let us look at what Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī described as his own personal experience of chanting the Holy Name. He said: “I do not know the potency of these two syllables: Kṛṣ-ṇa. I cannot explain what is within these two syllables. When Kṛṣṇa is chanted, the eye does not see anything. The ear does not hear anything else. The tongue does not chant anything else—there is no distraction. Only Kṛṣṇa.” He was not pretending; that was his actual, unadulterated experience. He never complained about his mind wandering or being distracted. His mind was asleep to this material world, and awake only to Kṛṣṇa.

✦

Protecting Our Consciousness From Sinful Desires

Why do we look at the Deities and feel nothing? Why do we chant and feel dry?

Because our eyes are completely dry. What is drying our eyes? Offenses. Offenses dry up our eyes.

We are roommates with Kṛṣṇa, but we are not on speaking terms! Is that normal? You stay in the same room with someone and you don’t talk to them—there is something seriously wrong! We stand before Him and treat Him like a stone or wooden idol. But the Deity is not an idol. If you are dealing with the Deities, you must communicate with Him. Kṛṣṇa is personally there, but because of our offenses, our vision is blocked.

This Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting has extraordinary spiritual potency. It is the exact same mantra! Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has told us that Chandra, the moon god, chanted Hare Kṛṣṇa. And because the moon chanted, Lord Śiva’s blue neck—which became blue from drinking the poison of the ocean—became completely white! The japa of the moon god actually removed the poison from Śiva's throat and turned it white. When Śiva went home, his wife Pārvatī was so shocked that she doubted him and said: “Don’t come near me! You are not my husband! Go!” She was not pretending; she was completely bewildered because her husband's neck had turned white from the chanting of the Holy Name!

And look at Lord Balarāma’s dhoti. His dhoti is eternally blue. But Nārada Muni’s japa turned Balarāma’s blue dhoti completely white! Even the Yamunā River turned completely milky because of the chanting, and the gopīs churned it and got sweet yogurt from it!

This is the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting. It is not a different mantra. Śrīla Prabhupāda is chanting the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, and the whole world is dancing in the kīrtana of the Holy Name. Why are we not having the same experience? Because we are criminals, deviants, and offenders since time immemorial. We have to pay the heavy price of purification before we can experience the same nectar. We must purify ourselves to get to that level.

When we are struggling on this path of purification, our minds become disturbed by the unjust, cruel, or negative behavior of others. Sincere devotees sometimes feel that when they face such trials, even performing Kṛṣṇa consciousness feels painful.

But I must tell you something very heavy: A sincere devotee can never feel mental disturbance. It is not possible.

If a sincere devotee feels mental disturbance and is overwhelmed by anxiety, it would mean that Kṛṣṇa is failing in His promise to protect His surrendered servants. But Kṛṣṇa never fails. He always protects His devotees.

Why do we get disturbed by the unjust behavior of others? Because we have the material contamination inside us. If I do not have lust in my body, nothing outside can make me lusty. If I do not have anger in my heart, no insult can make me angry. If I do not have envy, the competitive actions of others can never make me envious. The fire inside your heart is what connects with the fire outside. When people insult or mistreat you, they are simply throwing a spark. If your heart is full of dry, material attachments, it will instantly catch fire. But if your heart is saturated with the nectar of bhakti, that spark will immediately be extinguished.

Sincere devotees are protected by Kṛṣṇa. Let us look at a beautiful pastime in the Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata. One day, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was performing kīrtana, and the great devotee Kula Vāsudeva joined the kīrtana. The blasphemers and envious people stood right in front of him and started criticizing him directly: “See this, just see this one! Look, he has no cloth on his body, he has no food to eat. The whole night he cried due to hunger, but now he wants to show that he has the love of Godhead. Just look at him!”

They were not speaking far away from him; they were speaking right before his face. But Kula Vāsudeva was not even there! Why? Why was he not there? Because he was fully Kṛṣṇa conscious, dancing in the kīrtana of the Lord. There was no space for anything else to enter his heart. He was completely empty of material identification, and completely full of Kṛṣṇa.

Why do we hear what people talk about us? Why do we get so disturbed, so offended, and so angry when someone criticizes us? Because we are empty! We are empty of Kṛṣṇa, and full of ourselves. Because we identify with this temporary body, we respond to insults negatively: “Why did you say like that about me? Who are you to tell me that?” But Kula Vāsudeva didn’t say that. The great saint Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja didn’t say that. They were full of Kṛṣṇa, and there was no empty space in their hearts to receive insults. Sādhana is what fills that empty space. When we practice Sādhana nicely, we become strong, and all these material disturbances simply bounce off our chest.

✦

Overcoming the Illusion of Spiritual Advancement

When bhakti begins to work, it immediately relieves our suffering. This is the first quality of pure devotional service described by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī: klesagni—the distress-relieving quality. When we run to Kṛṣṇa consciousness from the burning fire of material life, bhakti immediately puts that fire out. Suddenly, we feel comfortable, peaceful, and relieved.

And that is exactly where the trap of Maya lies! We feel, “I am okay now. My mind is stable, my life is comfortable.” And so, we become complacent. We slide into a comfortable, lazy routine where our practices feel monotonous and mechanical. We start coming to the temple only when we feel like it, treating our Sādhana like a casual hobby.

In the Bhagavad-gītā (16.17), Kṛṣṇa uses a very heavy word for this state: ātmasambhāvitā—self-complacent and arrogant. He says that complacency is the quality of a demon, not a devotee! We came to Kṛṣṇa consciousness to become devotees, not self-satisfied demons.

Let me tell you a story about my musician friends from my youth. They formed a band called Cloud Seven. There were six boys. In the beginning, they had nothing, so they put an incredible, desperate amount of effort into practicing, writing, and refining their music. When their first record came out, it was an absolute hit. They made massive amounts of money and achieved huge success.

But then, they started enjoying their success. They became complacent. When it was time to record their second album, they didn’t put the same effort into practicing. They foolishly thought, “The effort we put into the first record will automatically carry the second one.”

Do you know what happened? The second album made nothing—it was a total failure!

Success is a stimulant, but success can also bring us down. If we are not careful to continuously expand our devotion, complacency will destroy us. The principle of love is this: if what I offer to Kṛṣṇa today pleases Him, tomorrow I must expand it. Bhakti is a dynamic, moving process. The moment you try to just “maintain” your standard without any desire to grow, you have already begun to slide backward. Maintenance is Maya!

Some people think, “This size of the temple is enough. Ten disciples is enough. Let us just keep things going.” No! That is maintenance, and maintenance is Maya. We have to expand it. We must always push ourselves further.

To defeat this complacency, we must understand our priorities: Sādhana is our primary, non-negotiable duty. Services come after Sādhana.

✦

Avoiding Bargains and Business with the Supreme

Often, when we face difficulties, we try to bargain with Kṛṣṇa. We pray desperately for certain material adjustments—like a job with better hours or relief from family stress—believing that these changes will make our environment more conducive for Sādhana. But when we pray and fast for years, and Kṛṣṇa does not answer, we feel neglected. Why does He ignore our sincere prayers?

There is a stunning statement in the Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta where Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself reveals His mind:

“This person is foolishly begging Me for material adjustments and temporary toys. I am not a fool like him. Therefore, I will not give him what he asks. Instead, I will take My time to completely purify him, and then I will give him the ultimate treasure of My lotus feet.”

Kṛṣṇa is not deaf! He hears everything. But when we are praying, we should check: does our karma allow us to get this now?

Let me give you an example. We have a devotee who has a very high night shift job. He is trying to get a morning job, but for a long time he is not getting it. He has been praying to Kṛṣṇa for a long time, but nothing helps. He feels he can practice better if he has a morning job. But because his karma doesn’t allow him to have what he is begging for, or because it is not right for him, Kṛṣṇa keeps quiet.

Let me give you another example. Some time back, a devotee told me—and I know him very well: “Maharaj, if I get a car... if I get a car every day, every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Please pray for me.”

Kṛṣṇa pleased, he got a car!

For three years, I did not see him. Finally, I met him and said, “Prabhu, how are you? Do you remember what you said? You said if you get a car, you will come for Maṅgala-ārati every day. Look, is this your own car?”

“My car,” he said.

“Then why are you not coming? For three years I have not been seeing you here with the car.”

And do you know what he told me next? He said, “Oh, Maharaj, if I have two cars, then I will give one to my wife, and use one to come to the temple!”

Look at the cheating, duplicitous mentality! We think we can play smart with Kṛṣṇa!

But you can never cheat Him. Kṛṣṇa is not a “straight” man. He is extremely crooked. In the Bhagavad-gītā (10.36), He boldly declares: “I am the gambling of the cheat.” He doesn’t hide it! If you try to play smart with Him, He will cheat you back so beautifully, so peacefully, and without any struggle, that you will spend your whole life struggling in confusion while He remains completely untroubled. We cannot artificially do it. Please let us not forget that we are dealing with a person who knows us completely better than we know ourselves.

So let us stop trying to bargain with God. The shadow always moves with the object. If you make your Kṛṣṇa consciousness very strong and healthy, your material needs will follow nicely and become easy. But if you are spiritually sick, then everything in your material life will also be a struggle, a guesswork, and a speculation.

Sincere, heart-centered service is our only business. Leave the results and the timeline to Kṛṣṇa. Do your part, and let Kṛṣṇa do His part.

✦

Developing Sincerity Beyond Transactions

Some of us have a lot of material career aspirations. To achieve them, we think we must invest a massive amount of time and effort, which inevitably pushes our devotional service into the background. But Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī warns us that prayāsaḥ ca—endeavoring desperately for material things that are extremely difficult to obtain—instantly destroys bhakti.

Look closely at those who have already achieved the high career positions you are dreaming of. Have you checked how wonderful their lives actually are? When you see them smiling for the camera, do you have any idea of the hollow, screaming vacuum that exists inside their hearts? We think that if we are materially successful, we will finally be happy. That is nothing but a complete lack of faith. Our absolute first priority in life must be Kṛṣṇa consciousness—everything else comes after.

This feeling of the goal being "too far" comes from a total lack of understanding. You are thinking that you are the one who has to cross this gap using your own tiny material strength. But Kṛṣṇa only asks us to do our part: janma karma ca me divyam—simply try to understand with faith how He appeared and what He did. Sages who try to reach perfection on their own strength always end up falling down, because they depend on themselves. But devotees who simply depend on Kṛṣṇa are always protected. Look at Haridāsa Ṭhākura: Maya Devi herself came to tempt him, and she confessed that while the greatest sages fell before her closed eyes, she could not touch Haridāsa because Kṛṣṇa was eternally dancing in his heart. Depend on Kṛṣṇa, and the gap will vanish in a single second.

✦

The Character and Qualifications of a True Disciple

How do we protect ourselves when strong lusty desires arise and overpower our minds? Lust never comes “suddenly.” It is already sitting there, hidden inside the crevices of your heart. When it manifests, it simply means your Sādhana is weak and your mind is idle. The secret to conquering lust is to never be idle! Be fully, intensely occupied in planning and executing Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Secondly, busily and lovingly care for other devotees. Lust is nothing but the diseased, selfish mirror-image of pure love. To transform it, you must practice active, selfless care for others. When you are busily engaged in making others happy for Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure, lust will find no empty space to enter and will naturally wither away.

How do we get to know who our spiritual master is? You pray to Kṛṣṇa. Go to your knees, look at the Deity, and tell Him: “Please, show me who my spiritual master is.” He will guide you. But you must be sincere. Plan your alignment tonight. Sleep early, wake up early, do not sit with those who gossip and criticize, and sit strictly with those who chant.

When can a disciple take the instruction of the spiritual master as their very life and soul? Only when you are surrendered. Without surrender, you can never take the order as your life and soul. If you are not surrendered, your mind will always rise up and challenge: “Why should I obey you? Why should I do this difficult service?” When devotees say, “Oh, I want to follow, but it is just too difficult,” they are self-deceived. It is not that the instruction is too difficult; it is simply that they have not surrendered yet. The moment you surrender, the strength to execute the order is immediately supplied.

A genuine disciple has zero duplicity. They are completely straightforward and transparent in their dealings with everyone, and most of all, with their spiritual master. They have no double life—their private life and their public life are exactly the same.

And to protect all of this, morning Sādhana is our ultimate key. Chanting early in the morning is the secret of perfect time management. If you finish your sixteen rounds before 6:30 AM, you will have an incredibly long, peaceful, and powerful day. You will have time to read, time to hear, and time to execute your services with a clear, happy mind. But if you do not chant in the morning, you don’t have a day. Your entire day becomes a frantic, stressful struggle in the mode of passion, where you are running late for everything, screaming at your mind, and achieving nothing of real value.

✦

Deep Desire for the Lord Attracts Divine Mercy

Some people say that because bhakti requires intense emotional control and focus, it is very difficult to perform pure devotion in a female body, and that women face too many material obstacles.

This is completely false! It is a total misunderstanding.

In reality, women can become pure devotees far faster than men. The only obstacle is when we focus too much on the temporary physical body, forgetting the spiritual treasure we possess within.

Look at our scriptures. In all of our history, in the Mahābhārata and the Rāmāyaṇa, have you ever read of a single woman who went to the forest to perform austerities and fell down? No! Not a single one!

But how many great male sages, powerful yogis, and kings went to the forest and fell down? Millions! And who caused their fall down? Women!

In physical combat and spiritual tests, the person who can push you down is always stronger than you. The determination of women is naturally far stronger, more enduring, and more resilient than that of men. All the women who performed austerities in our tradition succeeded without a single exception.

Let us change our minds and stop identifying with these temporary, physical bags of skin. Rise above the bodily platform altogether, tap into that natural, unshakeable determination, and use it to conquer the heart of Kṛṣṇa.

✦

Embracing the Purifying Burn of Pure Love

If attachment is extremely dangerous, and whether material or spiritual, attachment will make you cry, then what is the difference? What is the difference between material attachment and spiritual attachment if both of them cause pain and tears?

Both attachments are indeed painful, and both will make you cry. But there is a massive, infinite difference in the quality of that pain.

Material attachment brings a pain that drags you down into continuous, dry, and degrading suffering. It leaves you exhausted, empty, and spiritually dead.

But spiritual attachment—the pain of feeling separation from Kṛṣṇa—is intensely, unimaginably blissful! It is a pain that makes the soul dance in ecstasy!

The great saintly Śrīla Gaura Govinda Mahārāja once challenged everyone to make a beautiful, practical experiment:

Imagine you take raw sugarcane juice. You place it on a fire, and you boil it, and boil it, and boil it. Before it solidifies into dry jaggery, while it is still in a thick, golden, semi-liquid state—how does it taste? It is incredibly, intensely sweet!

Now, if you want to experience the absolute peak of that sweetness, you must drink it while it is boiling hot!

If you drink it hot, it will instantly burn your lips. It will burn your tongue and your throat. But because the taste is so staggeringly sweet, you will refuse to stop drinking! You will feel the burn, yet you will take another gulp, and another gulp, and you will never spit it out!

And the miracle of this spiritual sugarcane juice is that you will never end up in a hospital! Your lips will burn, but your soul will be cured of the material disease forever.

This is what we call Prema—pure, ecstatic love of Godhead.

Under the influence of Prema, your entire reality is turned upside down. The wild, dangerous forest becomes your sweet home, and your comfortable home feels like a barren, terrifying forest. The dark night is experienced as the bright midday sun, and the day is felt as night. Ordinary suffering is transformed into the highest, most exquisite happiness.

This is the supreme gift of the disciplic succession. This is the golden nectar that Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Lord Balarāma came to distribute freely to all of us. Since we must experience pain and tears in this material world anyway, let us choose the best one. Let us throw away our dry, material attachments, and drink deeply of this boiling-hot sugarcane juice of devotion to Kṛṣṇa!

Chapter 9 · Heart Check

The Sweet Burn of Pure Desire

Are you dealing with the Deities on the altar with straightforward, loving communication, or has your worship become a silent, dry room where you are not on speaking terms with Lord Kṛṣṇa?

Look deeply into your prayers from the last year: are you constantly bargaining with Kṛṣṇa for material adjustments—like better hours, a new car, or physical comforts—rather than begging for the costly diamond of pure devotional service?

The Temptation of Success

When your material and spiritual lives are comfortable and "safe," do you allow bhakti's initial relief to make you lazy and complacent?

Remember the Cloud Seven band: are you resting on the spiritual effort of yesterday, or are you actively trying to expand and boil the milk of your Sādhana today?

Sincerity in our Daily Actions

Are you attempting to perform management during Japa, or stepping onto the altar and into the kitchen before settling your morning rounds?

Is there any duplicity, hidden motives, or a double life that you are keeping behind your public identity as a devotee?

My Personal Prayer
Choosing the Burning Nectar

Let us lay down our material shopping lists, stop trying to play smart with the Lord, and beg Him to forgive our history of duplicity. Write a raw prayer to Srila Gurudeva and Lord Kṛṣṇa, asking for the unyielding determination to choose the blissful, burning sugarcane juice of Prema over the dry, degrading attachments of this world.
