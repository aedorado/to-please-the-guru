Part Two · The Internal Battle
VIII
Seeing Kṛṣṇa Through the Ears

How do we deepen our connection with Kṛṣṇa?

In the modern world, our motto is always: “See to believe.” We want to see God with our own material eyes. We say, “If God exists, let Him stand before me, and then I will believe.” But Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and our great ācāryas tell us that the process of spiritual seeing begins through the ears.

There is a beautiful, defining teaching in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.8.36) given by Queen Kuntī:

śṛṇvanti gāyanti gṛṇanty abhīkṣṇaśaḥ
smaranti nandanti tavehitaṁ janāḥ
ta eva paśyanty acireṇa tāvakaṁ
bhava-pravāhoparamaṁ padāmbujam

“O Kṛṣṇa, those who continuously hear, chant, and repeat Your transcendental activities, or take pleasure in others doing so, certainly see Your lotus feet, which alone can stop the repetition of birth and death.”

Please listen carefully to this word Queen Kuntī uses: abhīkṣṇaśaḥ—continuously. She says we must hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, non-stop.

Why? Because our material eyes are dry, and they are completely blind.

We have this immense disease of wanting to see Kṛṣṇa physically, but we do not realize that our present conditional vision is completely unqualified to see Him. When we look at the beautiful Deities on the altar, we don’t faint. We don’t cry. We see the Deities, and our minds are still thinking about our money, our business, and our family. We look at them and we think: “Oh, this is just wood, this is just stone, this is an idol.” But the Deity is not an idol; Kṛṣṇa is personally standing there. It is our own lack of qualification, our own dry eyes that make us blind to His presence. Offenses against the Vaiṣṇavas have dried up our eyes. We are trying to see Kṛṣṇa before we have learned how to hear Him.

✦

Sincere Hearing vs. Physical Proximity

We must understand that physical proximity to God does not guarantee spiritual vision.

When Lord Kṛṣṇa was personally present on this earth, walking, talking, and performing His pastimes, did everyone see Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead? No!

Materialists like Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Jarāsandha, and Śiśupāla were highly qualified personalities. They had immense wealth, political power, and high social standing. But they were completely blind to the presence of the Lord.

Rāvaṇa saw Lord Rāmacandra physically. But how did he see Him? He saw Him as a weak, unfortunate prince who was crying because his wife had been stolen. Rāvaṇa thought, “I will easily kill this helpless man.”

Kaṁsa saw Kṛṣṇa every day in his mind, but he saw Him only as a terrifying enemy with a sword, waiting to cut off his head.

Jarāsandha saw Kṛṣṇa face-to-face and fought Him seventeen times. But his vision was blocked by the thick, heavy curtain of material desire. He saw Kṛṣṇa only as a cowherd boy, a runaway, and a mortal foe whom he wanted to destroy.

Hiraṇyakaśipu performed the most extreme physical austerities in the history of the universe—standing on one leg for tens of thousands of years. He did so much severe austerity that his brain was burning! But because his heart was full of envy and pride, what did he get at the end? He did not get Kṛṣṇa; he got death.

By contrast, what austerity did the child Prahlāda Mahārāja perform? He did not stand on one leg. He did not do severe fasting. He simply heard. He sat and heard about Kṛṣṇa with complete faith from his spiritual master, Nārada Muni. And because he heard with his mind, he got Kṛṣṇa. He got protection and mercy. This is the power of hearing.

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu once said a very heavy thing: “If I do not give My mercy to someone, I can stand face to face with them, and they will never see Me.”

We cannot force our way into Kṛṣṇa’s presence using our material qualifications, our wealth, or our academic degrees. True spiritual vision is not a physical adjustment; it is a gift that is granted only when our hearts are purified. And that purification can only come when we learn to sit down submissively and hear from the right sources.

✦

The Raging River and Sincere Hearing

To understand the difference between mechanical ritual and genuine, attentive hearing, our ācāryas share a powerful history.

There were once two ladies who lived in the same village. Every day, for seven days, they went together to hear a great sage speak on the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. They sat side-by-side in the same audience, listening to the same speaker.

For seven days, the speaker repeatedly emphasized a single, powerful instruction: “This material existence is a vast, dangerous ocean, but you can easily cross it simply by chanting ‘Govinda, Govinda’ from the core of your heart.”

At the end of the seven days, the two ladies began their journey back to their homes. On the way, they came to a wide river. While they had been sitting in the kathā, a heavy rainstorm had fallen, and the river had become swollen, raging, and extremely dangerous to cross.

The first lady looked at the turbulent water and remembered the words of the speaker. She thought: “Our spiritual guide told us that by chanting ‘Govinda,’ we can easily cross the entire ocean of material birth and death. If we can cross the ocean, then what is this tiny, insignificant river? It is nothing!”

With absolute, unwavering faith, she stepped straight onto the raging water, chanting “Govinda, Govinda!” Miraculously, she did not sink. She walked cleanly across the surface of the river, as if she were walking on dry land, and reached the other side.

The second lady saw this and thought: “Wow! My friend is walking on water! Let me do the same thing.” She jumped into the river, but the moment her feet touched the water, she began to sink. The heavy current dragged her down, and she began to drown.

As she was going under, she screamed desperately to her friend on the bank: “Help me! Please, save me! Come and pull me out!”

The first lady called back: “Don’t you remember what the speaker told us? Why don’t you chant Govinda?” But the drowning lady could not chant. She fumbled, choked, and drowned in the river.

Why did this happen? They sat in the same class, heard the same speaker, and went to the same temple. But for the second lady, she did not hear. Her body was in the class, but her mind was completely somewhere else.

When her friends asked her, “Oh, how was the lecture today?” she would smile nicely and say, “Oh, the speaker was so wonderful! He spoke the best lecture I have ever had!” But if they asked her, “Can you share one point that he made?” she had nothing to say. She couldn’t remember a single thing.

She did not hear. Because she did not hear, she could not remember. Because she could not remember, she had no faith. And because she had no faith, when the crisis came, she could not act. She drowned.

Genuine hearing is not just about the sound waves hitting your eardrum and falling off. Hearing means that the message penetrates the ear, pierces through our defenses, and goes straight into the heart, completely transforming our consciousness. If you hear with faith, even if you are completely unqualified, your material situation will be destroyed. You will walk across the raging rivers of this world with complete peace and fearlessness.

✦

The Sweet Flower Handcuffs of Vṛndāvana

Why does Kṛṣṇa demand this deep, attentive hearing? Because He is not conquered by dry scholarship, heavy penances, or formal awe and reverence. He is conquered only by spontaneous, informal love.

Let us look at the sweet, playful nature of this love. Look at a beautiful, joyful pastime of Vṛndāvana.

One day, Lord Kṛṣṇa was sitting in one corner of the forest, and His cowherd friends, the gopas, put Him on a mock throne. They worshiped Him, and after the worship, Kṛṣṇa looked at them and said: “Now that I am the king, you must go and announce it. Anyone who disobeys, arrest them and bring them to the king for punishment!”

The cowherd boys were very excited. Madhu Maṅgala, His dear brāhmaṇa friend, was the first person to start jumping here and there, shouting loudly: “Kṛṣṇa is the king! If you see anyone picking flowers in this forest, arrest the person and take them to the king for punishment!”

As they were marching through the forest making this announcement, they met Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī and the gopīs. The gopīs looked at the boys and said: “What did you say? Kṛṣṇa is the king? No! Rādhārāṇī is the queen of this forest!”

Madhu Maṅgala stepped forward, waved his hand, and argued: “No! You can’t say that! Kṛṣṇa is the king!”

The gopīs looked at each other and said: “We have to deal with this boy seriously.”

Suddenly, the gopīs descended on the cowherd boys. The boys were completely outnumbered. Seeing the fierce determination of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s party, all the boys ran away—but Madhu Maṅgala was caught! He was arrested and thrown to the ground.

The gopīs looked down at him and demanded: “Now you tell us. Who is the king here?”

Madhu Maṅgala looked at their faces, realized his friends had abandoned him, and quickly changed his side: “Oh, actually... I think Kṛṣṇa is your queen!”

The gopīs laughed and said: “For the heavy offense you made of declaring Kṛṣṇa the king, we are going to handcuff you!”

Now, what were their handcuffs made of? In this material world, handcuffs are made of cold, hard iron. But in Vṛndāvana, the gopīs handcuffed Madhu Maṅgala with beautiful, fragrant, soft flowers! They chained his hands tightly with garlands of sweet blossoms.

Madhu Maṅgala looked at his flower handcuffs and said: “Well, handcuffing me is one thing. But for my next punishment... I want you to give me laddus!”

Since he was a “laddu brāhmaṇa” who didn’t know how to chant any Sanskrit mantras, the gopīs took great pleasure in executing this punishment. They loaded Madhu Maṅgala’s pockets on both sides with big, sweet laddus, and stuffed his mouth so full of laddus that he couldn’t even speak!

Then they told him: “Now go back to your friend and bring him here to declare that our queen is the supreme head of this forest.”

Madhu Maṅgala waddled back to Kṛṣṇa, who was still sitting on his mock throne.

Kṛṣṇa looked at him, barely recognizing him, and asked: “Hey, my friend, what happened to you?”

Madhu Maṅgala mumbled through his mouthful of laddus: “What happened to me? You are asking me? This is the result of declaring you the king! I am handcuffed like this! They beat me and pulled me seriously! They caught me completely! The only way you can save yourself is to come with me right now and surrender. Otherwise, all of them are coming here, and you will see what they will do to you!”

Madhu Maṅgala took Kṛṣṇa by the hand, dragged Him from the throne, and took Him straight to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s assembly.

When they arrived, the gopīs looked at them and demanded: “Kṛṣṇa, tell us. Who is the queen of this forest?”

Kṛṣṇa looked at Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, bowed His head, and said: “Rādhārāṇī is the queen.”

Immediately, the gopīs picked up Kṛṣṇa, sat Him on the same throne next to Rādhārāṇī, and worshiped them both together with great love.

This is the feast for the soul.

We take prasāda for our physical bodies every day. But hearing these sweet, playful, and intimate activities of the Lord is the true food that nourishes our dry, parched souls. When we hear with faith, we are handcuffed not by the iron chains of material karma, but by the sweet flower handcuffs of Kṛṣṇa’s love.

Let us stop being superficial, mechanical ritualists. Let us put away our dry, academic arguments, sit submissively on the floor, and hear with our whole hearts. That is the only way our blind, dry eyes will finally be opened to see the beautiful lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa.

Chapter 8 · Heart Check

Am I Actually Hearing?

Think honestly about your recent classes, reading sessions, or daily chanting:

Am I sitting physically present while my mind is completely somewhere else, scrolling through anxieties, duties, and plans?

Did I walk away from the last Bhagavatam lecture with a specific point, instruction, or pastime planted in my heart, or did I simply think “that was a nice class” while remembering nothing?

Realize: She did not hear. If we cannot remember what we hear, we cannot build faith, and when the turbulent rivers of material crisis rise, we will drown.

Cleansing Our Blocks to Hearing

Our material eyes are dry and blind to Kṛṣṇa's presence because of our offenses against His devotees.

Is there any hidden criticism, territorial pride, or envy in my heart toward another devotee?

Do I stand before the Deity on the altar and see only wood and stone, treating Him like an ordinary material idol because my mind is distracted?

Realize: Jarāsandha stood face-to-face with the Lord but saw only a cowherd enemy, while the child Prahlāda simply sat and heard from his Guru, and got Kṛṣṇa.

The Feast for the Soul

Aural Reception: Spend at least ten uninterrupted minutes this week listening to the words of Śrīla Prabhupāda or your spiritual master. Turn off all phones, close your eyes, and listen as if your very life depended on it. Let the sound penetrate your ears, pierce your defenses, and go straight into your heart.

The Food: What sweet pastime or instruction will I feed my dry soul with today?
