Part One · The Foundation of Devotion
III
It Is Compulsory, Not Optional

Have you ever thought about why you are here?

We are all looking for happiness, we are all looking for some peace, but we are looking in the wrong place. Look at a dog and a cat. A dog sees something, but a cat will see and immediately close its eyes. Which one is better—the dog or the cat? The cat! It means we should not be concerned with material things; we must be concerned only with Krishna. Sincere practice means we are not concerned with what our material eyes are seeing, but with what our soul is hearing. Just as we need our physical eyes all the time to see where we are going in this world, we need the Guru all the time to see the truth. So having a Guru is compulsory.

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and our great ācāryas tell us there is a formula. And the very first step in that formula is not a matter of “if you like it” or “if you feel like it.”

It is compulsory. It is not optional.

In the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī outlines sixty-four principles of devotional service. Sixty-four! But do you know how he begins? He doesn’t begin with big, complicated rituals. He begins right from the spiritual master.

The first five items of those sixty-four are entirely about the Guru:

  1. Guru-padāśraya: Accepting the shelter of the lotus feet of a bona fide spiritual master.
  2. Kṛṣṇa-dīkṣādi: Receiving formal initiation from him.
  3. Śikṣaṇam: Being submissively instructed by him on how to discharge service.
  4. Obedience: Obeying his orders with absolute faith and devotion.
  5. Following the Ācāryas: Walking strictly in the footsteps of the great previous teachers.

Rūpa Gosvāmī says you must accept the shelter of a spiritual master. It is a must! If you are a human being, having a Guru is compulsory. Except you are not a human being! If you are a human being, Guru is compulsory.

The moment you accept initiation, your real journey back to Godhead begins. It doesn’t end there—it only begins!

✦

The Sacred Vow of Initiation

What is initiation?

Initiation is a contract.

When we get initiated, we accept three basic commitments:

You promise to keep the four regulative principles (no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, no illicit sex). You promise to chant a minimum of sixteen rounds of the Hare Kṛṣṇa maha-mantra every day. * You accept a fixed, dedicated service under his direction.

And once we accept that contract, the Guru goes to work.

His business is to gradually, day by day, remove the thick, heavy curtain of māyā from your eyes so you can actually see Kṛṣṇa.

Think of it like going to a corporate job. When you arrive at the office, there is a register, isn’t it? Your name is written there. The company records when you resume work, how many hours you spend on the job, what you actually do, and when you close. Your promotions, your bonuses, and your salary increases depend entirely on that daily record.

When you take shelter of a bona fide Guru, he is also keeping a register. He is monitoring you every day. He is looking at your hours, your chanting, your service, and most importantly, your consciousness.

Every day, the Guru is marking our progress, looking especially at our consciousness.

And what is he marking? He is checking to see how much of your material contamination is being removed. If we are sincere, we don’t add new dirt. We just keep reducing what is already there.

Our material ego, our pride, our lust, our envy—we keep letting the Guru cut them down. And one day, your contamination is reduced to zero. That is the day you achieve perfection. On that day, your life begins to shine like the brilliant midday sun!

✦

Sincere Effort to Receive Divine Mercy

Some people sit back and say, “Well, if Kṛṣṇa wants me to be a devotee, He will show me mercy. If my Guru wants to save me, he will just shower his grace on me. Why do I have to work so hard?”

Laziness! You are not serious!

I warn you: Mercy alone is partiality. Why not everybody? Why only you? We are all Kṛṣṇa's parts and parcels. His mercy is always available.

The truth is, divine mercy is constantly, eternally available. It is always there—not that it has to come, but it is already there, waiting for us. But if you want to receive it, you must straighten your hand to take it!

If someone is offering you something, but you keep your hands tightly folded behind your back, how will you ever get it? You have to straighten your hand.

Reaching out means putting in sincere, serious effort. It means doing something practical in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, taking the instructions on your head and executing them in your daily activities.

But instead, we sit in our comfortable laziness and complain: “Kṛṣṇa has never shown me mercy yet. If He shows me mercy, I will immediately become a pure devotee.”

We wait for eternity, making excuses, while the treasure is lying right in front of us.

✦

The Beggar’s Gold

Look at how we behave when mercy is offered to us. Let us look at a beautiful, striking history.

One day, Lord Śiva—the greatest of all Vaiṣṇavas—was traveling through the material world with his wife, Pārvatī. As they were walking along, they passed a beggar sitting by the roadside.

Pārvatī looked at him and pleaded, “Prabhu, please help him.”

Lord Śiva looked at him and turned his face away. As they were coming back from their journey, they met the beggar again. Pārvatī said, “Prabhu, please help this man. Please.”

So Lord Śiva straightened his hand to the beggar. He opened his hand, and dropped something precious right there.

The beggar looked at what was dropped in his hand and thought, “What is this?” He did not value it, and simply gave it to somebody else. That other person took the precious gift and instantly became wealthy.

Meanwhile, the beggar remained standing there, begging just as before.

Lord Śiva turned to Pārvatī and said, “See. I gave it to him, but he gave it to somebody else. He is not right for it.”

We are exactly like that beggar. We are getting so much, yet we still remain spiritually poor. Kṛṣṇa has given us the Holy Name, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, devotees, and the shelter of Guru. Yet we often fail to appreciate what we have been given. We remain poor because we don't know how to receive the mercy.

Let us stop being roadside beggars. Let us straighten our hands, take the gold we have been given, and honor the contract of initiation with our very life and soul.

Chapter 3 · Heart Check

Am I Honoring My Initiation?

Initiation is a sacred contract with Guru and Kṛṣṇa. How am I honoring that contract every day? Take a few quiet minutes and ask yourself:

My rounds: Am I actually chanting with attention, or have sixteen rounds become a mechanical obligation?

My vows: Am I consciously protecting the four principles Gurudeva has asked me to follow?

My service: Am I serving under Guru's direction, or have I quietly made myself the proprietor?

My appreciation: What spiritual treasure has Kṛṣṇa already placed in my hands that I have been treating casually?

My hand: Where am I waiting for mercy instead of straightening my hand to receive it?

Write down one concrete change you will make this week to straighten your hand, followed by a sincere prayer:

My Commitment:

My Personal Prayer
Straightening My Hand

Write a personal prayer asking Gurudeva for the sincerity and determination to straighten your hand and receive the mercy of the instructions you have been given.
