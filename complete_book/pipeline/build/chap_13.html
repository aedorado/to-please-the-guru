Part Two · The Internal Battle
XIII
Slaying the Pretender Within

We read in the scriptures, especially in the Tenth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, that one day, Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Balarāma entered the forest of Vṛndāvana to graze the cows along with Their cowherd boyfriends 1.

Every single day, the cowherd boys enter the forest with Their hearts bursting with joy, eager to play and serve the Lord. But on this particular day, there was a specific test waiting for them—a test of absolute sincerity, straightforwardness, and transparent truth.

✦

The Transformation of Summer

When Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma, and the cowherd boys stepped into the forest, it was the height of the summer season. Normally, the Indian summer is incredibly harsh, parching, and exhausting. The rays of the sun beat down like poison, parching the earth, drying up the green grass, and driving everyone to seek shelter. But because the Supreme Personality of Godhead was personally walking on the ground, the forest underwent a supernatural, ecstatic change 1.

Summer instantly manifested the sweet, cool, and pleasing qualities of spring! 1

Loud, cascading waterfalls drowned out the noisy crickets with their deep vibrations. Clusters of trees were constantly moistened by the fine, sweet spray of those waterfalls, keeping the entire forest lush, damp, and cool. The wind, wafting over the gentle waves of the lakes, carried the cooling, fragrant pollen of golden lotuses and water lilies across the pasture lands 1. Even the fierce summer heat and the threat of forest fires could not touch the residents of Vṛndāvana. The deep rivers drenched their banks with flowing waves, keeping the clay moist and muddy, so the parching sun could not evaporate the earth's vital sap 1. This is the nature of the spiritual world: when Kṛṣṇa is in the center of your life, the harshest summer of your material struggles is instantly transformed into the sweet, cooling spring of spiritual joy.

✦

Mother Yaśodā’s Anxious Love

Before the boys entered the forest, Mother Yaśodā was feeling the deep pain of maternal anxiety (vātsalya-rasa) 2.

She sat at home, her heart trembling with affection as she worried: “How can I allow my young, delicate boys to wander through the deep forest the whole day while I sit comfortably at home? Today, I will personally travel with you to the forest to take care of you!”

Kṛṣṇa did not like this idea. He twisted His face, frowned, and became completely distraught 2. He wanted to play freely without any rules or parental control.

Yaśodā tried to persuade Him, saying: “Do not worry about the household duties. Rohiṇī Devī will stay back and manage the home today, and tomorrow she will go and I will stay back. In this way, we can share the service of protecting you.” 2

But Kṛṣṇa remained silent. In spiritual etiquette, Kṛṣṇa did not speak because His senior brother, Balarāma, was present 2. He submissively allowed Balarāma to speak for Him.

Balarāma stepped forward, smiled warmly, and spoke the truth directly: “My dear Mother, let me tell you the actual truth. We boys are not going to the forest to tend the cows. The cows naturally take care of themselves. We only go there to play! If you come with us, you will completely interrupt our enjoyment! You will say: ‘No, don't climb that tree! Don't run there, you will fall down! Don't touch that!’ You will control us, and we will not be able to play freely. If you really want to come with us, you must become a gopa boy first! Otherwise, please go back to the house.” 2

Hearing Balarāma speak His inner mind so perfectly, Kṛṣṇa smiled. Because they are not conditioned souls, they wanted to play without any material control or institutional rules. Mother Yaśodā submissively accepted Balarāma’s words and returned home 2.

✦

The Ornaments of Vṛndāvana’s Forest

The moment the boys were out of their mothers’ sight, they removed all the heavy, expensive gold ornaments and jewels that had been placed on their bodies 3. They did not care for material riches.

They threw them aside and decorated themselves with the natural, simple wealth of Vṛndāvana 3: - They wore newly grown leaves and peacock feathers. - They hung garlands of fragrant Mallikā (jasmine) flowers around their necks 3. - They put delicate Śirīṣa flowers in their ears as earrings 3. - They decorated their hair with colored minerals and beautiful Kruci flowers 3.

Dressed in these simple forest clothes, Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma, and the cowherd boys danced, wrestled, and sang. Sometimes Kṛṣṇa would dance, and the boys would play the flute, hand cymbals, and buffalo horns to accompany Him. At other times, Balarāma and the boys would dance, and Kṛṣṇa would loudly praise them: “Very good! Wonderful!” 3

✦

The Innocent Games of Vṛndāvana

The pasture grounds of Vṛndāvana were a limitless playground. The boys whirled about until they became dizzy (laghanai), leaped like frogs, chased one another, built bridges, and played with bilva or kumbha fruits 3.

They played the game of netra-bandha (blindfolding), where one boy would approach a blindfolded boy from behind and place his palms over his eyes 3. Simply by the feel of the palms, the blindfolded boy had to guess who his friend was. If he won, he would claim a walking stick or a flute as a prize 3.

Sometimes, they would play at the riverbank of the Yamunā, acting like customs officers. They would block the path of the young gopīs and jokingly demand: “This forest belongs to our King! Before you cross the river, you must pay a heavy customs tax on your milk and yogurt!” The forest was filled with innocent joking, laughter, and spiritual happiness 3.

✦

The Disguise of Duplicity

While the boys were in the middle of their play, a powerful demon named Pralambāsura entered their midst 4. He came with a very specific, dark intention: to kidnap and kill both Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma 4.

But Pralambāsura was a coward. He did not attack openly. Instead, he disguised himself as a cowherd boy, specifically mimicking Madhumāṅgala who had stayed home that day with duties to perform, and seamlessly blended into the playing party of devotees 4.

In our Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava theology, Pralambāsura represents deceit, crookedness, and duplicity (kapaṭatā). He represents the dangerous tendency to pretend to be a devotee on the outside while internally harboring completely separate, self-serving, and poisonous motivations 4.

Let us look at the history of ancient India in the Mahābhārata. Generally, the whole world knows that Duryodhana is considered Balarāma's best disciple in club-fighting 4. But let me tell you the actual truth: Duryodhana was never a disciple of Balarāma! He was actually a disciple of Kali—he worshipped Kali! He came to Lord Balarāma only to trick Him, to butter Him up, so he could learn how to fight and defeat his enemies 4. And Lord Balarāma, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, cheated him very nicely! Balarāma trained him externally and supported him because Duryodhana was crooked. Balarāma stands for crooked, deceitful people; because Duryodhana was deceitful, Balarāma cheated him. He trained him very nicely so that Bhīmasena could eventually kill him on the battlefield, and Balarāma did not step in to protect him when Bhīma was smashing his thighs, because that is exactly what Balarāma wanted 4! When we try to cheat the Lord, we will be very nicely cheated.

There was only one Pralambāsura in Vṛndāvana five thousand years ago. But today, how many Pralambasuras do we have? There are so many Pralambasuras in our movement! They walk among us, attend programs, and wear the Vaishnava dress, but their hearts are completely full of politics, business, and deceit.

✦

The Game of Carriers

Lord Kṛṣṇa, who sees everything, instantly understood the demon's mind 5. But He did not expose him immediately. He pretended to accept him as a friend and proposed a game of teams 5: “Hey, boys! Let's play now. We will divide ourselves into two even teams, with Me leading one team and Balarāma leading the other.” 5

The winners of the game would climb onto the backs of the losers, who would have to carry them to the banyan tree known as Bhāṇḍīraka 5.

Kṛṣṇa cleverly arranged the game so that His own team would lose 5. Why did He do this?

Pralambāsura had joined Kṛṣṇa’s team because he was thinking: “Kṛṣṇa is too powerful to attack directly. I don't want to go directly to Him. But Balarāma is close to Him. If I carry Balarāma, I can take Him far away and kill Him. Once Balarāma is dead, Kṛṣṇa will die of grief on His own.” 5

Kṛṣṇa knew this deceitful plan perfectly. He willingly let His team lose so the demon would be forced to carry Balarāma 5. Defeated in the game, Kṛṣṇa carried Śrīdāmā on His back, Bhadrasena carried Vṛṣabha, and Pralambāsura carried Balarāma, the son of Rohiṇī 5.

✦

The Weight of the Supreme Lord

Pralambāsura carried Balarāma on his shoulders, but instead of putting Him down at the Bhāṇḍīraka banyan tree, he kept running 6. He ran swiftly, carrying Balarāma far beyond the playground 6.

As the demon ran, Lord Balarāma suddenly made Himself as heavy as massive Mount Sumeru! 6

The immense weight crushed the demon's shoulders. In panic, Pralambāsura was forced to abandon his disguise. He assumed his actual, gigantic demoniac form—an effulgent, terrifying body covered in golden ornaments that resembled a dark cloud flashing with lightning 6.

His body touched the sky. He had blazing, fiery eyes, terrible teeth, and reached up to his scowling, angry brows 7.

When Lord Balarāma saw this massive, terrifying demon carrying Him swiftly through the sky, He acted as if He were a little frightened and bewildered 7. In Kavi Karṇapūra’s Ānanda-vṛndāvana-campu, it is described that Balarāma called out to His brother: “Damodara! Manoram! Where are you? This giant demon is taking Me away in a different direction! Please help Me!” 7

But Kṛṣṇa called back: “Don't call Me! Don't you know who You are? You are the source of all strength! Deal with him Yourself! This is a chance to show Your power!” 7

✦

Slaying the Pretender

Hearing Kṛṣṇa’s words, Lord Balarāma became furious 8.

He did not even take up His club or His plow. He simply clenched His hand and hammered a single, crushing blow with His fist directly onto the demon's head! 8 Lord Balarāma's hand possesses the strength of billions of thunderbolts.

Just as Indra strikes a mountain with his thunderbolt, Balarāma’s fist cracked the demon’s skull open 8. Pralambāsura immediately vomited blood, lost all consciousness, and fell lifeless to the ground with a massive crash, like a mountain devastated by lightning 8.

The cowherd boys ran forward, completely astonished, embraced Balarāma with ecstatic love, and threw their arms around Him as if He had come back from the dead, crying: “Excellent! Well done! All glories to Balarāma!” The demigods in the heavens showered beautiful flower garlands and chanted His glories 8.

Balarāma’s plow is a gardening tool. It is meant to till the hard, dry ground of our hearts, tilling the soil so the seed of devotion (bhakti-latā-bīja) can be planted and safely grow. But His fist is a thunderbolt meant to crush our duplicity.

When we celebrate Balarāma Jayantī, we spend so much time on external functions—cleaning, cooking, decorating, and waiting to break our fast 8. We can hire people to clean, we can hire people to decorate, and we can even hire people to cook 8.

But let me tell you straight: you cannot hire anyone to do your hearing and praying! That is your personal business with Balarāma. The primary purpose of Balarāma's appearance is to kill the demons, and on this special day, He is coming to kill the demon of deceit and duplicity in our lives 8.

Let us pray honestly to Lord Balarāma to help us remove the Pralambasura of deceit in our lives.

Chapter 13 · Heart Check

Slaying My Inner Pretender

Take a few honest, quiet minutes before Lord Balarāma's plow to examine your consciousness:

My Disguise: Where am I carrying separate, selfish, or material desires (for fame, respect, or control) while outwardly presenting myself as a dedicated, humble devotee?

My Crookedness: Do I engage in subtle political games, gossip, or double-dealing with my godbrothers and sisters to protect my own position or reputation?

Cheating the Guru: Am I pretending to strictly follow the instructions of my spiritual master while secretly compromising my vows and standards when no one is watching?

The Fast vs. The Heart: Am I more focused on the external, cosmetic rituals of my devotional life (decorations, fasting times, feasting) than on the internal, painful work of begging for purification?

The Vow of Honesty: Settle this standard in your mind: “Lord Balarāma cannot be cheated. If I try to cheat Him, I will only cheat myself of His mercy. I will lay down my disguise and speak the raw, unpolished truth to my spiritual master today.”
