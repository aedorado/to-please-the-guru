### **PREFACE**

This book is not a dry academic thesis, nor is it a polished collection of theoretical essays. It is a compilation of transcribed heart-to-heart spiritual discourses spoken by His Holiness Bhakti Dhīra Dāmodara Swami directly to gatherings of devotees [3, 7, 14, 15, 16]. From the bustling centers of Bengaluru and Hyderabad to SNGM and ISKCON Hosur, these words were spoken to challenge our comfortable pretenses, to smash our false egos, and to call us back to our active duties in spiritual life [3, 7, 14].

Throughout these pages, you will hear the direct, personal voice of Srila Gurudeva [3, 7, 14]. As a dedicated disciple of His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami and a faithful follower of His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, Maharaj carries their standard and teachings into every chapter [2, 13, 14]. He does not compromise the truth to keep his audience comfortable [14, 18].

Instead, his focus is entirely on surrender, purity, and the active sacrifices we must make to protect our consciousness and build a real, devotee-centered future [15, 17]. This is a handbook for those who are ready to put their heads on the ground in genuine surrender [15, 19].

# Chapter 1: Putting Our Head on the Ground

Where does the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* begin? [450] Before the main verse of the scripture is spoken, the invocation begins with *oṁ namo bhagavate vāsudevāya*—an offering of obeisances to the Supreme Personality of Godhead [450]. 

Have you ever stopped to ask yourself what that actually means? We walk around in human bodies, but Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam suggests that human life does not actually begin with a human body [451]. It begins with human behavior [451]. And that behavior begins the very moment we decide to come and offer our heads to Kṛṣṇa [451]. Out of nine billion people on this planet, how many are trying to be devotees? A very small number [451, 452]. 

If you want to get a mathematical equation correct, you have to follow the formula [452]. If you ignore the formulas of algebra, will your equation be correct? [452] No, of course not. In the exact same way... In the exact same way, we cannot be successful devotees if we do not follow the formula of *bhakti* [452, 453]. 

The formula begins with putting our head on the ground [450, 453]. 

Since time immemorial, we have been wandering in this material world [453]. And what have we been doing all this time? We have been trying deliberately, systematically, to avoid Kṛṣṇa [453]. No one has ever succeeded in this, and nobody ever will [454]. Yet we keep trying [453]. We only become fortunate when we finally say, *“I am tired. Now, Kṛṣṇa, I surrender to you.”* [454] That is the true beginning of life [454].

There is nothing ordinary in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [454]. Everything is real, and there is no fiction [454, 455]. Look at HH Dhanurdhara Swami [455]. He spent four years of his life trying to understand everything in Vṛndāvana [455]. How many of us do like that? [455] We don’t absorb ourselves in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, yet we think, *“Oh, I am a pure devotee.”* [455] But a pure devotee is not artificial; they are real, and they are enjoying their Kṛṣṇa consciousness fully [455, 456].

Yet, look at our behavior in the temple [456]. Many of us stand there during the *pranama* mantras with no obeisances [456]. Some of us have even forgotten our own Guru’s *pranama* mantras because we are “too busy” to chant them [456, 457]. How will you be a devotee if you cannot even bow your head? [456]

---

### **The Order is the Life Substance**

How do we please the spiritual master? [450] We must understand that pure devotees do not come to this world to take anything from us [457, 458]. They do not care for our scholarship, our money, or our artificial glorification [457]. If they wanted glorification, they would have stayed in the spiritual world where the appreciation is real and from the heart [457, 458]. Here, almost everything we do is contaminated by lust, envy, greed, or delusion [458]. It is artificial [458]. The spiritual master comes here strictly to teach us, to give us the absolute truth [458, 459].

Look at the Pracetās, the ten sons of King Prachinabarhi [459]. When they went to the forest to perform austerities, they were favored by the appearance of Lord Shiva, who became their spiritual master and instructed them [459, 460]. And how did they respond? They took his words so seriously that simply by meditating upon his instructions, they became perfect [460]. 

This is the secret of success [460]. Once you are initiated and receive the orders of the spiritual master, you must unhesitatingly think about those instructions and refuse to be disturbed by anything else in this world [460, 461]. 

Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura explains this beautifully in his commentary on the *Bhagavad-gītā* (2.41): *vyavasayatmika buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana* [461]. He points out that the order of the spiritual master is the very life substance of the disciple [461]. As a disciple, your first business is not even to worry about whether you are going back home, back to Godhead [461]. Your only business is to execute the order of your spiritual master [461]. That is what we call “directional meditation.” [461] We must meditate on the order, and then actively find the practical means to execute it [461].

We have beautiful examples of this in our own movement [467]. Śrīla Prabhupāda often mentioned that book distribution gave him life [464]. When he was physically present, he noted that hearing the sankirtan results made his desire to continue living in his physical body increase [464, 465]. 

The best person who understood this was His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami (then Ghanashyam Prabhu) [464, 465]. When Prabhupāda heard his sankirtan results, tears would pour from his eyes [464, 465]. He would embrace Bhakti Tīrtha Swami (then Ghanashyam Prabhu), rub his head, and show him unlimited affection [465]. That disciple’s life was a complete success because he brought tangible satisfaction to the spiritual master [465].

Shortly before Śrīla Prabhupāda left this world, some devotees wanted to make it official [465]. They went to his bedside and asked, *“Prabhupāda, what pleases you the most?”* [466] They were expecting him to name a specific project or book distribution [466]. But Prabhupāda shocked them [466]. He looked at them and said: *“If you become a pure devotee, it will please me the most.”* [466]

And how do you become a pure devotee? [466] By pleasing a pure devotee through the sincere, unhesitating execution of his instructions [466].

### **The Principle of Vaishnava Etiquette**

There is an important standard we must understand at the very beginning of our spiritual life: we must never separate the spiritual master from those who serve him [488]. 

A genuine disciple is one who honors the entire family of the Guru—his godbrothers, his godsisters, and the junior devotees who assist him [488]. We must avoid the diseased mentality of treating the Guru as an isolated star while neglecting or ignoring the devotees around him [488, 490]. When we learn to treat every Vaishnava with love and submissive respect, our service becomes pure, pleasing, and free from material offenses [458, 488]. This is the preliminary definition of Vaishnava etiquette, which protects our surrender from becoming a cheap, prestigious show [488].

### **Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī’s Extreme Sacrifice**

To understand what genuine, risk-taking surrender looks like, we must study a pastime of Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī, an exalted householder disciple of Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya [467, 468, 469]. 

She was extremely, painfully poor—possessing only one single, veiled, torn dress [234]. She hardly had anything to eat [235]. But her heart was pure, and her dedication to her spiritual master was absolute [234].

One day, Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya was traveling all over India, preaching Kṛṣṇa consciousness [235]. He arrived unexpectedly at Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī’s village [235]. He did not come alone; he came with many disciples [235]. He purposefully bypassed the comfortable homes of his wealthy disciples and walked straight to the house of his poorest disciple, Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī [235, 246, 247]. He wanted to use her to teach all of us a lesson, to test her and show us where we truly stand [246, 247].

Rāmānujācārya looked at her and said, *“Please, provide Prasadam for me and all my disciples.”* [235]

Now, what would a modern disciple do in that situation? [236] We would immediately complain: *“Gurudev, did you tell me you’re coming? How can you just come like that? You are a pure devotee, you’re supposed to know I have no money! How can you bring all these people to my empty house?”* [236] But Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī did not have even a single thought of complaint [236]. She never felt like that [236]. She was simply overjoyed: *“My Gurudev has come! My life has come!”* [236]

But there was not even a single grain of rice in her home [241]. Zero [241]!

In that same village, there lived an incredibly wealthy merchant [238]. For years, this man had been lusting after Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī [238]. Every time he saw her in her torn clothes, he would mock her husband and tempt her: *“Your husband is very poor. He cannot maintain you [238]. You are such a beautiful girl—why did you make the mistake of marrying that man? [239] Come and enjoy with me! All this wealth will be yours if you become my girlfriend [239]. Your father gave birth to you to suffer like this? Is it your karma? Even if it is, if you become my girlfriend, is that not also your karma? [239]”* 

And every time, she had fiercely rebuffed him: *“Nonsense! I have not died because of my husband’s poverty. I don’t want to pollute my situation. Get out! [240]”* She had blocked every single entrance for Maya to enter her life [240].

But on this day, she realized her Guru and his followers were at her door, and they were hungry [235, 241]. In her mind, the calculation was simple: she was ready to do anything—including sacrificing her own body and her chastity—if it meant her Guru and his disciples would be fed [241, 242]. 

She walked straight to the wealthy merchant’s house, looked him in the eye, and said: *“My Gurudev has come with so many people, and I must feed them [241]. There is nothing in my house [241]. Give me the grains and supplies I need to serve them [242]. After they are fed, I am here for you [242]. Whatever you want to do with me, I am free [242].”* 

She was ready to break the principles, to break her Sādhana, out of pure, unadulterated love to feed her Guru [242]. 

When she spoke those words, something miraculous happened [242]. Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī’s desire to serve her Guru was so incredibly, blindingly pure that her purity pierced straight through the merchant’s lust and entered his soul [242]. 

The wealthy merchant began to weep [242]. 

He fell to the floor, touched her feet, and cried: *“For years, I tempted you, and I failed [243]. Today, you are ready to sacrifice your very chastity just to feed your Guru [243]. You are not an ordinary woman; you are Lakshmi Devi herself! [243] Please, take everything you need [243]. I beg you, let me touch your Guru’s lotus feet so I can be free from the heavy contamination in my own heart [243]. Please, you become my Shiksha Guru, and take me to your Diksha Guru so I can surrender my sinful life [245].”*

The merchant loaded her with the finest grains, ghee, and vegetables [245]. Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī returned home, cooked a magnificent feast, and joyfully served Rāmānujācārya and his disciples [245]. 

Now, when the feast was over, did she pretend nothing had happened? Did she hide the truth to protect her reputation? No [245]. She was transparent [245]. She knelt before Rāmānujācārya and confessed everything: *“Gurudev, I had no alternative [245]. I went to the wealthy merchant who has been tempting me for years and offered myself to him so I could get food for you [245]. But when I did, his heart was completely transformed by your mercy, and he gave me all of this [245].”*

Rāmānujācārya smiled and embraced her in his heart, declaring: *“You are a truly bona fide disciple [246]. You are ready to take the ultimate, genuine risk for your Guru [246].”* 

**However, we must be extremely careful here. Gurudeva immediately warns us against a dangerous, self-serving interpretation of this pastime: “Someone can say, ‘Yes, I want to break principles so I can raise money for my Guru’s project.’ Please, understand it is not the same [244]. You will not get the result Śrīmatī Lakṣmīdevī got [245]. They are two different things, not the same [244, 245].”**

**In her mind, there was absolutely no self-interest, no calculation, and no desire to enjoy [241, 245]. But when our minds try to copy her under a false pretense, Maya will immediately trap us [245]. Taking a cheap, calculated shortcut for our own convenience is entirely different from her unalloyed, risk-taking surrender [244, 245].**

If we do not take risk, we cannot satisfy Kṛṣṇa, we cannot satisfy Guru [247]. When we take a genuine risk, it touches the heart of a pure devotee [247]. Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya did not come to her house because he was a fool [247, 248]. He did it to stage a living drama, to teach us where we truly stand in our Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and to show us the depth of pure surrender that can touch the heart of a pure devotee [247, 248].

### **Nirasta-Kuhakam: The Uncheating Realm**

What is the ultimate, highest destination we are looking for when we speak of Goloka Vṛndāvana? [492, 493] 

We find the answer in the very opening words of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (1.1.1): ***nirasta-kuhakam, satyam param dhimahi*** [493]. 

*Nirasta-kuhakam* means the realm that is 100% free from all illusion, duplicity, and cheating pretenses [493]. In the spiritual world, cheating is 100% absent—one hundred percent! [493] What we think here, what we see here, and what we say here in this material world is almost always full of hidden motives [458, 493]. But the spiritual realm is a place of absolute, uncheating purity [493, 494].

And that purity can only be reached when we stop playing spiritual games [493]. We must follow the formula of *Sādhana* [452, 453, 494]. We must put our heads on the ground, take the Guru’s order as our very life and soul, and serve his followers with a sincere, open heart [450, 461, 488]. Only then will the heavy curtain of Maya be swept aside, and we will finally see Kṛṣṇa face-to-face [165, 249].

# Chapter 2: What Are We Living For?

Have you ever stopped to think about what you are actually doing with this life? 

I know we are all very busy. We have so many plans, so many programs, and so many desires. But if you look closely, where is all this activity actually taking us? 

Almost all of our desires, all of our struggles, and all of our interests are centered on just one thing: this temporary bag of skin, bones, and stool that we call the body [194]. We spend our mornings, our afternoons, and our nights trying to feed it nicely, dress it nicely, protect it nicely, and make it comfortable [197]. 

But let me ask you a very honest question: *What are we living for?* 

Is it just to keep this physical body going for as long as possible? Is that the ultimate success of a human being? 

---

## The Man on the Crutches

Some time ago, I saw something that completely stopped me in my tracks and forced me to think deeply about this material world [195]. 

I saw a man who was completely, severely deformed [195]. By any ordinary human standard, his physical situation was tragic. He had no control over his legs, and his hands were so twisted that it was extremely difficult for him to use them [195]. He sat on a pair of crutches, depending entirely on other people for every basic need—every meal, every bath, everything [195, 196]. There was no hope of medical treatment or physical recovery; he was going to be in this condition for the rest of his life [195].

As I was watching, this man began to cross a busy road [195]. Suddenly, a motor vehicle came speeding down the street, heading straight toward him [195, 196]. 

At that moment, a shocking thing happened. The sheer speed and frantic energy that this deformed man used to drag his crutches and dodge that vehicle was unbelievable [196]. He moved with an absolute, desperate intensity just to save himself from being hit [196]. 

I watched him reach the other side of the road, and I began to contemplate: *What is the purpose of this person’s life?* [196] 

Materially speaking, he has nothing. He has no independence, no physical comfort, and no worldly prospects. He suffers at every moment. So why was he fighting so desperately, with every ounce of his energy, just to keep that severely afflicted body alive for one more day? [196]

The answer is both simple and profound: **the soul by nature is eternal** [196]. 

Because the soul is eternal, it completely rejects death. No matter how miserable, broken, or 100% useless our material body becomes, we still desperately want to hold onto it [196]. We do not want to die [196]. And so, the entire struggle of the conditioned soul in this material world is reduced to a single, exhausting goal: *keeping this temporary physical shell alive for as long as possible* [196]. 

---

## Better Than the Animals?

We think we are very advanced because we are human beings. We build big houses, buy fast cars, go to engineering colleges, and eat "scientific" meals with forks and spoons [166, 197, 200]. 

But if we look at our activities honestly, what are we doing that is so special? We eat, we sleep, we defend, and we mate [197]. And if you look carefully, you will find that **every other species of life does these things far better than us** [197]! 

An animal does not need to pay school fees or rent a house to stay [200]. An animal does not need to take transport for its enjoyment [200]. Animals do not cook their food or mix artificial spices, and because they live naturally, they do not have constipation, they do not suffer from our constant diseases, and they never go to the hospital [166, 168]. They have no anxiety and no fear [200]. Any aspect of sense gratification you can think of, the animals are executing perfectly, without any of our mental stress [197, 200].

So if our life is only about eating nicely, sleeping nicely, and protecting our bodies, we are not living as human beings. We are just dressed-up animals [197]. 

Even the greatest living entities in this material world are frustrated [197]. Mārkaṇḍeya Ṛṣi witnessed the devastation of the universe, but he was not satisfied [198, 199]. Even Lord Brahmā, who lives the longest life possible in the material world, feels miserable and anxious, and eventually, his body also has to change [126, 199]. 

So why are we going to the spiritual master asking for material adjustments? 

Why do we go to a *spiritual* master and ask him to fix our business, improve our health, or make our material lives comfortable? [194, 202, 211] 

This is like going to a multi-billionaire and begging him for a single piece of dry bread. Material adjustments are already available to every species of life [198]. A pig gets its food. A dog gets its mate. You do not need a Guru or a scripture to get material facilities—your karma will bring them to you automatically [198, 200]. 

When we go to a spiritual master, we should ask for something that is **not of this material world** [198]. We must ask for a permanent solution to our eternal problem [198].

## The Formula of the Ācāryas

There is a beautiful verse in the twelfth canto of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (12.10.34) where Mārkaṇḍeya Ṛṣi is standing before Lord Shiva [192]. Lord Shiva is the greatest of the Vaiṣṇavas (*vaishnavanam yatha shambhuh*), full of all perfection and capable of granting any material blessing in the universe [193]. 

But listen to what Mārkaṇḍeya Ṛṣi begs of him:

> *varam ekaṁ vṛṇe ’thāpi pūrṇād varada-gīr-atam*  
> *bhagavaty acyutām bhaktiṁ tat-parāṇāṁ ca tad-yuji* [192, 193]

He prays: *\”I request only one blessing from you, who are full of all perfection and able to shower down the fulfillment of all desires. I ask to have unfailing, unalloyed devotion (Achyuta-bhakti) for the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and for his dedicated devotees—especially you.\”* [193]

Mārkaṇḍeya did not ask for a longer life. He did not ask for a gold palace, a disease-free body, or material wealth. He asked only for **Bhakti** [193]. 

The great ācārya, **Śrīla Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa** (who was also known as Balabhadra), once answered a beautiful question: *“What is the beauty of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam?”* [202] Is it the print quality? Is it the design? Or is it even the philosophy? [202, 203]

He said: *“No. The true beauty of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is that it strictly forbids its followers from accessing or desiring anything from Kṛṣṇa except Bhakti, pure devotional service.”* [203] 

Why? Because Bhakti is the only thing Kṛṣṇa is missing. He is the proprietor of everything, but He is conquered only by love. 

And Bhakti is the only thing we actually need. 

Think about it: in every lifetime you have had in this material world, you have already had a mother, a father, a husband, a wife, and children [200]. You have had homes, wealth, and education [200]. Whether you were born as a human, a demigod, a cat, or an insect, you had a family and you had a body [200, 201]. 

But there are only two things you do not get in every birth: **a bona fide Guru and Lord Kṛṣṇa** [201]. 

These are the greatest treasures, and they are only available in the human form of life [149, 201]. If you find a genuine spiritual master, and through him you find Kṛṣṇa, your material existence is terminated for eternity [201]. You go back home, back to Godhead [201].

## Sadhus are Not Business Masters

Today, we have turned spiritual life into a supermarket. 

People go to sadhus and gurus to ask for material blessings, physical health, or business advice [203, 204]. But why go to a sadhu for business advice? If you want to be a successful businessperson, go to an experienced businessman—he will train you much better! [201, 203] If you are sick, go to a hospital and pay a doctor [203, 204]. 

A spiritual master is not a business master or a medical doctor [202, 203]. His profession is **Kṛṣṇa** [202, 203]. 

Srila Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura beautifully wrote: *\”O Sadhu! Your business is to distribute Kṛṣṇa. I do not have Kṛṣṇa, and that is why I have come to beg at your feet.\”* [203] 

The real business of a sādhu is to destroy our ignorance and cut our material attachments, planting the seed of Kṛṣṇa in our hearts [205].

But we must ask ourselves: *Are we ready to receive what he is actually offering?*

Often, when we face difficulties, we try to solve them by just \”shifting the load\” [211]. If our head hurts from carrying a heavy burden, we shift it to our shoulder and think we are making progress [211]. But the load is still on our body! [211] We are still in the material world. 

Instead of trying to make temporary adjustments, we must change our pattern of thinking [211]. We must rise above the bodily platform altogether [211].

## Method and Intention

We do not need to invent new, artificial processes [156]. We are not going to change the morning temple program from 4:30 AM to 6:30 AM [213]. We are not going to change the Hare Kṛṣṇa Maha-mantra, and we are not going to change our scriptures or our Deities [214]. We are going to do this same practice for the rest of our lives [213, 214]. 

But we must examine **how** we are doing it [214]. What is our method? What is our internal intention? [214]

Are we just executing dry, mechanical rituals? [156, 160] Are we sitting in the Bhagavatam class physically, while our mind is wandering off to our business, our family, or our material anxieties? [150] 

When we chant our rounds every day, what is the quality of our focus? [215] Are we chanting with a sincere desire to please Guru and Kṛṣṇa, or are we just trying to get the sixteen rounds out of the way so we can get on with our \”real\” material day? [131, 214, 215]

And most importantly: *How do we treat our fellow travelers on this path?* [215] Are we carrying hidden envy, politics, and critical thoughts in our minds while pretending to be pure devotees on the outside? [173, 215]

A genuine spiritual master plants the seed of Kṛṣṇa in our hearts, and he teaches us how to water and maintain that plant [205]. But the growth of that plant depends entirely on our sincerity, our honesty, and our willingness to surrender. 

Let us stop playing games with Kṛṣṇa. Let us stop asking the Guru for temporary toys, and let us beg only for that supreme benediction: unfailing, eternal love for Kṛṣṇa and his devotees [193, 205].

This is accountability. We have to regularly stop and ask ourselves: How am I actually living my Kṛṣṇa consciousness every day? [214, 215]

# Chapter 3: It Is Compulsory, Not Optional

Have you ever thought about why you are here? [120, 208] 

We are all looking for happiness, we are all looking for some peace, but we are looking in the wrong place [205, 210]. Look at a dog and a cat [145]. A dog sees something, but a cat will see and immediately close its eyes [145]. Which one is better—the dog or the cat [145]? The cat [145]! It means we should not be concerned with material things; we must be concerned only with Krishna [145]. Sincere practice means we are not concerned with what our material eyes are seeing, but with what our soul is hearing [145]. Just as we need our physical eyes all the time to see where we are going in this world, we need the Guru all the time to see the truth [119]. So having a Guru is compulsory [119]. 

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and our great ācāryas tell us there is a formula [117, 118, 209]. And the very first step in that formula is not a matter of “if you like it” or “if you feel like it.” [119] 

It is compulsory [119]. It is not optional [119].

In the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*, Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī outlines sixty-four principles of devotional service [117, 118]. Sixty-four! [117] But do you know how he begins? [117, 118] He doesn’t begin with big, complicated rituals [118]. He begins right from the spiritual master [117, 118]. 

The first five items of those sixty-four are entirely about the Guru:

1.  ***Guru-padāśraya:*** Accepting the shelter of the lotus feet of a bona fide spiritual master [118].
2.  ***Kṛṣṇa-dīkṣādi:*** Receiving formal initiation from him [118].
3.  ***Śikṣaṇam:*** Being submissively instructed by him on how to discharge service [118].
4.  ***Obedience:*** Obeying his orders with absolute faith and devotion [118].
5.  ***Following the Ācāryas:*** Walking strictly in the footsteps of the great previous teachers [118].

Rūpa Gosvāmī says you *must* accept the shelter of a spiritual master [119]. It is a must! [119] If you are a human being, having a Guru is compulsory [119]. Except you are not a human being! If you are a human being, Guru is compulsory [119, 131]. 

The moment you accept initiation, your real journey back to Godhead begins [120]. It doesn’t end there—it only begins [120]!

## **The Sacred Vow of Initiation**

What is initiation? [120] 

Initiation is a **contract** [120]. 

When we get initiated, we accept three basic commitments:
*   You promise to keep the four regulative principles (no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, no illicit sex) [120, 151].
*   You promise to chant a minimum of sixteen rounds of the Hare Kṛṣṇa maha-mantra every day [120, 184].
*   You accept a fixed, dedicated service under his direction [120].

And once we accept that contract, the Guru goes to work [120, 121]. 

His business is to gradually, day by day, remove the thick, heavy curtain of *māyā* from your eyes so you can actually see Kṛṣṇa [120, 175]. 

Think of it like going to a corporate job [120]. When you arrive at the office, there is a register, isn’t it? [120] Your name is written there [120]. The company records when you resume work, how many hours you spend on the job, what you actually do, and when you close [120]. Your promotions, your bonuses, and your salary increases depend entirely on that daily record [121].

When you take shelter of a bona fide Guru, he is also keeping a register [120, 121]. He is monitoring you every day [121]. He is looking at your hours, your chanting, your service, and most importantly, **your consciousness** [120, 121]. 

Every day, the Guru is marking our progress, looking especially at our consciousness [121]. 

And what is he marking? He is checking to see how much of your material contamination is being removed [121]. If we are sincere, we don’t add new dirt [121]. We just keep reducing what is already there [121]. 

Our material ego, our pride, our lust, our envy—we keep letting the Guru cut them down [121, 200]. And one day, your contamination is reduced to **zero** [121]. That is the day you achieve perfection [121]. On that day, your life begins to shine like the brilliant midday sun [121]!

## **Sincere Effort to Receive Divine Mercy**

Some people sit back and say, *“Well, if Kṛṣṇa wants me to be a devotee, He will show me mercy. If my Guru wants to save me, he will just shower his grace on me. Why do I have to work so hard?”* [123, 124]

Laziness! You are not serious [124]! 

I warn you: **Mercy alone is partiality. Why not everybody? Why only you?** [123] We are all Kṛṣṇa's parts and parcels. His mercy is always available [123]. 

The truth is, **divine mercy is constantly, eternally available** [123, 125]. It is always there—not that it has to come, but it is already there, waiting for us [123]. But if you want to receive it, **you must straighten your hand to take it!** [123, 124]

If someone is offering you something, but you keep your hands tightly folded behind your back, how will you ever get it? [123] You have to straighten your hand [123]. 

Reaching out means putting in sincere, serious effort [122, 124]. It means doing something practical in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, taking the instructions on your head and executing them in your daily activities [124, 128]. 

But instead, we sit in our comfortable laziness and complain: *“Kṛṣṇa has never shown me mercy yet. If He shows me mercy, I will immediately become a pure devotee.”* [124] 

We wait for eternity, making excuses, while the treasure is lying right in front of us [124, 140].

## **The Beggar’s Gold**

Look at how we behave when mercy is offered to us. Let us look at a beautiful, striking history [125].

One day, Lord Śiva—the greatest of all Vaiṣṇavas—was traveling through the material world with his wife, Pārvatī [125]. As they were walking along, they passed a beggar sitting by the roadside [125]. 

Pārvatī looked at him and pleaded, *“Prabhu, please help him.”* [125]

Lord Śiva looked at him and turned his face away [125]. As they were coming back from their journey, they met the beggar again [125]. Pārvatī said, *“Prabhu, please help this man. Please.”* [125]

So Lord Śiva straightened his hand to the beggar [125]. He opened his hand, and dropped something precious right there [125]. 

The beggar looked at what was dropped in his hand and thought, *“What is this?”* [126] He did not value it, and simply gave it to somebody else [126]. That other person took the precious gift and instantly became wealthy [126]. 

Meanwhile, the beggar remained standing there, begging just as before [126]. 

Lord Śiva turned to Pārvatī and said, *“See. I gave it to him, but he gave it to somebody else. He is not right for it.”* [126]

We are exactly like that beggar [126]. We are getting so much, yet we still remain spiritually poor [126]. Kṛṣṇa has given us the Holy Name, *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, devotees, and the shelter of Guru [23, 118, 120, 151]. Yet we often fail to appreciate what we have been given [18, 126]. We remain poor because we don't know how to receive the mercy [123, 126].

Let us stop being roadside beggars [125, 126]. Let us straighten our hands, take the gold we have been given, and honor the contract of initiation with our very life and soul [119, 120, 123].

# Chapter 4: Servants, Not Bosses

What is the real qualification to be a disciple? [178] 

If you look at the scriptures, the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and the instructions of the great ācāryas, they define the qualities of a genuine disciple very clearly [175, 176]. But today, we have developed our own ideas [173, 174]. We think that if we can speak nice philosophy, play some instruments nicely, or raise some money, we are first-class disciples [159, 255]. 

But Śrīla Prabhupāda writes a very heavy instruction in his purports [175]. He says: **“The servant or disciple of the spiritual master should be free from false prestige, never considering himself to be the doer.”** [175, 177]

Prestige is a very dangerous thing [179]. If there is prestige in your heart, you cannot serve the spiritual master [179]. Why? Because the moment prestige enters, you want to become the boss [179]! You want to be respected [457, 458]. You want people to look at you and say, *“Oh, see what a great devotee he is. See how many books he is distributing. See how nicely he is managing.”* [159, 201]

The moment you think like that, you are no longer a servant [179]. You are a boss [179]. And a boss cannot be a disciple [179]. To serve the spiritual master, you must be completely selfless, without false prestige [179].

---

## **Relinquishing False Ego and Pride**

We hear this phrase all the time in our philosophy: *“I am not the doer.”* [178] It is a common word [178]. We recite it, we speak it in classes [178]. But do we understand what it means? [178, 182] 

Let me tell you a story [178]. 

One day, many years ago, a disciple was serving Śrīla Prabhupāda [178]. This disciple had heard the philosophy very nicely, but his mind had made a very clever twist [178]. He went to Prabhupāda and said: 

*“Prabhupāda, I am not the doer [178]. So today, I am not going on sankirtan [178]. I am not going out to distribute books, because I am not the one distributing them [178]. It is actually you who is distributing the books [178]. Since I am not the doer, why should I go?”* [178]

He thought he was being highly philosophical! [178] He was using the scripture to justify his laziness [180]. 

But what did Śrīla Prabhupāda tell him? [178] 

Prabhupāda looked at him and said: **“That is not the meaning. That is not the meaning!”** [178]

Prabhupāda explained that the spiritual master is the one who empowers the disciple to distribute the books [178]. Without the mercy of the spiritual master, you cannot do anything [178]. You cannot even open your mouth to speak about Kṛṣṇa [178, 179]. So we must never forget the person who is giving us the intelligence, the strength, and the ability to perform the service [179]. 

We are not the doer, yes [178]. But that does not mean we sit back and do nothing [180]. It means we execute the service with deep gratitude. We should never forget the person who is giving us the intelligence and ability to perform our service [182]. If you succeed, you don’t take the credit [29, 30]. You give the credit to your spiritual master, just as Śrīla Prabhupāda always gave all the credit to his spiritual master for everything he achieved [119].

---

## **The Guru Waking the Disciples**

There is another quality mentioned here: **“A disciple should be active and never lazy.”** [180] 

I want to say something very direct about our behavior today [180]. In our temples, we see a complete reversal of spiritual etiquette [180]. 

**A Guru should not wake the disciples from sleep [180]. A disciple should wake the Guru!** [180]

But what do we see? [180] The spiritual master is awake, preparing for the program [180]. He is performing the arati for the Deities, he is chanting his rounds, and the disciples are still in bed, sleeping peacefully [180]! 

This is opposite [180]! It is upside down [180]. How can we make any spiritual advancement if we are behaving like this? [180] 

We cannot [180]. Laziness is the enemy of bhakti [21, 180]. A genuine disciple is always active, always eager, and always ready to serve [180]. They don’t need to be pushed, dragged, or begged to attend the morning program [213, 237]. Their love for the Guru makes them wake up early because they don’t want to miss a single opportunity to please him [138, 251].

And let me tell you what Śrīla Prabhupāda mentioned in one of his purports [180]. He warned us: **“The moment a disciple says ‘no’ to the Guru, at that very moment, the roots in the seed is cut off.”** [180, 183] This is extremely serious. We must think about this very carefully. 

---

## **The Caretaker Consciousness**

Another heavy quality of a disciple is that they must **“give up all sense of proprietorship over the objects of the senses, including his wife, children, home, and society.”** [175]

Who among us can claim to be the permanent owner of anything in this world? [176] 

We build a nice house, we get a nice family, we have a husband, a wife, and children, and we think: *“This is mine. This is my family, this is my home.”* [176, 181] 

But Śrīla Prabhupāda writes in his purport that these material relationships are temporary [176]. They appear and disappear just like bubbles on the surface of the ocean [176]. You are walking on the beach, you see a bubble, and the next second it is gone [176]. That is your family [176]. That is your wealth [176]. That is your prestigious position [84, 176]. 

If you think you are the creator of your children, let me ask you: *If parents are the creators of their children’s bodies, why can’t they stop them from dying?* [176] Why can’t a parent simply create a new body for their child when they get sick? [176, 177] And why do parents themselves die? [177] Why don’t they just create new bodies for themselves to replace the old ones? [177]

The truth is, we create nothing [177]. 

Kṛṣṇa is the only creator [177]. He creates the material elements, and He creates every body [177]. Therefore, before death comes and violently drags these things out of our tight grip, we should voluntarily hand them over to Kṛṣṇa [177]. We must engage them in the service of the spiritual master [177]. 

When you use your home, your money, and your family to serve the Guru, then instead of causing you deep lamentation, they become the source of unlimited spiritual happiness [177]. 

We should understand that everything belongs to the spiritual master. Including one's spouse and children, all are the Guru's properties [184]. Everything is the Guru's property [184]. Guru has given His property in our care, so we are simply to take care of it [184]. It does not belong to us; we are simply to take care of it [184].

To explain this caretaker consciousness, Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura is our perfect, shining example [181]. 

In one of his beautiful songs, Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura wrote: *“My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, my wife, my children, my home, my properties—everything belongs strictly to You. I am simply a caretaker.”* [181]

And he did not just write this as a nice poem [182]. He lived it [182]! He acted exactly like that in his daily life [182]. He had twelve children [182]! 

Now, please think about this [182]. Today, we have just one child, or two children, and we have so many complaints [182]! We say: *“Oh, my child is so difficult. I have no time for Sādhana. I am so stressed, I have so many headaches.”* [182] 

But Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura had twelve children, and he had **no complaint** [185]! 

Why? Because his consciousness was clear [182]. He knew: *“I am not the owner of these twelve children [181, 182]. They belong to Kṛṣṇa [181]. They are the Guru’s property [181]. Kṛṣṇa has simply placed them in my care for a few short years [181]. My only duty is to take care of them and train them to become pure devotees.”* [181, 182] 

Because he had no false prestige and no sense of proprietorship, his life was free from all material complexities and obstacles [182]. He used everything within his reach to serve Kṛṣṇa [182].

## **Loving Friendship and Undeviated Focus**

A genuine disciple should be endowed with feelings of loving friendship towards the spiritual master, and he should never become deviated or bewildered [185]. We must never become deviated [185]. If we are listening attentively to the spiritual master, all of our doubts will be cleared [186].

Why does the Guru speak? The Guru speaks to destroy the deep ignorance in the life of the disciple [186]. When the disciple listens attentively, that ignorance is destroyed [186].

The servant should always work for advancement in spiritual understanding, and he should be free from envy [179, 186]. He should not envy anyone [179].

## **Avoiding Useless Conversation**

Any conversation which does not involve Kṛṣṇa is useless [186]. Devotees should speak only about the subject that concerns Kṛṣṇa [186]. This is the topic devotees should discuss when they come together [187]. We should talk about Kṛṣṇa, discuss how to perform our duties nicely for His pleasure, and discuss what we write from the scriptures [186, 187]. Anything outside of this is useless and worthless conversation [187].

According to Śrīla Prabhupāda, useless secular conversation is like the croaking of a frog, which simply invites the biting of a snake [187]. And what is our own snake? Our snake is death [187]. When we spend our time talking about unnecessary things, we forget that we are wasting our time [187]. The time is going imperceptibly [187]. We waste our time, and then death comes, leaving us with nothing but lamentation [187].

I have seen many devotees who have spent many years in this movement, who have spent so much time in meditation, yet they are lamenting when they are leaving the body [188]. They are angry when they are leaving the body [188]. They are worried about what they are leaving behind [188]. That means failure [188]!

A devotee should never lament [188]. We should not be angry, frustrated, or bewildered [188]. A devotee should live joyfully, smiling [188]. That means success [188]!

Let us drop our false prestige, wake up from our laziness, and strictly follow our spiritual master’s instructions with an open, loving heart [179, 180, 185]. This is Kṛṣṇa consciousness [188].

# Chapter 5: Fighting Our Spiritual Laziness

What is the greatest lack in our modern spiritual movement? [14] 

If we look around honestly, what is missing is a deep, unshakeable dedication to the spiritual master [14]. Today, there is a very prominent disease of disconnection [14]. Many of us are physically present in the temple, but we are disconnected from the heart of our Guru [14]. 

Let us look at a person who had a truly deep connection: my own spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami [15]. I was fortunate to serve him for many years, and I saw his qualities firsthand [16]. He did not live an artificial, superficial spiritual life [16, 201]. He communicated with his Guru, Śrīla Prabhupāda, every day [14, 15]. 

My own spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, maintained an extraordinarily deep, intimate spiritual connection with his spiritual master, Śrīla Prabhupāda [15, 28]. He communicated with his Guru every day through constant, submissive prayer and accountability [14, 15, 28].

Every night, before Srila Gurudeva went to sleep, he would look at his diary to review the tasks he had planned for that day [15, 29]. Even though his services and activities were fully completed, he would meditate submissively, reporting his day directly to Śrīla Prabhupāda [15, 29]. He would examine his heart and ask his Guru if there was anything he had failed to execute, or if he had done everything exactly as his master wanted him to do [15, 29]. This daily review was his constant, living communication and accountability with his spiritual master [15, 29].

This same uncompromised connection guided Srila Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami during challenging GBC leadership meetings [16, 30]. That quality of uncompromised, prayerful loyalty is extremely rare today [4, 32].

Srila Gurudeva used his massive intelligence to design a Kṛṣṇa consciousness that is sustainable—both materially and spiritually [13, 252]. When I started, he gave us a single, heavy word that has stayed with me to this day: **“lazy”** [13, 252]. He used that single word, but there are so many deep things inside it [13, 252]. He warned us that we are spiritually lazy [13, 252]. 

Because of our laziness, we cannot access the incredible wealth we have already been given [13, 14, 252]. We have been given the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, which is the absolute solution to every problem in human life [154]. Within Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books, he has packed the realizations of all the greatest ācāryas—Srila Jiva Goswami, Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura, Srila Sanātana Gosvāmī, and Srila Rūpa Gosvāmī [15, 23, 253]. ## **Jñāna vs. Bhakti**

There is a dangerous direction many of us are taking [25]. I see it every day [25]. We are moving toward *jñāna*—dry, intellectual knowledge—instead of *bhakti* [25].

# Chapter 6: If You Don’t Plan Your Day, Your Sādhana Will Suffer

There is a setting in the fifth canto of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Chapter Eleven, where the great self-realized soul Jaḍa Bharata is instructing King Rahūgaṇa [117]. 

The text states:
> *“When the living entity’s mind becomes absorbed in the sense gratification of the material world, it brings about its conditioned life and suffering within a material situation. However, when the mind becomes unattached to material enjoyment, it becomes the cause of liberation.”* [117]

Everyone is suffering in this material world because of the mind [131]. It is the mind that causes all the sufferings we are going through [131]. Our present situation and body were created by our past minds, and our next body is being created by our mind right now [129, 140]. If we keep our minds engaged in material activities, it will cause continuous bondage and make us remain in this world in different bodies, suffering the consequences of our actions [118, 130]. Therefore, the only real solution is to engage the mind fully in Kṛṣṇa conscious activity [118, 130]. This is called spiritual engagement [129].

To explain this properly, our scriptures give a beautiful analogy of a lamp [129]. When the flame in the lamp burns the wick improperly, the lamp becomes blackened with soot [129]. But when the lamp is filled with pure ghee and is burning properly, it gives bright illumination [129, 135]. 

Improper burning is the misuse of our mind—when the mind is absorbed in material sense gratification, it blackens our consciousness and causes suffering [129, 135]. But a lamp filled with ghee is when a person’s mind is properly engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [135]. When the mind is detached from material enjoyment and filled with the ghee of devotion, it brings about the original brightness and enlightenment of the soul [129, 135].

## **The Fortunate Jaḍa Bharata**

To understand how to train the mind, let us look at the history of Jaḍa Bharata [119]. 

Jaḍa Bharata was once a great emperor who renounced his entire kingdom, opulence, fame, and power to go to the forest to practice devotion [119]. But while in the forest, he became distracted by his attachment to a baby deer [120]. He forgot what took him to the forest, and eventually died thinking of the deer [120].

But let me tell you: Jaḍa Bharata was a *fortunate* Jaḍa Bharata [119]. Why? Because he made his mistake only once, and he learned his lesson [121, 134]. He said, *“Once beaten, twice shy.”* [121] For him, it was not a normal case; it was an extreme case [145]. Kṛṣṇa used him to teach all of us a lesson so that we would be careful while dealing with the material energy [144, 145]. In his next life, Jaḍa Bharata planned his Kṛṣṇa consciousness perfectly [121]. He blocked himself completely from the world, pretending to be deaf and dumb so that nothing could enter his mind [121, 122]. He was living in this world, but he was not of this world [122]. 

But many of us are *unfortunate* Jaḍa Bharatas [119]. Some of us left our family, left our secular education, and abandoned everything to come to Kṛṣṇa consciousness [121]. But after some years, we drift back to the very things we rejected [120, 121]. 

In Sanskrit, they have a word for this: **vetasi** [120]. *Vetasi* means to eat your own vomit [120]. To leave material sense gratification behind, only to gradually take it back again, is a condemned, unfortunate life [120, 121]. We make mistakes a hundred times and still do not learn, whereas the fortunate Jaḍa Bharata learned everything from a single mistake [121, 134, 145].

## **Mr. Boeing: The Mind’s False Promise**

The mind always tricks us. It says: *“You are not happy? That is because you don’t have this object. When you get it, you will be happy.”* [139] The mind will never tell us that this is not the way [139]. 

Let me tell you about Mr. Boeing [136]. At that time, he was the number-one wealthy American in the world [136]. He had only one child, a son who was studying animal husbandry [136]. When the son finished his studies and was preparing his dissertation, he went into the forest to study animal life—and because he went in without security, an animal made a feast out of him [136, 137]. He was gone [137]! 

Journalists went to Mr. Boeing and said, *“You are the number-one American, you must be the happiest person in the world.”* [137] 

But Mr. Boeing replied, *“I am not the happiest person. I am the most unhappy person in the world. My only child died. All my wealth, my sacrifices, everything is a waste. How can I be happy?”* [137] 

But look at the foolishness of the mind [139]. Those who use his aircraft look out the window in business class, drinking their water, and think, *“See how I am enjoying! I wish I were Mr. Boeing.”* [138] The person who made the aircraft is miserable, but we are dreaming of being him [138]! 

We go before Deities, standing in the rain, praying, *“Jai Jagannātha! Please make me Mr. Boeing!”* [138, 139] We do so much austerity just for some material adjustment, because the mind has convinced us that if we get what we want, we will be happy [139]. 

We must train our minds to be detached from these false promises [139, 141].

## **Squeezing the Dead Body**

Because Jaḍa Bharata had planned his consciousness perfectly, he was completely carefree and situated in pure devotion [122, 174]. 

One day, Maharaja Rahūgaṇa forced Jaḍa Bharata to carry his palanquin [122, 123]. Because Jaḍa Bharata was walking carefully to avoid stepping on ants, the palanquin was jerking [123]. The king became angry and shouted: *“What is this? Are you a dead body? Don’t you know you are carrying a king?”* [123, 132]

Jaḍa Bharata quietly looked at him and said: *“Today you are a king, and I am carrying you [123, 132]. Tomorrow, you will be my carrier, and I will be the king [123, 132]. You want to punish me, but you are actually trying to punish a dead body [123, 132]. If you grind iron into powder, and you continue to grind it, will it ever become iron again?”* [124, 132]

When the king heard these words, he offered up his senses, accepted initiation from him, and was delivered [132].

Jaḍa Bharata was teaching us a heavy lesson: this material body is just a dead body [132, 133]. And when our mind is focusing on gratifying the dead body, is that life [133]? 

No! But the mind convinces us: *“Squeeze it! Squeeze the dead body! You are not doing it properly, squeeze it more, squeeze it more, and you will get some juice from it!”* [133] But let me ask you: how can juice come out of a dead body [133]? Squeeze it all you want, but you will get nothing [133]. 

Yet we keep trying, because our mind is not trained [145]. The more the senses are gratified, the worse our spiritual situation becomes [134]. If we want to be happy, we must practice detachment and remove the mind from material enjoyment [134].

## **Intensifying Our Practice on Special Days**

The *Padma Purāṇa* describes this history [155]. One day, Lord Kṛṣṇa went to visit Yamarāja, the lord of death [155]. While He was there, Kṛṣṇa heard a horrific, groaning cry of agony [155]. The sound was coming from the hellish inhabitants being tortured by Yamarāja’s assistants for their sins [155]. 

Kṛṣṇa became deeply concerned and full of sorrow [155]. He was thinking seriously: *“I am suffering like this, what should I do? I must find a way to help these poor conditioned souls get My mercy easily.”* [155] 

Thinking and thinking about this, Kṛṣṇa went to sleep to get a solution for the problem [156]. While He was sleeping, a powerful demon named Mura Asura came to fight Him [156]. Mura Asura came there and started talking: *“I will kill you because you are running away from fighting! You are sleeping, I will kill you!”* [156] 

As he was talking like that, a female personality came out from Kṛṣṇa’s body and killed the demon Mura [156]. Kṛṣṇa woke up and saw the lady standing there. *“Who did this?”* He asked [156]. She replied, *“It is me. The demon was trying to disturb you, so I killed the demon.”* [156] 

Kṛṣṇa was so pleased that He told her, *“Because today is the eleventh day of the month, your name is Ekādaśī [156]. Your duty is to chase away the Papa Purusha (the personification of sin) and give people a chance to get My mercy easily.”* [156] 

The Papa Purusha realized he had nowhere left to run, so he came back and begged Kṛṣṇa for shelter [156]. Kṛṣṇa told him, *“She will appear two times in a month [156]. So on those days, you can enter inside grains, and she will not disturb you there.”* [156] 

This is why eating grains on Ekādaśī is **extremely dangerous** [156]. It is so dangerous that Sri Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu recommended that a devotee should not eat grains on Ekādaśī—even in a dream! [156] 

On Ekādaśī, we must design our lives in such a way that we have more time to chant more rounds [156]. Sixteen rounds is the minimum prescribed standard—the “minimum property” Śrīla Prabhupāda gave us for daily maintenance [156]. But on Ekādaśī, we should take advantage and increase our chanting [156]. Don't chant only sixteen—sixteen is too small, especially on Ekādaśī! [156] We should go far beyond the minimum; we should double our rounds [156, 157]. 

And that requires reducing what we talk the whole day [157]. Reduce it, make sure it is within only Kṛṣṇa consciousness [157]. Let our speech be within Kṛṣṇa consciousness, not outside [157]. Especially on Ekādaśī, we should try to control our speech so that we don't talk anything against any devotee [157]. Never! [157] If you want to say something, let it be positive—a glorification, an appreciation [157]. Find a way to talk good about other devotees, and spend the day reading about the previous ācāryas and appreciating the hard work they did to establish a system that is saving our lives [157].

## **Engaging the Mind in Service**

Let me share a beautiful pastime of how my spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva, celebrated Janmāṣṭamī [157]. It is a lesson in how to occupy the mind so that Maya has no way to enter [157, 159]. 

When we were new in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, Srila Gurudeva—who was our Regional Secretary at the time—designed a very powerful meditation for Janmāṣṭamī [157, 159]. He literally **tricked our minds** so that we forgot about the physical struggle of fasting [159]. 

He gave us two mandatory assignments for Janmāṣṭamī:

1.  **The Chapter Narration:** Every devotee was assigned exactly one specific chapter from the *Kṛṣṇa* book [157]. We had to read it, condense it, and stand before all the senior devotees to narrate the entire chapter from memory! [157, 158]
2.  **The Personal Letter:** We had to sit down and write a deeply personal, confidential letter to Lord Kṛṣṇa [158]. 

Do you know what happened? [159] 

The entire temple was in an absolute state of spiritual absorption [159]. We were so busy thinking about our chapters and wondering, *“How will I write this letter to Kṛṣṇa? Will He reply? If He replies, how will I get the reply?”* that nobody even remembered the hunger or the fasting! [158, 159] 

Even the devotees who were cooking for the massive festival—before they could even touch a pot, they had to sit and read their assigned chapters [159]. While they were standing over the hot stoves, stirring the preparations, they were silently memorizing their pastimes [159]. 

The cooking was first-class because there was no gossip, no talking, and **no animosity**! [159] Everyone was simply consumed by the thoughts of Kṛṣṇa [159]. 

That is real Janmāṣṭamī [157]. If we plan our Kṛṣṇa consciousness like this, we will soon be there [159]. Very, very soon we will be there [159].

## **Chanting Sincerely and Audibly**

How should we chant our Japa? [160] 

Śrīla Prabhupāda, drawing from the commentaries of Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura, gives us the ultimate key: **Chant nicely, which means distinctly and loudly!** [160]

Why loud and distinct? [160] 

Because when you shout at someone, they are forced to give you their attention [160]. Chanting loudly and distinctly is literally **shouting at your own distracted mind to sit down and listen!** [160] It forces the ear to hear, and when the ear hears, the mind cannot wander off to Maya [160]. 

We must chant clearly while our body is still fit and healthy [160]. At the time of death, your bodily functions will be completely in disorder [160]. You will be bewildered and in pain [160]. You may not be able to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra very distinctly at that moment [160]. 

But Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura reveals a secret: If you have practiced chanting loudly and distinctly during your life, then at the time of death, Kṛṣṇa Himself will look at you and feel: **“This person has called My name clearly throughout their life. It is now My personal duty to give them all protection.”** [161] 

What a beautiful, comforting promise! [161] Kṛṣṇa takes it as His personal obligation to save you because you called Him clearly [161].

## **First Japa, Then Management**

Let us be very clear about our priorities: **Sādhana is our primary duty.** [67, 162] Services come *after* Sādhana [162]. 

If we want to be a good book distributor, we must chant our rounds first [162]. Otherwise, it is just ordinary selling of books, not a spiritual activity [162]. We must chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra distinctly and clearly, then book distribution becomes a spiritual activity [162]. It is the same with everything else we do in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [162]. One is not qualified to cook for Kṛṣṇa without chanting, and one should not do Deity worship without chanting [162]. Our consciousness has to be purified to do what is offerable to Kṛṣṇa [162].

But today, we are managing in Japa—it is wrong! [162] That is not what Śrīla Prabhupāda gave to us [162]. Managing in a class is wrong! [162] Prabhupāda gave us distinctly clear activities: a time for Japa, a time for classes, a time for book distribution, and a time for cooking [162]. 

If you do not chant your rounds properly *before* you step out to manage, you are going to manage the devotees for your own selfish benefit and prestige [162, 164]. A good leader manages people for *their* benefit [162]. If you chant sincerely first, you will receive the spiritual potency from your Kṛṣṇa consciousness to lead and manage the devotees purely for their spiritual benefit [162, 164]. A good devotee will be a good manager because his practice makes him good, so he will manage devotees for the benefit of devotees [162, 163].

Purity is our only strength [164]. Let us take our Sādhana seriously, shout at our distracted minds, and straighten our hands to receive the mercy [156, 160, 164].

# Chapter 8: Seeing Kṛṣṇa Through the Ears


How do we deepen our connection with Kṛṣṇa? [62]

In the modern world, our motto is always: *“See to believe.”* We want to see God with our own material eyes [62]. We say, *“If God exists, let Him stand before me, and then I will believe.”* But *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and our great ācāryas tell us that the process of spiritual seeing begins through the ears [64, 97]. 

There is a beautiful, defining teaching in *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (1.8.36) given by Queen Kuntī:

> *śṛṇvanti gāyanti gṛṇanty abhīkṣṇaśaḥ*
> *smaranti nandanti tavehitaṁ janāḥ*
> *ta eva paśyanty acireṇa tāvakaṁ*
> *bhava-pravāhoparamaṁ padāmbujam* [61]

*“O Kṛṣṇa, those who continuously hear, chant, and repeat Your transcendental activities, or take pleasure in others doing so, certainly see Your lotus feet, which alone can stop the repetition of birth and death.”* [61]

Please listen carefully to this word Queen Kuntī uses: *abhīkṣṇaśaḥ*—continuously [61, 63]. She says we must hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, non-stop [63]. 

Why? Because our material eyes are dry, and they are completely blind [64, 81]. 

We have this immense disease of wanting to see Kṛṣṇa physically, but we do not realize that our present conditional vision is completely unqualified to see Him [62]. When we look at the beautiful Deities on the altar, we don’t faint [160]. We don’t cry [80]. We see the Deities, and our minds are still thinking about our money, our business, and our family [150, 196]. We look at them and we think: *“Oh, this is just wood, this is just stone, this is an idol.”* [78, 79] But the Deity is not an idol; Kṛṣṇa is personally standing there [62]. It is our own lack of qualification, our own dry eyes that make us blind to His presence [64, 81]. Offenses against the Vaiṣṇavas have dried up our eyes [81]. We are trying to see Kṛṣṇa before we have learned how to *hear* Him [64, 81].

---

## **Sincere Hearing vs. Physical Proximity**

We must understand that physical proximity to God does not guarantee spiritual vision [97]. 

When Lord Kṛṣṇa was personally present on this earth, walking, talking, and performing His pastimes, did everyone see Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead? No [62, 64]! 

Materialists like Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Jarāsandha, and Śiśupāla were highly qualified personalities [62]. They had immense wealth, political power, and high social standing [62]. But they were completely blind to the presence of the Lord [62]. 

Rāvaṇa saw Lord Rāmacandra physically [64]. But how did he see Him? He saw Him as a weak, unfortunate prince who was crying because his wife had been stolen [64]. Rāvaṇa thought, *“I will easily kill this helpless man.”* [64] 

Kaṁsa saw Kṛṣṇa every day in his mind, but he saw Him only as a terrifying enemy with a sword, waiting to cut off his head [29, 64]. 

Jarāsandha saw Kṛṣṇa face-to-face and fought Him seventeen times [64]. But his vision was blocked by the thick, heavy curtain of material desire [64]. He saw Kṛṣṇa only as a cowherd boy, a runaway, and a mortal foe whom he wanted to destroy [64]. 

Hiraṇyakaśipu performed the most extreme physical austerities in the history of the universe—standing on one leg for tens of thousands of years [65]. He did so much severe austerity that his brain was burning [65]! But because his heart was full of envy and pride, what did he get at the end? He did not get Kṛṣṇa; he got death [65]. 

By contrast, what austerity did the child Prahlāda Mahārāja perform? [65] He did not stand on one leg. He did not do severe fasting [65]. He simply heard. He sat and heard about Kṛṣṇa with complete faith from his spiritual master, Nārada Muni [65, 142]. And because he heard with his mind, he got Kṛṣṇa. He got protection and mercy [65, 173]. This is the power of hearing.

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu once said a very heavy thing: **“If I do not give My mercy to someone, I can stand face to face with them, and they will never see Me.”** [66] 

We cannot force our way into Kṛṣṇa’s presence using our material qualifications, our wealth, or our academic degrees [62, 123]. True spiritual vision is not a physical adjustment; it is a gift that is granted only when our hearts are purified [62, 67]. And that purification can only come when we learn to sit down submissively and hear from the right sources [68].

---

## **The Raging River and Sincere Hearing**

To understand the difference between mechanical ritual and genuine, attentive hearing, Śrīla Gaura Govinda Mahārāja shared a powerful history [69].

There were once two ladies who lived in the same village [69]. Every day, for seven days, they went together to hear a great sage speak on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* [69]. They sat side-by-side in the same audience, listening to the same speaker [69]. 

For seven days, the speaker repeatedly emphasized a single, powerful instruction: **“This material existence is a vast, dangerous ocean, but you can easily cross it simply by chanting ‘Govinda, Govinda’ from the core of your heart.”** [69]

At the end of the seven days, the two ladies began their journey back to their homes [69]. On the way, they came to a wide river [69]. While they had been sitting in the kathā, a heavy rainstorm had fallen, and the river had become swollen, raging, and extremely dangerous to cross [69]. 

The first lady looked at the turbulent water and remembered the words of the speaker [69]. She thought: *“Our spiritual guide told us that by chanting ‘Govinda,’ we can easily cross the entire ocean of material birth and death. If we can cross the ocean, then what is this tiny, insignificant river? It is nothing!”* [69] 

With absolute, unwavering faith, she stepped straight onto the raging water, chanting **“Govinda, Govinda!”** [69] Miraculously, she did not sink [69]. She walked cleanly across the surface of the river, as if she were walking on dry land, and reached the other side [69].

The second lady saw this and thought: *“Wow! My friend is walking on water! Let me do the same thing.”* [69] She jumped into the river, but the moment her feet touched the water, she began to sink [69]. The heavy current dragged her down, and she began to drown [69]. 

As she was going under, she screamed desperately to her friend on the bank: *“Help me! Please, save me! Come and pull me out!”* [69]

The first lady called back: *“Don’t you remember what the speaker told us? Why don’t you chant Govinda?”* [69] But the drowning lady could not chant [69]. She fumbled, choked, and drowned in the river [69].

Why did this happen? [69] They sat in the same class, heard the same speaker, and went to the same temple [69]. But for the second lady, she did not hear [69]. Her body was in the class, but her mind was completely somewhere else [172]. 

When her friends asked her, *“Oh, how was the lecture today?”* she would smile nicely and say, *“Oh, the speaker was so wonderful! He spoke the best lecture I have ever had!”* [70] But if they asked her, *“Can you share one point that he made?”* she had nothing to say [70]. She couldn’t remember a single thing [80]. 

She did not hear [69]. Because she did not hear, she could not remember. Because she could not remember, she had no faith. And because she had no faith, when the crisis came, she could not act. She drowned [69]. 

Genuine hearing is not just about the sound waves hitting your eardrum and falling off [192]. Hearing means that **the message penetrates the ear, pierces through our defenses, and goes straight into the heart, completely transforming our consciousness.** [192] If you hear with faith, even if you are completely unqualified, your material situation will be destroyed [64, 67]. You will walk across the raging rivers of this world with complete peace and fearlessness [69, 173].

## **The Sweet Flower Handcuffs of Vṛndāvana**

Why does Kṛṣṇa demand this deep, attentive hearing? Because He is not conquered by dry scholarship, heavy penances, or formal awe and reverence [112, 197, 198]. He is conquered only by spontaneous, informal love [62, 110]. 

Let us look at the sweet, playful nature of this love. Look at a beautiful, joyful pastime of Vṛndāvana [65].

One day, Lord Kṛṣṇa was sitting in one corner of the forest, and His cowherd friends, the gopas, put Him on a mock throne [65]. They worshiped Him, and after the worship, Kṛṣṇa looked at them and said: *“Now that I am the king, you must go and announce it. Anyone who disobeys, arrest them and bring them to the king for punishment! [65]”*

The cowherd boys were very excited [66]. Madhu Maṅgala, His dear brāhmaṇa friend, was the first person to start jumping here and there, shouting loudly: *“Kṛṣṇa is the king! If you see anyone picking flowers in this forest, arrest the person and take them to the king for punishment! [66]”*

As they were marching through the forest making this announcement, they met Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī and the gopīs [66]. The gopīs looked at the boys and said: *“What did you say? Kṛṣṇa is the king? No! Rādhārāṇī is the queen of this forest! [66]”*

Madhu Maṅgala stepped forward, waved his hand, and argued: *“No! You can’t say that! Kṛṣṇa is the king! [66]”*

The gopīs looked at each other and said: *“We have to deal with this boy seriously. [67]”* 

Suddenly, the gopīs descended on the cowherd boys [67]. The boys were completely outnumbered [67]. Seeing the fierce determination of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s party, all the boys ran away—but Madhu Maṅgala was caught [67]! He was arrested and thrown to the ground [67]. 

The gopīs looked down at him and demanded: *“Now you tell us. Who is the king here? [67]”*

Madhu Maṅgala looked at their faces, realized his friends had abandoned him, and quickly changed his side: *“Oh, actually... I think Kṛṣṇa is your queen! [67]”*

The gopīs laughed and said: *“For the heavy offense you made of declaring Kṛṣṇa the king, we are going to handcuff you! [67]”*

Now, what were their handcuffs made of? [67] In this material world, handcuffs are made of cold, hard iron [67]. But in Vṛndāvana, the gopīs handcuffed Madhu Maṅgala with beautiful, fragrant, soft flowers [67]! They chained his hands tightly with garlands of sweet blossoms [67].

Madhu Maṅgala looked at his flower handcuffs and said: *“Well, handcuffing me is one thing. But for my next punishment... I want you to give me laddus! [68]”* 

Since he was a “laddu brāhmaṇa” who didn’t know how to chant any Sanskrit mantras, the gopīs took great pleasure in executing this punishment [68]. They loaded Madhu Maṅgala’s pockets on both sides with big, sweet laddus, and stuffed his mouth so full of laddus that he couldn’t even speak [68]! 

Then they told him: *“Now go back to your friend and bring him here to declare that our queen is the supreme head of this forest [68].”*

Madhu Maṅgala waddled back to Kṛṣṇa, who was still sitting on his mock throne [68]. 

Kṛṣṇa looked at him, barely recognizing him, and asked: *“Hey, my friend, what happened to you? [68]”*

Madhu Maṅgala mumbled through his mouthful of laddus: *“What happened to me? You are asking me? This is the result of declaring you the king! I am handcuffed like this! They beat me and pulled me seriously! They caught me completely! [68, 69] The only way you can save yourself is to come with me right now and surrender. Otherwise, all of them are coming here, and you will see what they will do to you! [69]”*

Madhu Maṅgala took Kṛṣṇa by the hand, dragged Him from the throne, and took Him straight to Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī’s assembly [69]. 

When they arrived, the gopīs looked at them and demanded: *“Kṛṣṇa, tell us. Who is the queen of this forest? [69]”*

Kṛṣṇa looked at Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, bowed His head, and said: *“Rādhārāṇī is the queen [70].”*

Immediately, the gopīs picked up Kṛṣṇa, sat Him on the same throne next to Rādhārāṇī, and worshiped them both together with great love [70]. 

**This is the feast for the soul [70].** 

We take prasāda for our physical bodies every day [70]. But hearing these sweet, playful, and intimate activities of the Lord is the true food that nourishes our dry, parched souls [70]. When we hear with faith, we are handcuffed not by the iron chains of material karma, but by the sweet flower handcuffs of Kṛṣṇa’s love [67, 70].

Let us stop being superficial, mechanical ritualists [183]. Let us put away our dry, academic arguments, sit submissively on the floor, and hear with our whole hearts [190, 192]. That is the only way our blind, dry eyes will finally be opened to see the beautiful lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa [61, 62].

# Chapter 9: Like Hot Sugarcane Juice

Our spiritual society, as my supreme preceptor Śrīla Prabhupāda taught us, is not a place of blind dogmas or silent, passive followings [173, 433]. It is a “hearing and questioning society.” [173, 433] An intelligent class of human beings must hear, and from that hearing, they must ask deep questions [173, 433]. In the religions of the world, you go and the priest speaks, and you leave without any questions—no questions are allowed [173, 433]. But our process is built on perfect questions and perfect answers [106, 357]. 

I remember a beautiful, sacred session where many sincere souls gathered and laid their cards on the table, asking their deepest doubts, their struggles, and their pain [112, 359]. Let us enter into those questions directly, without any cosmetics, and let the truth speak.

## **Valuing the Rare Gift of Devotion**

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness is undoubtedly very difficult, why would anyone willingly accept it? [56, 113, 279]

If Kṛṣṇa consciousness were cheap, I would not follow it [57, 279]. I tell you this straight. Why? Because anything which is cheap has no value, and anyone can follow it [57, 279]. 

Look at the material world. If you go to the market, the vegetable shop is crowded, packed with people shouting and pushing [58, 280]. But if you walk down the street to the diamond shop, how many people do you see inside? Very few [58, 280]. Why? Because a diamond is costly [58, 280]. The value, the quality, is of a completely different grade [58, 280]. 

Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the ultimate diamond; it can never be a cheap process [58, 280]. It is difficult because when you achieve perfection in this path, your material existence is terminated for eternity, and you return back home, back to Godhead, never to return to this world of misery again [58, 280]. Because we have been deviant and have misbehaved in millions of ways since time immemorial, we have a massive mountain of dirt to clean [58, 64, 280, 287]. And that purification process is not cheap—it is difficult [58, 59, 280, 281]. 

So when we say Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has made the process “easy,” what do we mean? We mean that if you compare bhakti-yoga to other paths like Ashtanga Yoga, bhakti is a walk in the park [59, 281]. Do you have any idea what Ashtanga Yoga requires? You have to go to the forest, tie your leg to a tree, and hang upside down with your head on the ground for years and years [59, 281]. How many of us can do that? None of us [59, 281]! Compared to that brutal, agonizing physical torture, chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa is wonderfully sweet and easy [59, 281]. 

But make no mistake: to chant the Holy Name with pure, undivided attention is not a simple, mechanical trick [282]. If it were easy, we would not be standing here pretending [282]. We would have tears pouring from our eyes every single morning [62, 282, 285]. 

When pure devotees saw the Deities on the altar, they did not just stand there politely checking their watches [223, 283, 489]. When Śrīnivāsācārya saw Lord Govinda, he fainted on the spot, completely unconscious in ecstatic love [282, 423]. When Sañjaya saw the peacock fan of Kṛṣṇa, he collapsed [282]. 

Think of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī: when Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī gave him a copy of his book *Lalita-mādhava*, Raghunātha dāsa took the book and cried every single day just by holding it and reading its pastimes [79]! When Rāmānanda Rāya went to read the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, he couldn't even read the letters because his eyes were so full of tears of ecstatic love that the water literally wiped out every single syllable on the page [79]! The tears from his eyes dipped the book inside water, and he had to go and look for a different copy [80, 81].

Let us look at what Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī described as his own personal experience of chanting the Holy Name [84]. He said: *“I do not know the potency of these two syllables: Kṛṣ-ṇa. I cannot explain what is within these two syllables. When Kṛṣṇa is chanted, the eye does not see anything. The ear does not hear anything else. The tongue does not chant anything else—there is no distraction. Only Kṛṣṇa.”* [84] He was not pretending; that was his actual, unadulterated experience [84]. He never complained about his mind wandering or being distracted [84]. His mind was asleep to this material world, and awake only to Kṛṣṇa [84].

## **Protecting Our Consciousness From Sinful Desires**

Why do we look at the Deities and feel nothing? Why do we chant and feel dry?

Because our eyes are completely dry [81]. What is drying our eyes? Offenses [81]. Offenses dry up our eyes [81].

We are roommates with Kṛṣṇa, but we are not on speaking terms [79]! Is that normal? You stay in the same room with someone and you don’t talk to them—there is something seriously wrong [79]! We stand before Him and treat Him like a stone or wooden idol [79]. But the Deity is not an idol [79]. If you are dealing with the Deities, you must communicate with Him [79]. Kṛṣṇa is personally there, but because of our offenses, our vision is blocked [79, 81].

This Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting has extraordinary spiritual potency [95]. It is the exact same mantra [83]! Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has told us that Chandra, the moon god, chanted Hare Kṛṣṇa [82]. And because the moon chanted, Lord Śiva’s blue neck—which became blue from drinking the poison of the ocean—became completely white [82]! The japa of the moon god actually removed the poison from Śiva's throat and turned it white [82]. When Śiva went home, his wife Pārvatī was so shocked that she doubted him and said: *“Don’t come near me! You are not my husband! Go!”* [82] She was not pretending; she was completely bewildered because her husband's neck had turned white from the chanting of the Holy Name [82]!

And look at Lord Balarāma’s dhoti [83]. His dhoti is eternally blue [83]. But Nārada Muni’s japa turned Balarāma’s blue dhoti completely white [83]! Even the Yamunā River turned completely milky because of the chanting, and the gopīs churned it and got sweet yogurt from it [83]!

This is the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra we are chanting [83]. It is not a different mantra [83]. Śrīla Prabhupāda is chanting the same Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra, and the whole world is dancing in the kīrtana of the Holy Name [83]. Why are we not having the same experience? Because we are criminals, deviants, and offenders since time immemorial [85]. We have to pay the heavy price of purification before we can experience the same nectar [85]. We must purify ourselves to get to that level [85].

When we are struggling on this path of purification, our minds become disturbed by the unjust, cruel, or negative behavior of others [86]. Sincere devotees sometimes feel that when they face such trials, even performing Kṛṣṇa consciousness feels painful [86].

But I must tell you something very heavy: **A sincere devotee can never feel mental disturbance.** [35] It is not possible [35].

If a sincere devotee feels mental disturbance and is overwhelmed by anxiety, it would mean that Kṛṣṇa is failing in His promise to protect His surrendered servants [35]. But Kṛṣṇa never fails [35]. He always protects His devotees [35].

Why do we get disturbed by the unjust behavior of others? Because we have the material contamination inside us [35]. If I do not have lust in my body, nothing outside can make me lusty [35]. If I do not have anger in my heart, no insult can make me angry [35]. If I do not have envy, the competitive actions of others can never make me envious [35]. The fire inside your heart is what connects with the fire outside [35]. When people insult or mistreat you, they are simply throwing a spark [35]. If your heart is full of dry, material attachments, it will instantly catch fire [35]. But if your heart is saturated with the nectar of bhakti, that spark will immediately be extinguished [35].

Sincere devotees are protected by Kṛṣṇa [87]. Let us look at a beautiful pastime in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata* [87]. One day, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was performing kīrtana, and the great devotee Kula Vāsudeva joined the kīrtana [87]. The blasphemers and envious people stood right in front of him and started criticizing him directly: *“See this, just see this one! Look, he has no cloth on his body, he has no food to eat. The whole night he cried due to hunger, but now he wants to show that he has the love of Godhead. Just look at him!”* [87]

They were not speaking far away from him; they were speaking right before his face [88]. But Kula Vāsudeva was not even there! Why? Why was he not there? Because he was fully Kṛṣṇa conscious, dancing in the kīrtana of the Lord [88]. There was no space for anything else to enter his heart [88]. He was completely empty of material identification, and completely full of Kṛṣṇa [88].

Why do we hear what people talk about us? Why do we get so disturbed, so offended, and so angry when someone criticizes us? **Because we are empty!** [88] We are empty of Kṛṣṇa, and full of ourselves [88]. Because we identify with this temporary body, we respond to insults negatively: *“Why did you say like that about me? Who are you to tell me that?”* [89] But Kula Vāsudeva didn’t say that [89]. The great saint Gaura Kiśora dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja didn’t say that [89]. They were full of Kṛṣṇa, and there was no empty space in their hearts to receive insults [89]. Sādhana is what fills that empty space [228]. When we practice Sādhana nicely, we become strong, and all these material disturbances simply bounce off our chest [35].

## **Overcoming the Illusion of Spiritual Advancement**

When bhakti begins to work, it immediately relieves our suffering [62]. This is the first quality of pure devotional service described by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī: *klesagni*—the distress-relieving quality [62]. When we run to Kṛṣṇa consciousness from the burning fire of material life, bhakti immediately puts that fire out [62]. Suddenly, we feel comfortable, peaceful, and relieved [62].

And that is exactly where the trap of Maya lies! We feel, *“I am okay now. My mind is stable, my life is comfortable.”* And so, we become complacent [62]. We slide into a comfortable, lazy routine where our practices feel monotonous and mechanical [58]. We start coming to the temple only when we feel like it, treating our Sādhana like a casual hobby [62].

In the *Bhagavad-gītā* (16.17), Kṛṣṇa uses a very heavy word for this state: *ātmasambhāvitā*—self-complacent and arrogant [65]. He says that complacency is the quality of a demon, not a devotee! [65] We came to Kṛṣṇa consciousness to become devotees, not self-satisfied demons [65].

Let me tell you a story about my musician friends from my youth [92]. They formed a band called **Cloud Seven** [92]. There were six boys [92]. In the beginning, they had nothing, so they put an incredible, desperate amount of effort into practicing, writing, and refining their music [92]. When their first record came out, it was an absolute hit [92]. They made massive amounts of money and achieved huge success [92].

But then, they started enjoying their success [92]. They became complacent [92]. When it was time to record their second album, they didn’t put the same effort into practicing [92]. They foolishly thought, *“The effort we put into the first record will automatically carry the second one.”* [92]

Do you know what happened? The second album made nothing—it was a total failure! [92]

Success is a stimulant, but success can also bring us down [92]. If we are not careful to continuously expand our devotion, complacency will destroy us [92]. The principle of love is this: if what I offer to Kṛṣṇa today pleases Him, tomorrow I must expand it [92]. Bhakti is a dynamic, moving process [69]. The moment you try to just “maintain” your standard without any desire to grow, you have already begun to slide backward [64]. **Maintenance is Maya!** [64]

Some people think, *“This size of the temple is enough. Ten disciples is enough. Let us just keep things going.”* [66] No! That is maintenance, and maintenance is Maya [64]. We have to expand it [64]. We must always push ourselves further [64].

To defeat this complacency, we must understand our priorities: **Sādhana is our primary, non-negotiable duty.** [23] Services come *after* Sādhana [23].



## **Avoiding Bargains and Business with the Supreme**

Often, when we face difficulties, we try to bargain with Kṛṣṇa [37]. We pray desperately for certain material adjustments—like a job with better hours or relief from family stress—believing that these changes will make our environment more conducive for Sādhana [37]. But when we pray and fast for years, and Kṛṣṇa does not answer, we feel neglected [37]. Why does He ignore our sincere prayers [37]?

There is a stunning statement in the *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta* where Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself reveals His mind [37]:

*“This person is foolishly begging Me for material adjustments and temporary toys. I am not a fool like him. Therefore, I will not give him what he asks. Instead, I will take My time to completely purify him, and then I will give him the ultimate treasure of My lotus feet.”* [37]

Kṛṣṇa is not deaf! He hears everything [37]. But when we are praying, we should check: does our karma allow us to get this now [37]?

Let me give you an example [37]. We have a devotee who has a very high night shift job [37]. He is trying to get a morning job, but for a long time he is not getting it [37]. He has been praying to Kṛṣṇa for a long time, but nothing helps [37]. He feels he can practice better if he has a morning job [37]. But because his karma doesn’t allow him to have what he is begging for, or because it is not right for him, Kṛṣṇa keeps quiet [37].

Let me give you another example [37]. Some time back, a devotee told me—and I know him very well [37]: *“Maharaj, if I get a car... if I get a car every day, every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Every Maṅgala-ārati I will be there. Please pray for me.”* [37]

Kṛṣṇa pleased, he got a car [37]!

For three years, I did not see him [37]. Finally, I met him and said, *“Prabhu, how are you? Do you remember what you said? You said if you get a car, you will come for Maṅgala-ārati every day. Look, is this your own car?”* [37]

*“My car,”* he said [37].

*“Then why are you not coming? For three years I have not been seeing you here with the car.”* [37]

And do you know what he told me next? He said, *“Oh, Maharaj, if I have two cars, then I will give one to my wife, and use one to come to the temple!”* [37]

Look at the cheating, duplicitous mentality! We think we can play smart with Kṛṣṇa [37]!

But you can never cheat Him [37]. Kṛṣṇa is not a “straight” man [37]. He is extremely crooked [37]. In the *Bhagavad-gītā* (10.36), He boldly declares: *“I am the gambling of the cheat.”* He doesn’t hide it [37]! If you try to play smart with Him, He will cheat you back so beautifully, so peacefully, and without any struggle, that you will spend your whole life struggling in confusion while He remains completely untroubled [37]. We cannot artificially do it [37]. Please let us not forget that we are dealing with a person who knows us completely better than we know ourselves [37].

So let us stop trying to bargain with God [37]. The shadow always moves with the object [37]. If you make your Kṛṣṇa consciousness very strong and healthy, your material needs will follow nicely and become easy [37]. But if you are spiritually sick, then everything in your material life will also be a struggle, a guesswork, and a speculation [37].

Sincere, heart-centered service is our only business. Leave the results and the timeline to Kṛṣṇa. Do your part, and let Kṛṣṇa do His part.

## **Developing Sincerity Beyond Transactions**

Some of us have a lot of material career aspirations [123]. To achieve them, we think we must invest a massive amount of time and effort, which inevitably pushes our devotional service into the background [123]. But Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī warns us that *prayāsaḥ ca*—endeavoring desperately for material things that are extremely difficult to obtain—instantly destroys bhakti [125].

Look closely at those who have already achieved the high career positions you are dreaming of [127]. Have you checked how wonderful their lives actually are [127]? When you see them smiling for the camera, do you have any idea of the hollow, screaming vacuum that exists inside their hearts [127]? We think that if we are materially successful, we will finally be happy [129]. That is nothing but a complete lack of faith [129]. Our absolute first priority in life must be Kṛṣṇa consciousness—everything else comes after [129].

This feeling of the goal being "too far" comes from a total lack of understanding [129]. You are thinking that *you* are the one who has to cross this gap using your own tiny material strength [129]. But Kṛṣṇa only asks us to do our part: *janma karma ca me divyam*—simply try to understand with faith how He appeared and what He did [24]. Sages who try to reach perfection on their own strength always end up falling down, because they depend on themselves [180, 181]. But devotees who simply depend on Kṛṣṇa are always protected [180]. Look at Haridāsa Ṭhākura: Maya Devi herself came to tempt him, and she confessed that while the greatest sages fell before her closed eyes, she could not touch Haridāsa because Kṛṣṇa was eternally dancing in his heart [180, 181]. Depend on Kṛṣṇa, and the gap will vanish in a single second.

## **The Character and Qualifications of a True Disciple**

How do we protect ourselves when strong lusty desires arise and overpower our minds? Lust never comes “suddenly.” It is already sitting there, hidden inside the crevices of your heart. When it manifests, it simply means your Sādhana is weak and your mind is idle. The secret to conquering lust is to **never be idle!** Be fully, intensely occupied in planning and executing Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Secondly, busily and lovingly care for other devotees. Lust is nothing but the diseased, selfish mirror-image of pure love. To transform it, you must practice active, selfless care for others. When you are busily engaged in making others happy for Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure, lust will find no empty space to enter and will naturally wither away.

How do we get to know who our spiritual master is? You pray to Kṛṣṇa. Go to your knees, look at the Deity, and tell Him: *“Please, show me who my spiritual master is.”* He will guide you. But you must be sincere. Plan your alignment tonight. Sleep early, wake up early, do not sit with those who gossip and criticize, and sit strictly with those who chant.

When can a disciple take the instruction of the spiritual master as their very life and soul? Only when you are surrendered. Without surrender, you can never take the order as your life and soul. If you are not surrendered, your mind will always rise up and challenge: *“Why should I obey you? Why should I do this difficult service?”* When devotees say, *“Oh, I want to follow, but it is just too difficult,”* they are self-deceived. It is not that the instruction is too difficult; it is simply that they have not surrendered yet. The moment you surrender, the strength to execute the order is immediately supplied.

A genuine disciple has **zero duplicity**. They are completely straightforward and transparent in their dealings with everyone, and most of all, with their spiritual master. They have no double life—their private life and their public life are exactly the same.

And to protect all of this, morning Sādhana is our ultimate key. Chanting early in the morning is the secret of perfect time management. If you finish your sixteen rounds before 6:30 AM, you will have an incredibly long, peaceful, and powerful day. You will have time to read, time to hear, and time to execute your services with a clear, happy mind. But if you do not chant in the morning, you don’t have a day. Your entire day becomes a frantic, stressful struggle in the mode of passion, where you are running late for everything, screaming at your mind, and achieving nothing of real value.

## **Deep Desire for the Lord Attracts Divine Mercy**

Some people say that because bhakti requires intense emotional control and focus, it is very difficult to perform pure devotion in a female body, and that women face too many material obstacles.

All the women who performed austerities in our tradition succeeded without a single exception.

Let us change our minds and stop identifying with these temporary, physical bags of skin. Rise above the bodily platform altogether, tap into that natural, unshakeable determination, and use it to conquer the heart of Kṛṣṇa.

## **Embracing the Purifying Burn of Pure Love**

If attachment is extremely dangerous, and whether material or spiritual, attachment will make you cry, then what is the difference? What is the difference between material attachment and spiritual attachment if both of them cause pain and tears? [22, 253]

Both attachments are indeed painful, and both will make you cry [22, 253]. But there is a massive, infinite difference in the quality of that pain [22, 253].

Material attachment brings a pain that drags you down into continuous, dry, and degrading suffering [22, 253]. It leaves you exhausted, empty, and spiritually dead [78, 311].

But spiritual attachment—the pain of feeling separation from Kṛṣṇa—is **intensely, unimaginably blissful!** [22, 23, 253] It is a pain that makes the soul dance in ecstasy [24, 257, 258]!

Imagine you take raw sugarcane juice [23, 253]. You place it on a fire, and you boil it, and boil it, and boil it [23, 253]. Before it solidifies into dry jaggery, while it is still in a thick, golden, semi-liquid state—how does it taste? It is incredibly, intensely sweet [23, 24, 253]!

If you drink it hot, it will instantly burn your lips [23, 253]. It will burn your tongue and your throat [23, 253]. And the miracle of this spiritual sugarcane juice is that **you will never end up in a hospital!** [24, 253] Your lips will burn, but your soul will be cured of the material disease forever [24, 171, 253].

This is what we call *Prema*—pure, ecstatic love of Godhead [24, 253, 254].

Under the influence of Prema, your entire reality is turned upside down [24, 254]. The wild, dangerous forest becomes your sweet home, and your comfortable home feels like a barren, terrifying forest [24, 254]. The dark night is experienced as the bright midday sun, and the day is felt as night [24, 254]. Ordinary suffering is transformed into the highest, most exquisite happiness [24, 254].

This is the supreme gift of the disciplic succession [24, 254]. This is the golden nectar that Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Lord Balarāma came to distribute freely to all of us [24, 25, 254, 385]. Since we must experience pain and tears in this material world anyway, let us choose the best one [24, 254]. Let us throw away our dry, material attachments, and drink deeply of this boiling-hot sugarcane juice of devotion to Kṛṣṇa [24, 254]!

# Chapter 10: Bypassing the Gatekeeper

When we look at the prayers of Prahlāda Mahārāja in the seventh canto of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (7.9.28), we find a foundational principle of our entire philosophy [180]. 

Prahlāda Mahārāja is a small, five-year-old boy standing before Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva [178, 180]. The Lord is highly pleased with his devotion and tells him, “Ask from Me any benediction you desire” [180]. Prahlāda has the opportunity to ask for anything in the three worlds—unlimited wealth, direct liberation, or material kingdoms [180]. 

But Prahlāda Mahārāja refuses to ask for any material blessings for himself [180]. Instead, he kneels down, offers his respects, and prays to the Lord [180]:

> *evam janaṁ nipatitaṁ prabhavāhi-kūpe*
> *kāmābhikaṁ bahu-yujā-bhara-bhajyamānam*
> *yasya prapāda-rajasā spṛśataḥ pramodaḥ*
> *so ’haṁ kathaṁ na visṛje tava bhṛtya-sevām* [178, 181]

Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:
**“My dear Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, because of my association with material desires, one after another, I was gradually falling into a blind well full of snakes, following the general populace. But Your servant Nārada Muni kindly accepted me as his disciple and instructed me how to achieve this transcendental position. Therefore, my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?”** [180, 181]

This is the ultimate, unyielding standard of discipleship [180]. Prahlāda Mahārāja is teaching us that even when the Supreme Lord Himself stands directly in front of you, offering you personal blessings and direct access, you must never try to leap over or bypass the Spiritual Master [180]. 

Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, in his commentary on this verse, writes a deeply moving instruction representing the heart of the disciple [181]: *“How can I give up the service of my Spiritual Master who has favored me in such a way that I am now able to see You face to face?”* [181]

We must understand this science [181, 182]. The Spiritual Master is the authorized guide, the representative of the Supreme Lord, through whose mercy we learn to approach and please Kṛṣṇa [180, 181]. We cannot bypass him to approach the Lord independently. 

I must tell you straight: **We don't know Kṛṣṇa. We don't understand Kṛṣṇa. Not that we don't understand Kṛṣṇa—we don't understand ourselves!** [181] We don't know who we are, what is our duty, or what is our relationship with Kṛṣṇa [181]. It is the Spiritual Master who gives us the training, the vision, and the qualification to approach the Supreme Lord [181].

## **The Guru as the Soldier of Our Ignorance**

Why is the Spiritual Master completely indispensable to our lives? [181]

Because we are blind [181]. All conditioned souls are born into this material world suffering from a thick, heavy, and sticky “cataract” covering our inner vision [181]. Because of this spiritual cataract, we identify ourselves with these temporary bodies of skin and bones, completely unable to see our actual spiritual identity or perceive the presence of Kṛṣṇa [181, 201].

Can a blind man perform surgery on his own eye? [181]

If you have a cataract, can you take a knife, sit in front of a mirror, and operate on your own eyeball? [181] No, of course not! If you attempt to do that, you will only blind yourself permanently [181]. You must submit yourself to an expert who knows how to operate on the eye and remove the obstruction [181].

The Spiritual Master is like a soldier [181]. He is authorized by the disciplic succession to fight our ignorance and operate on our blind eyes [181]. Through his sharp, heavy instructions, his daily chiding, and his diligent watch over our practice, he removes the cataract from our eyes [181]. It is a painful surgery [181]. Sometimes, when the Guru corrects us, our false ego bleeds, and we feel wounded [181]. But that purification is the only way our vision will be restored so that we can finally see Kṛṣṇa [181, 183].

## **The Science of Approach, Inquiry, and Service**

How do we begin this process of cure? [181] Śrīla Prabhupāda has guided us beautifully on the three things required at the beginning of spiritual life [181]:

1.  **Submissive Approach:** We must go to a bona fide Spiritual Master [181].
2.  **Submissive Inquiry:** We must present our inquiries with a submissive heart [181]. Absurd inquiries and blind following are both condemned [181]. We must ask questions which are beneficial for everyone [181].
3.  **Service:** Sincere, unmotivated service is compulsory [181].

Three things are required; two is not complete [181]. You can make a submissive approach, and you can ask submissive questions—but without *service*, the required purification will never come [181]. 

And that service must be completely free from motivation—100% free from motivation! [181] Then it is a service [181]. Sincere service of a disciple will touch the heart of the sādhu, which is very soft [181]. When pure, selfless service penetrates and touches his heart, a blessing automatically comes [181]. That blessing of the Guru is the secret of success for all of us [181].

Without service, we are simply playing games [181]. 

Today, we are missing this science [182]. I see some devotees trying to keep **one leg inside and one leg outside** the disciplic process [182, 183]. They stay in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, but they go to hear from other unauthorized persons outside of our disciplic line [182, 183]. 

They think: *“Oh, let me hear from here, and let me hear from there. What is the harm?”* [182, 183] 

But those unauthorized persons do not follow the principles, and hearing from them is a sheer waste of time [183]. The moment you start sadhu-hopping and hearing from different places, your mind starts to discriminate [183]. You begin to compare, and from that discrimination, offenses naturally arise [182, 183]. You start finding faults in the devotees and the disciplic succession [182, 183]. 

Eventually, those offenses dry up your taste completely, and both of your legs end up outside the temple altogether! [182, 183]

## **Servant of the Servant of the Servant**

To protect ourselves from this danger, we must strengthen our understanding of being the **“servant of the servant of the servant”** [181, 185]. Serving the Guru’s followers and associates is the true meaning of guru-pūjā [185, 186].

Kṛṣṇa told Uddhava in the eleventh canto, and He told Arjuna in the scriptures, that He is not pleased by direct worship that bypasses His devotees [180, 185]. In the *Ādi Purāṇa*, Kṛṣṇa boldly declares: *“Those who are My direct devotees are actually not My devotees. But those who are the devotees of My devotees are My real devotees.”* [185]

Yet, what do we see today? [185, 186] Many devotees will cook one thousand and eight elaborate preparations for the Spiritual Master, but they will not offer a single thing to his disciples and traveling followers! [185] 

They act as if the Guru is an isolated star, separate from those around him [185]. That is not guru-worship; that is celebrity **hero worship**! [185, 186] 

The Spiritual Master is a father who comes with a family of followers and associates [185]. The way you treat his disciples and the junior devotees around him actually pleases him far more than how you treat him personally [185, 186]. True guru-pūjā means recognizing that the Guru is a package deal [185]. Śrīla Prabhupāda famously quoted the proverb: *“Love me, love my dog.”* [186] The followers are the sādhu’s family, and we must serve them in connection with him [185, 186].

To show us how much He loves this principle, let us look at a beautiful, confidential pastime from the forest of Vraja [185].

One day in Vṛndāvana, Mother Yaśodā wanted to keep Kṛṣṇa home to feed Him all of His favorite preparations [185]. She approached Him and said, *“My dear boy, tomorrow you must not go to the forest with the cows. I have made an exclusive invitation for You. You must stay home and honor this feast.”* [185]

Kṛṣṇa looked at His mother and immediately asked, *“Does this invitation include my cowherd friends? Are Śrīdāmā, Subala, and Madhumaṅgala also invited?”* [185]

Yaśodā said, *“No, my dear. This feast is exclusively for You. I have prepared it only for You.”* [185]

Kṛṣṇa’s face instantly turned dark [185]. He shook His head and flatly refused [185]: **“If my friends are not invited, then I am not coming! Me means my friends! Wherever I am invited, my friends must be included, otherwise I will not accept a single bite!”** [185]

Another time, Mother Yaśodā wanted to make a beautiful pair of shoes for Kṛṣṇa [185]. Kṛṣṇa looked at her and said, *“If you want to make shoes for me, you must also make shoes for all my friends and all the cows.”* [185] 

Yaśodā said, *“No, I cannot do that.”* [185] So Kṛṣṇa refused to wear them and continued to walk bare-footed in the forest because His friends had no shoes [185]. 

This is the sweet, absolute reality of the Lord [185]. Kṛṣṇa never separates Himself from His friends [185]. In the same way, the Spiritual Master never separates himself from his followers and disciples [186]. If you try to worship the Guru while neglecting or criticizing his associates, your worship is artificial, dry, and offensive [180, 185, 186].

## **The Express Route of Sincere Devotion**

Our scriptures declare that service to a sādhu is the **express route** to liberation [183]. 

In the material world, if you drive on a high-speed express highway, there is always a danger of terrible, fatal accidents [183]. But on this spiritual express route, **there are no accidents and no breakdowns!** [183] 

Why? Because you are not the driver of the vehicle! [183] 

On this express highway, the sādhu is the expert driver [183]. He knows the road, he knows how to navigate every turn, and he is driving under the direct guidance of the disciplic succession [183]. For those who are under the able guidance of a bona fide sādhu, there is no fear, no accident, and no vehicle breakdown [183]. As long as the disciple remains submissively in the vehicle, keeping their head down, chanting their rounds, and executing instructions without trying to grab the steering wheel, they are carried safely to Kṛṣṇa [183].

## **The Disciplic Surrender of Rāmānujācārya’s Disciple**

To help us examine our own hearts, let us study a pastime from the life of the great Ācārya, Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya [187].

Once, during his travels across India, Rāmānujācārya was staying near a quiet pond [187]. Every afternoon, after taking his lunch, he would walk to the edge of the water and throw his leftover mahā-prasāda remnants into the pond for the fish [187]. 

When the simple fish in the pond swarmed to swallow his holy remnants, a miracle took place [187]. The very second those pond fish swallowed the remnants of the Ācārya, they instantly cast off their animal fish bodies, assumed beautiful, four-handed spiritual forms, and returned directly to the spiritual world of Vaikuṇṭha [187].

An intimate disciple of Rāmānujācārya, who had been traveling and serving him for many years, witnessed this and felt deeply bewildered [187]. He knelt before his master and asked:

*“Gurudev, I have been traveling with you for years, performing constant services, fasting, and observing Ekādaśī [187]. I have eaten your mahā-prasāda remnants a hundred times—yet my lust, my anger, and my envy are still here [187]! I am still completely bound by my material nature [187]. But these simple, foolish pond fish, who have never performed a single day of service or studied a single line of scripture, have been instantly liberated simply by swallowing your remnants [187]! Why am I still suffering while they are delivered?”* [187]

Rāmānujācārya looked at his disciple with great compassion and asked a single, penetrating question: 

*“My dear disciple, it is true you are traveling with me and serving me every day. But where is your consciousness? When you perform your services, where is your consciousness? Is your mind really with me, or is your consciousness on the food? Are you serving me sincerely from the heart, or are you secretly calculating: ‘When will I eat? When will I get prestige and respect? When will I become the authorized representative of my Guru?’ All these contaminations of pride, calculation, and desire for position are going on inside your heart. That is why the purification is blocked.”* [187]

This is the ultimate mirror for our lives [187]. We can sit in the temple for decades, dress like advanced devotees, and hold prestigious positions in management [187]. But if our hearts are full of calculations, competition, and desire for prestige, we are spiritually more diseased than the fish in the pond [187]. We must strip away all duplicity, follow instructions without calculation, and serve with a clean, open heart [187].

# Chapter 11: The Miracle of Eagerness

When we look at the pastimes of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu recorded in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, we find a magnificent history of how the Lord reciprocates with His servants [138]. But the secret of this history, the absolute spine of it, is **eagerness**—intense, uncalculating eagerness to see the Lord [139, 140].

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu traveled from Jagannātha Purī and arrived at the house of Vidyā Vācaspati [138]. Vidyā Vācaspati was a great pundit, the brother of Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya [138]. The Lord wanted to stay in secret, so He told him, *“I want to stay with you, but do not tell anyone that I am here.”* [138] Vidyā Vācaspati accepted because he wanted to please the Lord [138, 139]. He promised not to tell anyone [139].

But let me ask you: *Who can hide the sun?* [139] Can you hide the sun? How can one hide the Supreme Lord? [139]

Providence will tell everybody [139]. Very soon, everybody got to know [139]. Thousands and millions of people began coming [139]. Vidyā Vācaspati’s house was not a big estate, it was a very humble place [139]. Soon there was no space on the ground—not even a single spot [139]!

And what did the people do in their intense eagerness to see the Lord? [139] They began climbing up into the trees [139]. Millions and billions of people were climbing on those branches [139, 140].

And listen to the miracle: although millions were packed on those branches, not a single person fell down [140]! Why? Because the Lord was there, and their uncalculating eagerness to see Him attracted His personal protection [140]. 

The miracle of their safety was not a material accident [140]. It was the miracle of their eagerness [140]. When there is a genuine, uncalculating eagerness to see the Lord, that intense spiritual hunger attracts His personal protection and reciprocation [140].

## **Alone Is Not Lonely**

Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, however, wanted to be alone [140].

We must understand this: **to be alone is completely different from being lonely.** [140]

In this material world, millions of people are surrounded by friends, relatives, and servants, yet they remain lonely [140]. That loneliness is because we are empty of Kṛṣṇa [59, 140]. But to be alone is highly spiritual [140]. Every devotee who wants to make advancement must find some time to be completely alone with the Supersoul, alone with the Holy Name, and alone with the scriptures [140]. That is when spiritual life is celebrated [140].

But because the crowd was constantly coming and there was no space for Him, Mahāprabhu was not getting the spiritual bliss He desired [141]. They gathered at the door, pleading, *“Please let us see the Lord! Just to see Him, just to see alone!”* [141] And when He came out and they saw Him, nobody wanted to go back to their homes [141]. 

So, seeing that the crowd kept increasing, He decided to leave [141]. In the dead of night, He secretly departed without telling anyone—not even His host, Vidyā Vācaspati [141, 142].

## **The Sweets-Alone Accusation**

The next morning, Vidyā Vācaspati woke up and looked around [142]. The Lord was gone [142]! He was in deep anxiety, looking for the Lord [142].

Meanwhile, the massive crowd gathered at his door, pleading, *“Please, just allow us to see the Lord! I am alone, only one person. Let me just see Him!”* [142]

Vidyā Vācaspati told them, *“I do not know where the Lord has gone.”* [142]

But nobody believed him [143]. They began to shout at him from all directions, accusing him of being narrow-minded, selfish, and deceitful [143]. They said: **“One who buys sweets and eats it alone will go to hell!”** [143]

They accused him of hoarding the Lord’s mercy for himself, shouting: *“Brahmana, please don’t keep us from seeing the Lord. What is your problem? Will our seeing the Lord stop you from getting your own mercy? What will you gain by not allowing us to get the mercy of the Lord? Give us a chance to see the Lord!”* [143, 145]

Vidyā Vācaspati was in total agony, not telling a lie [143]. He didn’t blame anyone, and he did not try to defend himself [144]. The more he tried, the more they blamed him and shouted at him from all directions [144].

This is a heavy lesson. Do you want to be strong in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [144]? You must be ready to face difficulties happily and continue your service without blaming others [144]. Vidyā Vācaspati quietly turned to his only shelter [145]. He fell down and prayed: *“My Lord, please help me. Nobody believes me, and my reputation is destroyed!”* [145, 148]

## **The Miracle at the River**

As he was praying, a Brahmana appeared with the news: *“The Lord has gone to Śāntipura!”* [145, 146]

The crowd was overjoyed [146]. Vidyā Vācaspati came out and happily announced: *“Now I have got the news! The Lord has gone to Śāntipura. All of us will go together and see Him!”* [146]

Millions of people ran to the riverbank [146]. If you have ever been to the Kumbha Melā, you know what happened next [146]. Millions of people wanted to enter one single boat [146]. The boatman was desperately shouting: *“Please, enough! Enough!”* [146, 147] But nobody heard him [147]. The crowd kept entering, and the boat capsized and sank [147].

Millions of people were thrown into the deep, dangerous water—including pregnant women and people who had never learned how to swim a single stroke in their lives [147]. They were desperately grabbing onto whatever they could see, holding onto whatever they could grab [147].

But because of their intense eagerness to see the Lord, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu performed a miracle [147, 148]: **He made the deep river water shallow.** [148] Everyone safely swam and walked across the river without a single person drowning [147, 148]. Eagerness conquered the dangerous current [147].

## **When the Lord Protects His Servant**

Vidyā Vācaspati ran to Śāntipura and fell flat at Mahāprabhu’s lotus feet, weeping [148]: *“My dear Lord, my reputation is destroyed! Everybody is accusing me that I am hiding You [148]. How will I preach? [148] Nobody will believe me [149]. Please, help me. Just come out and let the people see You [149].”*

Mahāprabhu immediately came out and showed Himself to the crowd [149]. In this way, Vidyā Vācaspati was freed from the anxiety of being blasphemed [149]. The Lord always protects His sincere servant [149, 151]. He prayed to the Lord, and he got free [149]. And when the people saw the Lord, they believed that what Vācaspati told them was true [149].

Arjuna’s strength was that God was his friend [151]. No one could defeat him [151]. In the same way, a devotee should never be troubled, depressed, or in anxiety [151]. That is the proof of Kṛṣṇa consciousness: a devotee is always victorious, in all circumstances, because they depend on Kṛṣṇa [151, 184].

We should learn to pray to the Lord [151]. Even the Christians say, *“Why worry when you can pray?”* [151] Sincere prayer is our ultimate shelter [24, 151]. When you pray seriously, you do not need incense, you do not need garlands, you do not need a house, and you do not need an interpreter [152]. Just you and your Lord [152]. That is all [152].

If we can follow the same footsteps of Vidyā Vācaspati, and pray to the Lord with sincere, uncalculating eagerness, everything in our life will be resolved [150].

# Chapter 12: Balarāma’s Path of Strength

If you look at Him carefully, Balarāma is always mentioned as the senior brother of Kṛṣṇa [2]. But is He really the senior brother? No, He is actually the junior brother [2]! Who came first? His junior, Kṛṣṇa [2]. The senior sent the junior to go and check, to supervise, before the senior Himself arrives [2]. Balarāma came after expanding from Kṛṣṇa [2]. They are two bodies, but they are one soul, one blood—except one is white and one is black [2]. 

Everything began from Kṛṣṇa, who divided Himself into two: Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma [3]. And from this original division, Balarāma’s only meditation, His intense, burning desire, is to serve Kṛṣṇa [4, 5].

Lord Balarāma is called the safest form of Kṛṣṇa [4]. In the Western traditions, they speak of the Trinity: God the Father, God the Son, and the Holy Spirit [4]. In our eternal theology, Balarāma represents that supreme, protecting will [4]. And because He is the safest form, His absolute, undivided duty is to serve [4, 5]. 

---

## **His Intense Desire to Serve**

In His intense desire to serve, Lord Balarāma expands Himself into everything we see in both the material and spiritual worlds [4, 5]. 

First, He expands into the *Catur-vyūha*—Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha [5]. Then He expands into Mahā-Viṣṇu, Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, and Ananta Śeṣa [5]. All of these are Balarāma [5]! Every single one of them is manifested strictly for the purpose of service [5, 6]. Only *one* form of Balarāma is used for dancing and playing with Kṛṣṇa [6]. All other forms are used for creation, maintenance, and the physical heavy-lifting of the universes [6]. 

All the sciences we study—algebra, mathematics, physics, or chemistry—are not dry, secular subjects [6]. They are simply the engineering of Lord Balarāma [6, 8]. Even the planetary systems, the innumerable universes, the oceans, the mountains, the rivers—everything is maintained and taken care of by Lord Balarāma [8, 9]. 

Even Kṛṣṇa’s personal, intimate objects are expansions of Balarāma [8, 9]! Kṛṣṇa’s dhoti is Balarāma, His chador is Balarāma, His shoes, His bed, His chair, His flute, and His crown—all are Balarāma’s body, transformed into objects of comfort strictly to serve His brother [8, 9]. In the spiritual world, Kṛṣṇa’s Sandhinī and Saṁvit potencies are placed completely under Balarāma’s charge to maintain the entire structural reality of the spiritual and material realms [9, 10]. All these manifestations exist strictly for service [5].

---

## **Balarāma Holds the Key to Kṛṣṇa**

Because Balarāma maintains everything, He holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa [10, 12]. Balarāma holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa [10, 12].

The less intelligent class of people, without consulting the authorities, speculate and tell lies [11]. If you open an English dictionary, what does it say? It says Kṛṣṇa is just an “avatar of Viṣṇu” [11]. It says *Bhakti* is a mere “sentiment” [11]. They will never have access to the reality until they take shelter of Balarāma [11]. 

I remember visiting a devotee’s house where one of the guests was a highly qualified scientist [12]. He came to me and said, *“Maharaj, we have got to the point where we are all completely confused.”* [12] 

I looked at him and said, *“Yes, Prabhu, and you will remain confused! You will never get out of your confusion until you take shelter of Balarāma [12]. Only when you take shelter of Balarāma will your eyes open, because He holds the key to the locks of your intellect [12].”* Balarāma holds the key to understanding Kṛṣṇa [12].

---

## **Garbhodāna and Saṅkarṣaṇa**

Before we walk into a beautifully constructed temple room, an engineer must first build the structure [13]. The director of the temple did not stand there in the mud laying bricks; the director only walked in *after* the construction was complete [13, 14]. In the exact same way, the universal construction was done under Balarāma’s direction [14]. And only when the scene was fully set did Kṛṣṇa enter [14]. 

But when they were coming, Balarāma had to prepare the way [14]. To teach us how compulsory and sacred the *Garbhādāna-saṁskāra* is for all human beings, Lord Balarāma Himself performed it [3]. If we do not perform this saṁskāra, Śrīla Prabhupāda warns us that the child we produce will be no better than an animal [3]. Balarāma was the very first person in the universe to perform this sacred act of preparing the womb [3]. 

He entered the womb of Devakī first, installing His expansion, Ananta Śeṣa, to make Devakī's womb a comfortable place for Kṛṣṇa [14, 15]. Once Balarāma had made sure everything was complete and secure, Kṛṣṇa ordered Yogamāyā to transfer Balarāma from Devakī’s womb to Rohiṇī’s womb in Gokula [14, 15]. 

When Kṛṣṇa gave this instruction, Yogamāyā was hesitant and worried, feeling that she did not have the power to drag the supreme brother of the Lord [15]. But Kṛṣṇa insisted that this was her assigned service, so Yogamāyā executed the order and transferred Him [15]. 

To the non-devotees and the demons, this transfer appeared as a tragic miscarriage, and Kaṁsa’s guards reported that Devakī's seventh child was dead [15]. But to the devotees, it was the ultimate divine trick to protect them [15]. Because Balarāma was dragged from Devakī’s womb to Rohiṇī’s womb, He was given the name **Saṅkarṣaṇa**—which literally means “He who was dragged” [15, 16].

## **The Dragging of the Yamunā**

Because He was dragged, He sometimes behaves exactly like that [16]! Everything has an effect [16]. 

Later in His pastimes, Lord Balarāma was enjoying on the banks of the Yamunā River, having taken some Vāruṇī liquor [16]. He called out to the Yamunā River, asking her to come closer so He could play in her waters with His friends [16]. 

But the Yamunā River refused to move, thinking that He was acting like a drunkard and was contaminated [16]. 

Balarāma’s eyes flared with anger [16]. He shouted, *“You called me a drunkard? You think I am contaminated? Okay! Let me teach you exactly how a drunkard behaves!”* [16] 

He snatched His mighty plow, dug it deep into her banks, and dragged the Yamunā violently toward Him [16]! The Yamunā River was terrified, fell at His feet, and begged for His forgiveness [16, 17]. Even though Balarāma forgave her, the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* records that she was permanently divided, flowing in crooked curves to this day because she was dragged by Balarāma’s plow [17].

---

## **Akrūra and His Heavy Duty**

For eleven years, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma lived in Vṛndāvana, tending the cows and killing every single demon Kaṁsa sent [20, 24]. Balarāma was always the highly protective elder brother [21, 22]. When Mother Yaśodā, out of intense parental anxiety, wanted to go to the forest with them, Balarāma stepped forward and spoke the truth to protect Kṛṣṇa’s fun, telling her that they only go there to play and that if she came, she would spoil their games [21, 22]. 

But the peaceful days in Vṛndāvana were interrupted [24]. Kaṁsa sent Akrūra to Vṛndāvana to invite Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma to Mathurā for a wrestling match [25, 29]. Akrūra was a pure devotee [28, 29]. He knew Kaṁsa’s wicked plan, but he also knew that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma are the Supreme Lords [30]. 

When Akrūra arrived in Vṛndāvana, he did something shocking: he heavily chastised Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma [31]! He looked at the two brothers and asked: *“Kṛṣṇa! Balarāma! What are you doing? How can you sit here so peacefully taking care of cows and enjoying yourselves in the forest, while your own mother and father are being tortured every day in Kaṁsa’s prison? Every day, Kaṁsa enters their cell, draws his sharp sword, and threatens to cut off their heads! Have you forgotten your duty? The demigods begged you to appear to kill Kaṁsa, and yet you are sitting here quietly!”* [31, 32, 33] 

This brings us to a deep question: was Akrūra actually committing an offense, or was he serving Kṛṣṇa’s arrangement? 

The truth is, Akrūra was executing a heavy duty. He had to say these words to break the sweet, tranquil absorption of Vṛndāvana so that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma would fulfill Their ultimate mission. Hearing this heavy chastisement, They climbed onto Akrūra’s chariot [34].

---

## **The Footprint in the Dust**

When the Gopīs saw the chariot preparing to leave, their hearts broke [34]. They threw themselves on the ground in front of the horses, weeping and screaming [34, 35]. The Gopīs’ love was so intense that Kṛṣṇa’s heart melted [35, 36]. He jumped down from the chariot, and the Gopīs immediately took Him and ran deep into the forest [35]. 

Balarāma, sitting on the chariot, did not even see Kṛṣṇa jump down [35]! When Akrūra asked where Kṛṣṇa had gone, Balarāma climbed down and looked at the dust of Vṛndāvana [35, 36]. 

He saw Kṛṣṇa’s footprint in the dust and began to follow it [36]. He followed it deep into the thick bushes until He found Kṛṣṇa sitting in a clearing, completely surrounded by the weeping Gopīs [36]. 

Balarāma stepped into the clearing, looked at His younger brother, and said: *“My dear brother, please, come. Let us go. Akrūra is in intense anxiety [36]. A devotee must never cause anxiety to another devotee [36, 37]. What is immediate and compulsory right now is our duty to Mathurā. Come.”* [37] 

This is a vital lesson: **a devotee must never cause anxiety to another devotee.** Because Balarāma—their beloved *Dauji Baba*—was the one leading Him, the Gopīs did not say a single word [37]. Out of deep respect for Balarāma’s authority, they let them go [37].

---

## **The Mystery of Vrindavan vs. Mathura**

Akrūra drove the chariot as fast as he could [38]. They reached the border of Vṛndāvana at Akrūra Ghāṭ [38, 39]. 

This border is the ultimate, mysterious boundary of our philosophy [39, 41]. *Vrajendranandana* Kṛṣṇa—the original, sweet cowherd boy of Vṛndāvana—never leaves Vṛndāvana [41, 47]. He did not take a single step outside the boundary [39]. At the very border, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma stepped off and returned secretly into the forests of Braj [39, 42]. Simultaneously, Their formal, royal expansions—Vāsudeva and Saṅkarṣaṇa (the *Yadu Kumāra* expansions)—manifested and went to Mathurā on the chariot [39, 40, 47]. 

When Akrūra went to take his ritual dip in the river, he saw Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma on the chariot, and simultaneously saw Lord Viṣṇu resting majestically on Ananta Śeṣa in the water [40]. We do not need to over-explain this mystery. Kṛṣṇa wants us to enter His mysteries through undivided, submissive devotional service, not through dry intellectual speculation [11].

---

## **Pain That Brings Pleasure**

After Kaṁsa was killed, the royal brothers eventually moved to Dvārakā, leaving Vṛndāvana in the excruciating fire of separation (*vipralambha*) [41, 45]. 

Why did Kṛṣṇa arrange this? Feeling separation is what keeps the love eternally increasing [61]. 

Gurudeva gives us a simple, beautiful explanation of this spiritual separation: **“Spiritual pain brings pleasure because it is not pain. It is actually pleasure.”** In this material world, pain is dry and exhausting. But in the spiritual realm, the tears of separation are sweet and full of ecstatic bliss, making our love grow to infinite heights [61, 62].

---

## **Balarāma Returns Alone to Console Vrindavan**

After some time, Lord Balarāma could no longer tolerate His family’s pain in Vṛndāvana [48]. Having taken Lord Kṛṣṇa’s permission, He mounted His chariot and traveled alone to Nanda Gokula [49]. 

Śukadeva Gosvāmī calls Him **Balabhadra Kuru Śreṣṭha** [49]. Here, Gurudeva explicitly calls Him the supreme spiritual master, *Guru-śreṣṭha*—the best of the Gurus, full of sweetness and strength, who connects us back to Kṛṣṇa [49]. 

But why did Balarāma go alone? Why did Kṛṣṇa stay back? [50] 

The Yadus in Dvārakā knew the secret: Kṛṣṇa is completely subservient to pure love [50]. If Kṛṣṇa went back to Vṛndāvana even for a single second, He would be so conquered by their love that He would never be able to leave [50, 51]. 

Kṛṣṇa Himself revealed His heart to His queen Satyabhāmā: *“Satyabhāmā, I am not going back to Vṛndāvana [57]. It is not because I do not love them [57]. # Chapter 13: Cry Like a Queen

When the mortal body of our spiritual master expires, what should be our response? [206] How should we, who have received his fatherly guidance, navigate the devastating pain of his physical disappearance? [215, 646]

We find our answer in the pages of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (4.28.47), where the great sage Nārada Muni describes the daughter of King Vidarbha—the queen who was left alone in the forest after the departure of her husband, King Purañjana [213, 215]:

> *ātma-ruṣā śocatī dīnam*
> *abandhum ekalā vipine*
> *aśru-mukhī kuca-yugmaṁ*
> *susvaraṁ paruroda sā* [213]

**Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:**  
**“Being now alone and a widow in that forest, the daughter of Vidarbha began to lament, incessantly shedding tears, which shook her breasts, and crying very loudly.”** [213]

Our supreme preceptor, Śrīla Prabhupāda, writes a deeply moving instruction in his purport to this verse:  
**“Figuratively, the queen is supposed to be the disciple of the king. Thus, when the mortal body of the spiritual master expires, his disciples should cry exactly as a queen cries when the king leaves his body.”** [214]

---

## **Tears of Appreciation vs. Tama-guṇa Lamentation**

We must cry [215]. 

If we have genuine, deep feelings for the person who has saved our lives, how can we not weep? [216] You cannot claim to have a heart-connection and not shed tears of grief when he is gone [216]. If you have feelings, you cry because you feel the absence of the person [216]. One who does not cry when the spiritual master physically departs is simply not connected with him [647].

But we must understand exactly *why* we are crying [646]. 

We are not crying because we think the spiritual master has died [646]. No, a pure devotee never dies; he lives eternally in his words and instructions [438, 646]. 

That is how the non-devotees cry [646]. Worldly people weep because they think the person is finished, the love has ended, and everything is destroyed [646]. But for us, there is no such thing as death [218]. The preaching of a bona fide spiritual master does not end with the body; his preaching continues for eternity [218].

We are crying simply because we miss his physical form [646]. We miss his physical association, his sweet, smiling face, and his fatherly guidance [646]. We are crying out of intense, overflowing appreciation for what he has done for us [646, 647]. Sincere tears of appreciation are the natural, beautiful manifestation of our connection [647].

But while the tears of separation are sweet and necessary, they must never be allowed to freeze into morose, paralyzed helplessness [647, 648]. Tears of appreciation are not the same as worldly lamentation [647]. If we are just sat in a corner, crying in morose sadness, thinking that everything is lost and there is no hope left—that is a sign of ignorance [647]! 

Lamentation is in the mode of darkness [647]. Being sad and morose is not *bhakti*; it is *tama-guṇa* [647]. A disciple's crying should be a crying of gratitude, not of defeat [647]. Our tears must cleanse our hearts, not paralyze our hands [648]. 

---

## **The Arjuna and Duryodhana Test of Hearing**

Why do some disciples remain in ignorance while others make rapid progress? It all depends on how we hear [650, 651]. 

Srila Gurudeva gives a beautiful scriptural analogy of the battlefield of Kurukṣetra [650, 651]. Krishna stood in the middle, speaking the sublime philosophy of the *Bhagavad-gītā* [650, 651]. Arjuna was seated on his chariot on one side, and Duryodhana was present on the opposite side [650, 651]. 

Duryodhana did not hear a single word [651]! He was physically there in the middle of the battlefield, but his heart was blocked by envy, pride, and material calculations [77, 651]. Therefore, he failed completely [651]. But Arjuna heard every single word with deep, submissive attention, took the instructions on his head, and successfully executed his duty [651, 100].

Similarly, the spiritual master speaks the truth publicly to everyone [651]. He speaks to the entire crowd [651]. But only very few people actually hear [651]. Those who hear with a sincere, open heart will practice and attain perfection, while those who treat hearing as a mechanical ritual will remain dry, empty, and unchanged [651, 521].

## **Connecting to the Powerhouse**

The spiritual master is the representative of Krishna who carries and distributes His divine potency [435]. But our absolute responsibility as disciples is to establish a genuine, heart-centered connection to his *vāṇī* [435].

Imagine you build a magnificent, multi-story mansion [435]. You execute the wiring flawlessly, install the most expensive light fixtures, and fit beautiful, costly bulbs in every room [435]. But if that house is not physically connected to the powerhouse, what will happen when you flip the switch? Nothing! The house will remain in complete, pitch-black darkness [435, 436]. The light is everywhere in the city, but it is not inside your house because the connection is not made to the powerhouse [436].

One can be in this movement for years, even for lifetimes [436]. You can wear the saffron dress, perform Deity worship, or manage big projects [49, 436]. But if you don't have a genuine, heart-centered connection with the spiritual master, you are on your own, and mental speculation will be your only guide [436]. Sincere, attentive hearing is what establishes the wire to the powerhouse of *vāṇī* [435, 436].

## **The Heart of a Caretaker Sweeper**

In our communities, we often get caught up in prestigious management and false prestige [440]. We want to be the "boss" and show off how important we are [440].

But Srila Gurudeva warns us that this is not real service; it is just our own ego we are demonstrating [440]. 

He says: **“Look at a simple devotee—someone who is quietly sweeping the temple floor, washing the common pots, or cutting vegetables in the kitchen [440]. In their mind, they are constantly meditating: *‘My spiritual master is seeing what I am doing, and he is going to give me blessings. This I am doing is strictly for him.’* [440] That simple devotee is truly connected to the spiritual master [440].”**

Contrast this with a prestigious manager who walks around thinking: *“In this movement, I am the one maintaining the organization. I am the one holding everything together.”* [70 / 437] That manager is completely disconnected, acting on their own false pride [440]. True service is in the heart's submissive intention, not the social prestige of the post [440].

The tears we shed must not be the end of our relationship with the Guru; they must become the beginning of our active responsibility [648]. 

We cry, we appreciate, we remember his instructions, and then **we must move!** [648] 

This is a movement—the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement [644]! And **movement means we have to move. If we don’t move, Maya will move us!** [644] You cannot stay in one place [644]. If you sit still in your grief, Maya will quickly drag you down into complacency and dry laziness [644, 201]. 

**And remember: Maintenance is Maya!** [644, 653] 

To simply keep the temple clean, maintain the existing ten disciples, and keep things static and “safe” is a failure [653]! Just trying to keep things going as they are is Maya [653]! 

The physical disappearance of the Guru is the ultimate, urgent call to action [644]. It is the time for us to take risks, to train future leaders, and to expand the mission to every town and village [648].

# Chapter 14: When the Teacher Leaves the Classroom

When the physical form of the spiritual master is no longer present on this earth, how should a disciple feel? [199, 206] How should we, who have received his fatherly guidance, navigate the devastating pain of his physical disappearance? [208, 216]

To find our answer, we must first understand the fundamental difference between the physical presence of the Guru (*vapu*) and his eternal instructions (*vāṇī*) [214]. While the spiritual master is physically present, we should render loving service to his physical form [214]. This is sweet and necessary [214]. But his physical body does not live eternally [428]. His word lives eternally [428]. Therefore, while the spiritual master is physically present on this earth, the disciple must try to establish a deep, unshakeable connection with his *vāṇī* [428]. 

Make haste while the iron is hot! Establish that spiritual connection now, so that when the spiritual master is physically not present, you will continue uninterrupted [566].

---

## **The Metaphor of the Empty Classroom**

To understand our behavior after the Guru's departure, let us look at a simple, practical metaphor [572, 666]. 

When the teacher is physically present inside the school classroom, everyone is a good student [572, 666]. The children sit quietly, look at their books, pay attention, and pretend to study [572, 666]. It is very easy to look qualified when the authority is policing you [666]. 

But if you want to know the actual character of the students, you must observe what they do when the teacher leaves the classroom [572, 666]! The moment the teacher walks out the door, the behavior of the students shows who they really are [572, 666]. One student immediately starts jumping on the tables, another starts throwing papers, and the entire classroom becomes chaotic and wild [572]. Only the rare, genuine students keep their heads down, ignore the distraction, and continue their assignments exactly as instructed [572].

The physical disappearance of the Guru is the time when the teacher leaves the classroom [572, 666]. It is the ultimate test of what we have actually learned [572, 666]. How are we going to perform our Kṛṣṇa consciousness when his physical eye is no longer watching us [572, 666]? Are we going to close down the temple, or slide back into our lazy, old material habits [572, 648]? Our job, our hearing, our daily Sādhana—everything will be tested [667]. If we are not practicing properly now in the classroom of life, we will have nothing to write on our paper on examination day [667].

---

## **The Myth of Being an Orphan**

The moment the spiritual master is physically gone, some devotees fall into the trap of the "orphan mentality" [573]. They sit back, fold our arms, and cry: *“Oh, my Guru is gone, what can I do now? I am completely helpless. I am a poor, lonely orphan.”* [573]

You made yourself an orphan! Your Guru did not make you an orphan [573]!

The Guru did not make us orphans [574]. An orphan has no family, but in our spiritual society, do you not have the uncles—the senior godbrothers and godsisters of your Guru [574]? Do you not have the temple community [574]? Why do you isolate yourself and play the victim [574]?

The only reason we feel this dry, pathetic helplessness is that we are not following his orders strictly [574]. The moment you take the order of the spiritual master on your head and execute it, the feeling of orphanage vanishes [573, 574]. You instantly feel his living presence guiding you, and you realize you are surrounded by the loving protection of the entire disciplic family [574].

---

## **Śrīla Prabhupāda's Standard of Discipleship**

Look at the example of Śrīla Prabhupāda [568]. He did not live in an ashram with his Guru; he was a householder, running a business and managing his family responsibilities [569]. He and his spiritual master, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, met only occasionally [428]. Physically, they did not spend decades together [571]. Spiritually, their connection was unshakeable because they knew the assignment [428]. 

Before Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī left this planet, he wrote a letter to Prabhupāda containing his very first instruction: *“Preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness in the English-speaking countries.”* [568, 67] Prabhupāda never forgot it [67]. Even when he was working and his spiritual master had physically disappeared, he spent every single day preparing himself to execute that duty [67]. He didn't complain, *“Oh, my Guru is gone, and I am just a poor householder.”* He carried that order on his head, executed it, and always gave all the credit to his spiritual master for the global miracle of ISKCON [574, 354]. That is a bona fide disciple [67].

## **The Final Phone Call and Blessing**

I remember a deeply personal and sacred pastime of how this spiritual connection became a reality in my own life [566].

Three months before my beloved spiritual master—my Srila Gurudeva, His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami—left his physical body and departed from this planet, he was in the United States, and I was serving in India [567].

I called him on the phone, feeling the heavy, impending pain of his disappearance [567]. I said, *“Gurudeva, I am preparing to travel back to Africa, but I want to come to America to see you before I go. I must see you.”* [567]

But my Gurudeva looked at me and said: **“Don’t come. Stay there. The training I have given to you is such that my physical absence will not have any effect on you. And wherever you go in this world, people will see me in you.”** [567]

At that time, I did not fully understand the depth of his words [567]. But many years later, I was visiting a devotee’s house [358]. As we were sitting on the rug, the householder looked at me with tears in his eyes and said, *“Maharaj, simply by seeing you and hearing you speak, I am feeling the physical presence of your spiritual master, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, in my house.”* [358]

When he said those words, I was completely overwhelmed [358]. I had never told this devotee what my Gurudeva had promised me over the phone [358]. But his blessing was 100% true [358]. # Chapter 16: A Caring Father

I was very new in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and my beloved spiritual master, His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, had taught us a very simple and powerful practice: how to write a personal letter to Lord Kṛṣṇa [10, 157]. # Chapter 17: The Kāliya Inside Us

Why are we not making rapid progress in our spiritual life? We hear these beautiful pastimes of Kṛṣṇa year after year, yet our characters remain unchanged, our hearts remain hard, and we continue to carry critical, offensive, and envious thoughts in our minds [181, 184]. 

Let us look at a dynamic pastime: the subduing of the venomous serpent, Kāliya [163, 169]. 

Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura reveals a profound truth: these pastimes are not merely historical dramas or sweet myths [168, 169]. They represent our own internal struggles [169]. The dark, boiling poison of Kāliya represents our own deep-seated *anarthas*—treachery, crookedness, brutality, pride, envy, and maliciousness [169, 172]. 

And until we allow Kṛṣṇa to step into our hearts and dance on our pride, we will remain completely polluted [169, 177].

## **Chastisement is Very Purifying**

We must understand that chastisement is very purifying [178]. It is the mercy of the Guru that he chastises [178]. If the Guru does not chide us or correct us, how will we ever get purified [178]? It is not possible [178].

To understand how a soul receives the mercy of having Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet placed upon their head, we must look at Kāliya’s previous life [163, 168]. 

There was once a powerful king who was afflicted with an unbearable stomach disease [164]. He tried every medical treatment, spent fortunes, but nothing could cure his suffering [164, 165]. 

One day, an astrologer told him that there was a specific, rare bird in the deep forest, and if he could catch it and cook its flesh, he would be cured [164]. 

The king went to the forest every day searching for this bird [164]. Finally, he spotted it [165]. He drew his bow, but the bird was swift and escaped [165]. Day after day, the king chased the bird, but it slipped through his fingers [165]. 

After several attempts, the bird stopped on a branch and spoke in a human voice [165]. It told the king that eating its flesh would not cure him [165]. The bird said that deep in the forest, there lived an exalted, self-realized sage [165]. If the king could find this holy sādhu, wash his feet, and drink the sacred wash-water, the *caraṇāmṛta*, his disease would vanish forever [165].

The king immediately threw down his weapons and began searching for the sage [165]. After wandering through the thick forest, he finally found him sitting in silent meditation [165]. 

The king fell to his knees and begged the sage to allow him to wash his feet and drink the water to cure his suffering [165, 166]. 

The quiet sage nodded and slowly extended his right foot [165]. 

The king looked at the foot, but he hesitated [166]. The right foot was covered in sores and lesions [166]. Fearing the disease, the king asked the sage if he could wash his left foot instead [166]. 

The sage slowly pulled back his right foot and extended his left one [166]. 

But when the king looked at the left foot, it appeared ten times worse [166]. It was completely rotten, decaying, stinking, and covered in oozing pus with white worms dancing in the open wounds [166]. 

The king was filled with horror [166, 167]. His disgust overpowered him [167]. He washed the foot, but he could not bring himself to drink the water [167]. The odour was too intense, and his throat refused to swallow [167]. 

So, to show some respect while avoiding the taste, the king took the wash-water and rubbed it all over his head [167]. 

We must understand the secret of this pastime: the sage’s feet were not actually rotten or stinking [167]. To any pure, qualified soul, they were sweet and transcendental [167]. But because of the king’s heavily diseased, offensive material condition, his vision was blocked, and the feet appeared stinking and rotten to him [167]. 

Because the king lacked the purity to drink the water, he did not return back to Godhead in that lifetime [168]. But because he had sincerely rubbed that sacred water all over his head, Kṛṣṇa noted his gesture [167, 168]. 

In his next birth, that king was born as the massive, venomous serpent Kāliya [164, 168]. And because he had rubbed the wash-water on his head, Kṛṣṇa personally came to dance on his hoods, decorating his head with the dust of His divine lotus feet [168]!

## **The Proprietor Mentality**

In his serpent body, Kāliya lived in a deep lake in the Yamunā River [169]. He was so venomous that his poison polluted the entire atmosphere [169, 170]. The water of the Yamunā was boiling with heat and poison, the trees on the banks were dead, and even the birds flying in the sky would fall dead into the water [169, 170]. 

The Yamunā had descended from the spiritual world to serve Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes, but Kāliya had occupied her and polluted her water [170]. 

Kṛṣṇa decided to personally chastise this intruder and clean the Yamunā for His devotees [170, 180]. He climbed a high tree and jumped straight into the poisoned lake, swimming around joyfully and splashing the water [171]. 

When Kāliya heard the splashing, his hoods flared with rage [171]. He thought: “I am the proprietor here! This is my place. You cannot do anything here without my permission.” [171]

Do we also think like that [171]? We get a little position, some authority in a temple, or some management power, and we immediately think: “I am the boss here! You cannot preach here, you cannot serve here, you cannot do anything without my permission!” [171]

That is not Kṛṣṇa consciousness; that is the diseased, toxic mentality of Kāliya [172]. 

Kāliya swam to the surface and saw Kṛṣṇa [172]. Kṛṣṇa was exquisitely beautiful, but because Kāliya’s heart was full of envy and pride, he could not appreciate His beauty [172]. He saw only an enemy to be destroyed [174]. 

Why do we not appreciate the beautiful Deities on the altar [172, 173]? We see them, but we don't faint [172]. We don't cry [172]. Why is that? Because we have the same Kāliya inside us [172, 173]. We must know what we have to work on in our lives [173]. If we do not know what to work on, how will we become better [173]?

## **The Laughter of Dauji Baba**

Kāliya immediately attacked, coiling his massive body around Kṛṣṇa's delicate waist and squeezing Him tighter and tighter [175].

Why was Dauji Baba laughing [175, 176]? 

Because He knows His younger brother’s power [176]. He saw how Kṛṣṇa was showing Kāliya great mercy [176]. He laughed and thought that Kṛṣṇa had never allowed Him, Ananta Śeṣa, to coil Him or hold Him in this way, yet He was showing this ordinary snake such mercy [175, 176]!

## **The Dancing Chastisement of Lord Kṛṣṇa**

Hearing His brother’s call, Kṛṣṇa instantly expanded His body, freeing Himself from the serpent’s grip [176]. 

Kāliya raised his hundred hoods, spitting fire and poison, trying to bite Kṛṣṇa [176, 177]. But Kṛṣṇa was too fast [176, 177]. He began to leap from hood to hood, kicking them down with His lotus feet [176, 177]. Kṛṣṇa even invited His gopī friends to come over and watch Him dance on the hoods of the serpent [160]! He performed a magnificent, ecstatic dance right on the heads of the serpent [160, 177]! 

Whenever Kāliya tried to raise one of his hundred heads in pride, Kṛṣṇa would leap into the air and kick it down with a heavy, crushing blow [177]. 

Kṛṣṇa's kicking helped Kāliya get the poison out [177]. When Kṛṣṇa kicked him, the boiling poison was squeezed out of his hoods, and he began to vomit blood and toxic waste [177]. 

This is the exact nature of bhakti [177]. Bhakti is exhausting, tiring, and purifying [177, 178]. When we begin to practice seriously, the spiritual master and Kṛṣṇa will begin to kick our pride, our lust, and our envy [177]. It is an exhausting process, but that chiding and kicking is highly purifying [178]. It squeezes the poison out of our hearts [177, 178].

Finally, all the poison was gone, and Kāliya was completely exhausted [177, 178]. His wives, the Nāgapatnīs, emerged from the water and offered beautiful, submissive prayers [178, 179]. They asked Kṛṣṇa to spare his life [178]. They said: “Our husband is naturally a low-class, wicked snake. It is his animal nature to bite and spit poison. But You have purified him [178, 179]. Please spare his life [178].” 

Kṛṣṇa was pleased and stopped the chastisement [179].

# Chapter 19: Stop Cooking, Start Listening

When we gather for *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, it is not an ordinary, dry academic seminar [157]. *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the direct sound representation of Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself [157, 436]. ## **The Glories of gṛhastha-Asrama**

In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, we find a magnificent dialogue between the supreme emperor, Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj, and the great celestial sage, Nārada Muni [159, 438, 439]. 

Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj was an eternally liberated, pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa [159, 438]. Every single action he performed was fully transcendental [159, 438]. Yet, as a householder (*gṛhastha*), he submissively knelt before Nārada Muni and asked a question that is deeply relevant to every single one of us [160, 439]:

> *“My dear Nārada Muni, the brahmacharis, sannyāsīs, and forest yogis are free from material entanglements and are constantly serving Lord Kṛṣṇa. What about us who stay in the house, surrounded by family, wealth, and material responsibilities? How can we make the same advancement? How can we escape these clutches?”* [160, 439]

Listen carefully to the answer Nārada Muni gives [440]. He looks at Yudhiṣṭhira and points out a beautiful, shocking paradox: 

The great forest sages, the yogis, and the philosophers perform brutal, agonizing austerities for thousands of years in the wilderness just to catch a tiny, fleeting glimpse of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet [59, 440]. They freeze in the winter, burn in the summer, and fast for months [59]. 

But do you know what Kṛṣṇa did? He left all those forest sages behind and came to live in a house with Yudhiṣṭhira Maharaj [440, 441]! He moved into Yudhiṣṭhira’s palace and stayed there exactly as He lives eternally in Goloka Vṛndāvana or Dwaraka—as an intimate, loving family member [441]! 

Why? Because Yudhiṣṭhira’s pure love and unalloyed devotion were vastly superior to the dry, mechanical austerities of the forest-dwellers [441]. 

Household life is not a spiritual death sentence; it is a glorious, powerful opportunity to conquer the Supreme Lord Himself [160, 441]. You do not have to run away to the forest to find Kṛṣṇa [160, 441]. You can turn your own home into a temple, provided you follow the formula of *sādhana* and constantly associate with saintly persons [162, 229, 442].

> **“Please, bless my son that he may become a pure servant of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī.”** [162, 442]

Because of that householder’s pure, unselfish prayer, his son went on to shake the entire world and save millions of souls [77, 99]. That is the real duty of a *gṛhastha* [162, 442]. Your primary business is to use your home to associate with saintly people, hear the nectar of Bhāgavatam from them, and pray only for pure devotion [162, 163, 442].

## **Stop Cooking, Start Listening!**

But let me tell you what actually happens in our modern houses [165, 440]. Today, we have introduced a major, laughable disease into our society [165, 254]:

# Chapter 20: Giving Beyond the Modes

If you want to know how to give charity, you must study the instructions of the *Bhagavad-gītā* [3]. Kṛṣṇa explains that everything in this material world is tinged by the three modes of nature, and our giving is no exception [3].

## **The Three Modes of Charity**

Let us try to understand these three modes of charity carefully [3].

*   **Charity in Ignorance:** This is charity given to an unqualified person, in an impure place, and at an improper time [3]. According to the Vedic literature, there is no recommendation for giving charity indiscriminately [3]. You cannot just throw your money anywhere and call it charity [3]. The suitable time to give charity is during a solar or lunar eclipse, at a holy place of pilgrimage, or at the end of the month to a qualified Brāhmaṇa or Vaiṣṇava [3]. The Vedas strictly condemn giving charity in the night—it is considered condemned charity because the result is not wonderful [3]. Giving money to support sinful activities like intoxication and gambling, or giving in an impure place like a cemetery, a hotel, or a bar, is in the mode of darkness and brings only suffering [3]. Similarly, if a person gives charity to a suitable person but without respect and without attention, that sort of charity is also said to be in the mode of ignorance [47].
*   **Charity in Passion:** This is charity performed with the expectation of some return, with a desire for fruitive results, or in a grudging mood where you later complain and repent: *“Why have I spent so much money in this way?”* [3, 61] Sometimes it is performed for elevation to the heavenly kingdom, or simply out of obligation at the request of a superior [3, 61, 62].
*   **Charity in Goodness:** This is charity given as a duty, without expectation of return, at the proper time and place, and to a worthy, qualified person engaged in spiritual activities [3, 75]. Indiscriminate charity is not recommended in the Vedic literature; spiritual perfection is always a consideration [76]. Therefore, charity is recommended to be given at a place of pilgrimage, on solar or lunar eclipses, or at the end of the month to a qualified Brāhmaṇa or a pure devotee [76, 77].

But beyond goodness, there is **transcendental charity.** [77] This is charity given directly to a pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa to facilitate and expand his mission [77]. When you beg a pure Vaiṣṇava with a humble mind to accept your offering for your own benefit, the result is 100% pure and takes you straight back to Goloka Vṛndāvana [55, 78]. You give this charity with begging: *“Please, kindly accept this for my benefit.”* [78] Because if a pure devotee refuses to take your charity, you cannot get purified [78]. With a humble mind, we must give charity to a pure devotee with begging and consider it our greatest fortune [78].

## **The Madman’s Cigarette**

To understand how Kṛṣṇa looks at our intentions rather than our external actions, let me share a practical history that happened to me personally [57, 58].

One day, I was passing in the street, and a madman was there [57]. Madman [57]. He smiled, and he was smoking [57]. When he saw me, he said, *“Oh my friend, can you take some cigarettes also?”* He offered me cigarettes [57].

Would he benefit? He is offering cigarettes to a devotee [58]. Will he benefit or not?

Will he benefit? Yes, he will benefit [58]! The action is in which mode? Ignorance [58]. But what is the intention? The intention is good [58]! Just the desire to give is suspicious [meaning: wonderful and worthy of notice] [59]! So he will benefit [59]. Even though it is a forbidden item, the desire to give something is very suspicious [59]. Kṛṣṇa will respond to our intention, not just our activities [86]. He sees what we are keeping behind; He doesn't see what we are giving Him [86].

## **The Stingy Lady and the Dry Palace**

One day, a sadhu came by to beg from her [59]. She didn't want to give him anything, so she took a lump of dirt from the ground and put it on the begging bowl of the sadhu [59]. The sadhu left [59].

But look at the law of karma: because she had the desire to give *something*, in her next life she was promoted [59, 60]. She was born with a grand kingdom and a massive, beautiful palace [60]. The desire to give something is important, yes [60]. But we must understand what is charity and how to give charity properly [60].

## **ABOUT THE ACHARYAS & TEACHERS**

### **His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda**
His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda (revered eternally as the Founder-Ācārya of the International Society for Kṛṣṇa Consciousness) is the supreme spiritual father who delivered the ultimate gold of Gaudiya Vaishnavism to the modern world [14, 16]. At the advanced age of sixty-nine, carrying only forty rupees and a suitcase of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* translations, he boarded the cargo ship *Jaladuta* to cross the Atlantic and plant the seeds of bhakti on Western soil [14, 16]. His life was a manifestation of uncompromised compassion, translating and commenting on dozens of volumes of Vedic scriptures and establishing more than a hundred temples globally to provide shelter for all humanity [14].

### **His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami**
His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami (beloved spiritual master, pioneer preacher, and spiritual father) was an uncompromised spiritual warrior who served the mission of Śrīla Prabhupāda with absolute, fearless dedication [2, 11]. Born in Cleveland, Ohio, as John Favors, he graduated from Princeton University before dedicating his life to the disciplic succession [2, 11]. He took incredible, life-threatening risks to distribute books behind the Iron Curtain in the Soviet Union and war-torn Yugoslavia, and established flourishing devotee-centered communities, schools, clinics, and farms across West Africa [11, 12, 19]. His life was a testimony to selfless care, counseling thousands and devoting his last breath to protecting the bodies and souls of the Vaiṣṇavas [11].

### **His Holiness Bhakti Dhīra Dāmodara Swami**
His Holiness Bhakti Dhīra Dāmodara Swami is a sannyasi and initiating spiritual master within ISKCON, who travels tirelessly to preach the message of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* across India, Africa, and the world [1, 2]. As a disciple of HH Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, he represents the uncompromised standard of loving care, bold preaching, and intense sādhana that he received from his spiritual master [2, 11].
