# Chapter 13: Cry Like a Queen

When the mortal body of our spiritual master expires, what should be our response? [206] How should we, who have received his fatherly guidance, navigate the devastating pain of his physical disappearance? [215, 646]

We find our answer in the pages of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (4.28.47), where the great sage Nārada Muni describes the daughter of King Vidarbha—the queen who was left alone in the forest after the departure of her husband, King Purañjana [213, 215]:

> *ātma-ruṣā śocatī dīnam*
> *abandhum ekalā vipine*
> *aśru-mukhī kuca-yugmaṁ*
> *susvaraṁ paruroda sā* [213]

**Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:**  
**“Being now alone and a widow in that forest, the daughter of Vidarbha began to lament, incessantly shedding tears, which shook her breasts, and crying very loudly.”** [213]

Our supreme preceptor, Śrīla Prabhupāda, writes a deeply moving instruction in his purport to this verse:  
**“Figuratively, the queen is supposed to be the disciple of the king. Thus, when the mortal body of the spiritual master expires, his disciples should cry exactly as a queen cries when the king leaves his body.”** [214]

---

## **Tears of Appreciation vs. Tama-guṇa Lamentation**

We must cry [215]. 

If we have genuine, deep feelings for the person who has saved our lives, how can we not weep? [216] You cannot claim to have a heart-connection and not shed tears of grief when he is gone [216]. If you have feelings, you cry because you feel the absence of the person [216]. One who does not cry when the spiritual master physically departs is simply not connected with him [647].

But we must understand exactly *why* we are crying [646]. 

We are not crying because we think the spiritual master has died [646]. No, a pure devotee never dies; he lives eternally in his words and instructions [438, 646]. 

That is how the non-devotees cry [646]. Worldly people weep because they think the person is finished, the love has ended, and everything is destroyed [646]. But for us, there is no such thing as death [218]. The preaching of a bona fide spiritual master does not end with the body; his preaching continues for eternity [218].

We are crying simply because we miss his physical form [646]. We miss his physical association, his sweet, smiling face, and his fatherly guidance [646]. We are crying out of intense, overflowing appreciation for what he has done for us [646, 647]. Sincere tears of appreciation are the natural, beautiful manifestation of our connection [647].

But while the tears of separation are sweet and necessary, they must never be allowed to freeze into morose, paralyzed helplessness [647, 648]. Tears of appreciation are not the same as worldly lamentation [647]. If we are just sat in a corner, crying in morose sadness, thinking that everything is lost and there is no hope left—that is a sign of ignorance [647]! 

Lamentation is in the mode of darkness [647]. Being sad and morose is not *bhakti*; it is *tama-guṇa* [647]. A disciple's crying should be a crying of gratitude, not of defeat [647]. Our tears must cleanse our hearts, not paralyze our hands [648].

## **The Arjuna and Duryodhana Test of Hearing**

Why do some disciples remain in ignorance while others make rapid progress? It all depends on how we hear [650, 651]. 

Srila Gurudeva gives a beautiful scriptural analogy of the battlefield of Kurukṣetra [650, 651]. Krishna stood in the middle, speaking the sublime philosophy of the *Bhagavad-gītā* [650, 651]. Arjuna was seated on his chariot on one side, and Duryodhana was present on the opposite side [650, 651]. 

Duryodhana did not hear a single word [651]! He was physically there in the middle of the battlefield, but his heart was blocked by envy, pride, and material calculations [77, 651]. Therefore, he failed completely [651]. But Arjuna heard every single word with deep, submissive attention, took the instructions on his head, and successfully executed his duty [651, 100].

Similarly, the spiritual master speaks the truth publicly to everyone [651]. He speaks to the entire crowd [651]. But only very few people actually hear [651]. Those who hear with a sincere, open heart will practice and attain perfection, while those who treat hearing as a mechanical ritual will remain dry, empty, and unchanged [651, 521].

## **Connecting to the Powerhouse**

The spiritual master is the representative of Krishna who carries and distributes His divine potency [435]. But our absolute responsibility as disciples is to establish a genuine, heart-centered connection to his *vāṇī* [435].

Imagine you build a magnificent, multi-story mansion [435]. You execute the wiring flawlessly, install the most expensive light fixtures, and fit beautiful, costly bulbs in every room [435]. But if that house is not physically connected to the powerhouse, what will happen when you flip the switch? Nothing! The house will remain in complete, pitch-black darkness [435, 436]. The light is everywhere in the city, but it is not inside your house because the connection is not made to the powerhouse [436].

One can be in this movement for years, even for lifetimes [436]. You can wear the saffron dress, perform Deity worship, or manage big projects [49, 436]. But if you don't have a genuine, heart-centered connection with the spiritual master, you are on your own, and mental speculation will be your only guide [436]. Sincere, attentive hearing is what establishes the wire to the powerhouse of *vāṇī* [435, 436].

## **The Heart of a Caretaker Sweeper**

In our communities, we often get caught up in prestigious management and false prestige [440]. We want to be the "boss" and show off how important we are [440].

But Srila Gurudeva warns us that this is not real service; it is just our own ego we are demonstrating [440]. 

He says: **“Look at a simple devotee—someone who is quietly sweeping the temple floor, washing the common pots, or cutting vegetables in the kitchen [440]. In their mind, they are constantly meditating: *‘My spiritual master is seeing what I am doing, and he is going to give me blessings. This I am doing is strictly for him.’* [440] That simple devotee is truly connected to the spiritual master [440].”**

Contrast this with a prestigious manager who walks around thinking: *“In this movement, I am the one maintaining the organization. I am the one holding everything together.”* [70 / 437] That manager is completely disconnected, acting on their own false pride [440]. True service is in the heart's submissive intention, not the social prestige of the post [440].

## **“Maintenance is Maya” and the Call to Expand**

The tears we shed must not be the end of our relationship with the Guru; they must become the beginning of our active responsibility [648]. 

We cry, we appreciate, we remember his instructions, and then **we must move!** [648] 

This is a movement—the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement [644]! And **movement means we have to move. If we don’t move, Maya will move us!** [644] You cannot stay in one place [644]. If you sit still in your grief, Maya will quickly drag you down into complacency and dry laziness [644, 201]. 

**And remember: Maintenance is Maya!** [644, 653] 

To simply keep the temple clean, maintain the existing ten disciples, and keep things static and “safe” is a failure [653]! Just trying to keep things going as they are is Maya [653]! 

The physical disappearance of the Guru is the ultimate, urgent call to action [644]. It is the time for us to take risks, to train future leaders, and to expand the mission to every town and village [648]. 

Let us rise up [648]. Let us dry our tears of lamentation and replace them with the active, blazing fire of pure, unmotivated service [648]. Take the order of the spiritual master on your head, run to the battlefield of preaching, and carry his presence to the ends of the earth [567, 574, 648].
