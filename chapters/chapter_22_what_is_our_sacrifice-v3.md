# Chapter 22: What is Our Sacrifice?

We must think deeply about our future [193]. 

I remember in 2004, I was staying at the Radha Gopinath Temple in Chowpatty, Mumbai [190]. His Holiness Radhanath Swami observed something very alarming: the children of the congregation were spending far more time in the wrong, materialistic association than in good, spiritual association [190]. 

Maharaj asked the parents directly: **“Do you want to leave your children like this?”** [190] 

They said, *“No.”* [190] 

So, with just four students, they started a small school called Gopal Garden [190, 191]. In the beginning, there was no building, no big facilities, nothing [191]. But by the time I visited, that humble initiative had grown into a massive, beautiful building where children were being trained exquisitely in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [191, 201]. 

I had a Christian background before I came to Kṛṣṇa consciousness [191]. The Christians understand the power of education; they have their own schools and universities in Nigeria and all over the world [191]. And you cannot go to a Muslim school and teach them about the *Gītā* there; if you go there, you have to learn the Koran [186]. 

But why are “Kṛṣṇa’s children” not becoming devotees [193]? 

Have we checked why? It is because we do not have our own educational system [194]. We have simply not prioritized the spiritual formation of the next generation [194]. 

Śrīla Prabhupāda boldly declared that modern secular schools are nothing but **slaughterhouses** of consciousness [192]. Just as innocent animals are sent to slaughterhouses to have their bodies killed, we send our innocent children to these materialistic schools to have their spiritual consciousness systematically slaughtered [192]. 

If we want to save our children, we must make particular, serious arrangements for their spiritual education [192]. Sending them to the temple program for two hours on Sunday while leaving them for six hours a day in a secular school is a complete waste of labor [192]. It is exactly like trying to **use a basket to fetch water** [192]! 

If you try to carry water in a wicker basket, what will happen [192]? The water will leak out completely [192]! Only a mystic like Durvasa Muni can use a wicker basket to carry water without it leaking [192]. But let me ask you: how many Durvasa Munis are sitting in our temple rooms today [192]? None of us [192]! So why are you trying to carry the spiritual consciousness of your children in the wicker basket of secular education [192]? Settle this in your mind: we must take responsibility for our children's spiritual formation and build our own schools [192, 195].

---

## **Overcoming Selfishness and Prioritizing the Future**

Let me say something very heavy and very direct: **devotees are extremely, deeply selfish.** [191, 201] 

We make grand, lavish plans for weddings [191]. We design highly elaborate, static, and luxurious wedding ceremonies that last for just one day of prestige [191]. 

But what is our plan for the children that come after that marriage [191]? What is our plan for their spiritual future [191]? Zero [191, 192]! We have no plan [191, 192]. Is that selflessness or is that deep selfishness [191, 195]? 

We are constructing big temples, but where are the schools [176]? If you can build a grand temple, why can you not build a school [196]? 

In fact, you will get far more money and support for a school than a temple [196]! Many philanthropic and secular organizations will happily donate to build a school because they see the immediate benefit it brings to society [196]. But because we are selfish and short-sighted, we hesitate [191, 200]. 

I am sorry to say this, but **we have leaders without vision.** [200] 

Our leaders do not look at the importance of our children, and they are not planning for our future [200]. They think only about their own bellies, their personal positions, and their prestige [201]. Their philosophy is not clean because it is constantly controlled by politics [201]. 

Let us give up this artificial, prestigious lifestyle [61]. If you are not looking ahead to who is going to maintain this mission tomorrow, you are not a leader [193]. 

Before I came to India, I sat down with all the devotees in Nigeria and challenged them: **“Look at my body. My body is old. If I give this society to you today, which of you is ready to handle it?”** [193] 

If you have built a million temples, opened a hundred departments, and initiated a billion disciples, but not a single one of them is spiritually qualified to represent you and carry on the pure lineage after you leave—you are an absolute, total failure [193]! Succession means everything, and huge numbers and grand facilities mean little if nobody is spiritually qualified to carry the lineage forward [193].

---

## **Śrīla Prabhupāda’s Standard of Boiling the Milk**

What is real success in Kṛṣṇa consciousness? [193] 

Success is not measured by how many congregation members we have, or how many grand facilities we own [193]. Success is: **How many of us are actually becoming pure devotees?** [193, 235] 

Śrīla Prabhupāda famously gave us a crucial instruction: **“Boil the milk.”** [193] 

We are very expert at getting the milk—preaching, distributing books, attracting people, and building new facilities [193]. But are we boiling the milk to make it thick and sweet [193]? Are we developing devotees, character, depth, and genuine qualifications [193]?

If you do not boil the milk, it will spoil and become useless [193]. We must not be satisfied simply with bringing new people; we must be satisfied with keeping the devotees and refining their character [193]. 

Prabhupāda clearly made his intentions known: *“If I can make even one pure devotee, I will consider my entire attempt a success.”* [193, 235] 

He designed everything in ISKCON—from college preaching and Life Membership to food for life, restaurants, farm communities, and Deity worship—for that single, unadulterated purpose: to make pure devotees [193, 235]. He was completely purpose-driven [193, 194]. 

Because he was purpose-driven, he could tackle all the obstacles and successfully establish this movement [175]. If we want to please him, we must boil the milk [183].

---

## **Establishing Self-Sufficient Devotee Communities**

A true devotee-centered future cannot exist only inside concrete city buildings [202]. We need self-sufficient communities [202]. 

And what does a community mean to Gurudeva [202]? It means: **the school is there, the farm is there, the cows are there, and the agricultural lands are there.** [202] 

I have never read anywhere in the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* that Kṛṣṇa fed His cows dry grass inside a concrete house [203]. Kṛṣṇa took the cows out to graze [203]. He walked with them in the forests of Vraja, and they ate fresh, green grass [10, 203]. We must return to this natural, Vedic lifestyle where schools, agriculture, and cow protection are integrated together [202, 203]. 

In Nigeria, we have acquired one hundred acres of land, and in Ghana, we are developing similar projects [201, 202]. We are not building these farms for material business; we are developing them as a direct, loving offering to Śrīla Prabhupāda because that is what he wanted [117, 202]. 

A genuine disciple’s only duty is to fulfill the desires of the spiritual master [202, 235]. We are pushing these schools and farms with all our energy to show the world that Kṛṣṇa is always present when His devotees cooperate with selflessness [187, 202].

---

## **Making Sincere Sacrifices for the Preaching Mission**

Many parents tell me: *“Maharaj, we want to start a school, but we are hesitant. We are afraid of the challenges, the lack of money, and the opposition from others.”* [205, 206] 

Why are you afraid? Fear of what [205]? 

Are you afraid because Kṛṣṇa is not there [205]? Kṛṣṇa is always there [202]! 

If you are not ready to face challenges, you will never make anything wonderful happen in this world [206]. You will remain babies forever [206]! 

Look at how our previous Ācāryas struggled to give us this sublime path [203, 204]. They sacrificed for devotees they would never meet, and we have inherited their mission [203, 204].

*   **Śrīpāda Rāmānujācārya** was persecuted so severely by the Shaivite king that they wanted to kill him [203, 204]. He had to flee his home and run to Melkote [204]. The king’s soldiers arrested and brutally killed all of his disciples; only one managed to escape [204]. 
*   **Śrīla Prabhupāda** made many attempts, underwent unimaginable struggles, and made the ultimate sacrifice to give us this society [184]. 

They made these extreme, life-threatening sacrifices, and today we are sitting comfortably in these beautifully decorated rooms enjoying the fruits of their labor [204]. 

But let me ask you a very direct question: **What is our own sacrifice?** [204] 

What are you risking for Kṛṣṇa [248]? Sincere sacrifice is our turn to give back, and it must go beyond just schools. What time, money, comfort, prestige, career opportunity, energy, or ambition are we willing to sacrifice for the devotees who will come after us [204]? Sincere schools and farms are just our major examples [202].

Sometimes, devotees think with a very narrow, business mind: *“Maharaj, why should I spend my hard-earned money and time to build a school? By the time the school is finished, my own children will have already graduated. What will I get out of it?”* [208] 

If you think with this business calculation, **you are not a devotee!** [208] 

A devotee never asks, *“What will I get out of it?”* A devotee only asks, *“What can I give?”* [208]

Think of the person who studied and discovered electricity [208]. He did not sit back and think, *“Oh, if I invent this, I will not personally get to enjoy the light bulb before I die.”* [208] He simply wanted to help humanity, and because of his selflessness, we are all sitting under bright lights today [208, 209]. 

We must have that exact same spirit of selflessness [176]. We must make sacrifices today so that the devotees of tomorrow have a safe harbor to protect their souls and continue the disciplic succession after we are gone [183]. 

Our entire journey through these pages has been a steady, progressive ascent of the soul. In the beginning, we learned that spiritual life begins only when we are willing to put our heads on the ground in full, unreserved surrender at the feet of the Guru [118]. We then learned how to wire our hearts directly to the powerhouse of his instructions, hearing him with undivided attention and serving his Vāṇī even in separation [118]. And now, in this final hour, we are called to make the ultimate, risk-taking sacrifice to protect the future of his mission and preserve the next generation of devotees [176, 184].

So let us come together, sit down on the floor, and discuss what to do [185]. Let us stop acting like babies, drop our selfish prestige, and make a glorious, risk-taking offering of sacrifice at the feet of Śrīla Prabhupāda and our spiritual masters [173, 184, 185]! This is where our journey back to Godhead is completed—not in some proud, independent achievement, but by returning directly to where we first began: with our heads on the ground, fully surrendered to the dust of their lotus feet [118].

## **ABOUT THE ACHARYAS & TEACHERS**

### **His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda**
His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda (revered eternally as the Founder-Ācārya of the International Society for Kṛṣṇa Consciousness) is the supreme spiritual father who delivered the ultimate gold of Gaudiya Vaishnavism to the modern world [14, 16]. At the advanced age of sixty-nine, carrying only forty rupees and a suitcase of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* translations, he boarded the cargo ship *Jaladuta* to cross the Atlantic and plant the seeds of bhakti on Western soil [14, 16]. His life was a manifestation of uncompromised compassion, translating and commenting on dozens of volumes of Vedic scriptures and establishing more than a hundred temples globally to provide shelter for all humanity [14].

### **His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami**
His Holiness Bhakti Tīrtha Swami (beloved spiritual master, pioneer preacher, and spiritual father) was an uncompromised spiritual warrior who served the mission of Śrīla Prabhupāda with absolute, fearless dedication [2, 11]. Born in Cleveland, Ohio, as John Favors, he graduated from Princeton University before dedicating his life to the disciplic succession [2, 11]. He took incredible, life-threatening risks to distribute books behind the Iron Curtain in the Soviet Union and war-torn Yugoslavia, and established flourishing devotee-centered communities, schools, clinics, and farms across West Africa [11, 12, 19]. His life was a testimony to selfless care, counseling thousands and devoting his last breath to protecting the bodies and souls of the Vaiṣṇavas [11].

### **His Holiness Bhakti Dhīra Dāmodara Swami**
His Holiness Bhakti Dhīra Dāmodara Swami is a sannyasi and initiating spiritual master within ISKCON, who travels tirelessly to preach the message of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* across India, Africa, and the world [1, 2]. As a disciple of HH Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, he represents the uncompromised standard of loving care, bold preaching, and intense sādhana that he received from his spiritual master [2, 11].
