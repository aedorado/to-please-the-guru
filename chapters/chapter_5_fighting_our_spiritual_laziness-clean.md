# Chapter 5: Fighting Our Spiritual Laziness

What is the greatest lack in our modern spiritual movement? 

If we look around honestly, what is missing is a deep, unshakeable dedication to the spiritual master. Today, there is a very prominent disease of disconnection. Many of us are physically present in the temple, but we are disconnected from the heart of our Guru. 

Let us look at a person who had a truly deep connection: my own spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami. I was fortunate to serve him for many years, and I saw his qualities firsthand. He did not live an artificial, superficial spiritual life. He communicated with his Guru, Śrīla Prabhupāda, every day through constant, submissive prayer and accountability [1, 2].

Every night, before Srila Gurudeva went to sleep, he would look at his diary to review the tasks he had planned for that day. Even though his services and activities were fully completed, he would meditate submissively, reporting his day directly to Śrīla Prabhupāda. He would examine his heart and ask his Guru if there was anything he had failed to execute, or if he had done everything exactly as his master wanted him to do. This daily review was his constant, living communication and accountability with his spiritual master.

This same uncompromised connection guided Srila Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami during challenging GBC leadership meetings. When a highly sensitive or political issue was being debated, he would not simply jump into the dispute or depend on his own intellect. Instead, he would step aside, go to the toilet, and pray submissively to Śrīla Prabhupāda, listening for guidance and asking how his master would address the issue if he were physically present [1, 2]. He would wait for the spiritual inspiration, and only then return to the room to present what was pleasing to his Guru.

During one particular committee debate regarding a school leadership program, the GBC was heavily discussing bringing in someone else to lead. Gurudev left the meeting, went to the toilet to pray, and Śrīla Prabhupāda told him no. He returned to the room and requested on his Guru's behalf that everyone take a break to pray and seek inspiration from Prabhupāda [1, 2]. When other leaders opposed him and wanted to push through the agenda, he stood his ground on spiritual principles. He calmly told them that they were free to do their own thing, but since he had prayed and received a negative instruction from Śrīla Prabhupāda, he would not compromise. He made it clear that his stand was not based on his own false ego, declaring that we are all disciples and all have the right to communicate with our Guru. 

That quality of uncompromised, prayerful loyalty is extremely rare today. 

---

### **The Disease of Laziness**

Srila Gurudeva used his massive intelligence to design a Kṛṣṇa consciousness that is sustainable—both materially and spiritually. When I started, he gave us a single, heavy word that has stayed with me to this day: **“lazy”**. He used that single word, but there are so many deep things inside it. He warned us that we are spiritually lazy [1, 2]. 

Because of our laziness, we cannot access the incredible wealth we have already been given. We have been given the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, which is the absolute solution to every problem in human life. Within Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books, he has packed the realizations of all the greatest ācāryas—Srila Jiva Goswami, Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura, Srila Sanātana Gosvāmī, and Srila Rūpa Gosvāmī. He put all these great sadhus into his "pot-pot" for us.

But because we are lazy, we don’t study Prabhupāda’s books properly. We read them superficially, and when we see nothing, we think we have exhausted the teachings [1, 2]. Then we start jumping here and there, “sadhu-hopping” to other movements and other speakers, looking for some fresh excitement. 

I must ask you: if your belly is completely full, will you go around looking for food? No! If your belly is full, you don't look for food anywhere. So if we are still searching outside, it is because we are spiritually empty. We have not actually digested the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. 

And when we do that, we look like a joke, and Gurudeva chides us: **“Lazy, lazy. Lazy. Lazy.”** [1, 2]

We say, *“Oh, I have read all of that, but there is nothing there... so I want another sadhu.”* Is that what we are doing? **“Lazy! Lazy, that is the problem. Laziness.”** 

Srila Gurudeva did not train us to be spiritual nomads. He trained us to be loyal—unshakably loyal—to the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and to Śrīla Prabhupāda.

---

### **Jñāna vs. Bhakti**

There is a dangerous direction many of us are taking. I see it every day. We are moving toward *jñāna*—dry, intellectual knowledge—instead of *bhakti*. 

Now, let us try to understand this clearly. Knowledge itself is not bad. Knowledge of the scriptures is an important part of our practice. But the little we read from the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is often used only as a knowledge aspect, not as *bhakti* [1, 2]. Why do we love *jñāna*? Because *jñāna* is very cheap. It is easy. It entertains our intelligence. It allows us to speak big, complicated philosophy, memorize Sanskrit verses, and debate with others to show how smart we are. 

But does dry knowledge change our character? No. 

When we operate only on the platform of dry knowledge, our daily dealings with each other become unpalatable. We speak high philosophy on the Vyasasana, but in our daily life, we are miserable, disturbed, political, and quick to find faults in other devotees [1, 2]. We carry hidden envy and territorial pride. That is the result of dry knowledge. It is unpalatable.

*Bhakti* is completely different. Bhakti does not care to entertain your intellectual ego. Bhakti goes straight to the heart and destroys all the nauseous things, the dirt, and the duplicity inside [1, 2]. 

And when that dirt is removed, what is left? Sweet, pure, and loving dealings with each other. 

A devotee who has real *bhakti* doesn’t need to advertise themselves. Their very presence is sweet. Anyone who comes into contact with them is immediately affected by their purity. They don’t play political games, they don’t fight for position, and they don’t mistreat others. 

My own spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, lived this sweet standard of *bhakti*. He was deeply personal and caring in his dealings, providing physical, mental, mystical, and spiritual protection to his followers [3]. Even on his deathbed, when he stopped spending money on his own medical treatment, he took the remaining donations and divided them among the devotees who were facing health issues. He did not give those funds to the temple for maintenance—he gave them for devotee care, because his nature since childhood was always to protect and care for others. That is the sweet fruit of *bhakti*.

---

### **The Transparent Postman**

How do we protect ourselves from falling into the trap of false prestige and pride? 

We must always remember: *“I am representing my spiritual master. I am representing his service.”* [1, 2]

Srila Gurudeva taught us a beautiful analogy. He said we are simply **postmen** [1, 2]. A postman is given a valuable, sealed parcel to deliver to a specific address. The parcel does not belong to the postman. His only duty is to carry it carefully and deliver it exactly as it is, without opening it, altering it, or claiming it as his own property [1, 2]. 

But what do we do? We get the Guru’s service, we get some position or authority, and we immediately try to open the parcel. We put ourselves in the center. We think: *“I am the doer. I am the one distributing these books, I am the one managing this temple, I am the master of this project.”* 

This is criminal. The moment you try to open the Guru’s parcel and claim ownership over the service, you cut your own spiritual roots [1, 2]. You begin to experience heavy anxiety, obstacles, and dry, exhausting struggles because you are trying to carry the Guru’s immense load on your own head. You do not have the strength to carry that load!

Let go of the need to be the boss. Keep the parcel closed. Deliver it exactly as Śrīla Prabhupāda gave it to us, and you will get a promotion. That promotion is spiritual strength and absolute protection [1, 2].

---

### **How to Pray on the Battlefield**

Let us pause here. We must understand how to actually pray on the battlefield of spiritual life. He taught us the importance of prayer—it is one of our nine limbs of bhakti, and we should do it every day [1, 2]. Sincere prayers should come from the heart, not just the lips. 

Let us look at two stunning prayers that we should carry in our hearts like spiritual armor.

#### **1. The Prayer of the “Foolish Beggar”**
This is a prayer written by Srila Gurudeva himself. He taught us to beg Kṛṣṇa every day [2]:
> *“My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, I am a beggar. I do not know how to beg. My fortune is that I have someone who begs for me.”*

When we are too fallen, too distracted, or too spiritually bankrupt to even know what to ask Kṛṣṇa for, this prayer is our lifeline. We are simply depending on the begging power of our Guru [2].

#### **2. The Armor of a Representative**
Before you go out to preach, manage, or perform any responsible service, you are stepping onto a battlefield. If you do not pray first, you will end up serving your own ego [1, 2]. Here is the prayer we should make [1, 2]:
> *“Gurudev, I am representing you. Let me not become the master. Let me not become the master. I better remain like a servant. I do not know what you have arranged out there on the battlefield today, but as you have arranged it, please also arrange for my safety, because I am representing you.”*

This prayer is not a cheap formality; it is our active shield. It removes the load of “doership” from our head and places our service under the absolute protection of the Guru [1, 2].

---

### **Our Weapon on the Battlefield**

We must wake up to the reality of where we are. We are not in a playground. We are on a **battlefield** [1, 2]. 

* **The Battlefield:** Your own consciousness [1, 2].
* **The Target:** Maya [1, 2].
* **Our Weapon:** Hearing [1, 2].

Every day, we are going out to fight. And if you step onto this battlefield without your weapon, you are not going to fight—you are going to get killed! [1, 2]

Chanting our rounds and hearing *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is not a fashion, and it is not an optional hobby. It is our ammunition. When we hear attentively, we are reloading our weapon. 

If you do not hear about Kṛṣṇa continuously, your mind will automatically start hearing about Maya. 

So let us stop being lazy. Let us stop offering Kṛṣṇa our dry, academic *jñāna*. Let us pray with intense, serious sincerity, and become simple, transparent postmen [1, 2].

---

### **Uncompromised Loyalty: The Yugoslavian Trial**

My beloved spiritual father, Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, showed us what it means to be unshakeably loyal under the most extreme, life-threatening trials. 

During the height of his book distribution in Eastern Europe under the communist regime, he was arrested by the secret police in Yugoslavia [2]. The situation was terrifying. Anyone distributing religious materials was treated as a dangerous criminal. He was taken to a dark police cell and surrounded [2]. 

A female officer opened the cell door, walked inside, and looked at him. She said: *“Do you know the offense you have committed? You are going to jail... However, I have the power to release you right now on one condition: you must enjoy with me.”* 

Think about the sheer weight of that test! He was alone, in a foreign land, facing a brutal prison sentence, and Maya offered him a cheap, immediate escape through sense gratification. But Gurudev did not hesitate for a fraction of a second. He looked her in the eye and said: 

*“I have many people with me here. My spiritual master is seeing me, and he is seeing you. You think none of my members are here, but my Guru is here, and he is watching everything.”* [2]

His unshakeable faith and pure surrender pierced straight through her. She was so completely shocked and terrified by his purity that she immediately opened the door and discharged him! [2] That is the power of uncompromised disciplic connection. Maya cannot stand before a disciple who refuses to play double-games.

---

### **Our Protective Boundaries**

This same protective power was there in his personal dealings with me. I remember when I was young, I wanted to initiate a massive project that I thought would bring incredible prestige, money, and glory to our preaching. In my young mind, I thought this was the ultimate service. 

But Gurudev looked at me with deep concern. He did not care about the money or the superficial prestige. He cared about my soul. He looked me in the eye and said: 

*“Stop the music! Stop! If you do this, your spiritual life will not last. You will not stand in spiritual life.”*

If I had gone ahead, it would have brought so much external success, but my spiritual foundation would have been completely ruined. He was ready to stop me, to block my path, to keep me protected within the safe, humble boundaries of disciplic service. He wanted things to be real, not an artificial show [3].

Let us drop our spiritual laziness. Let us stop looking for external excitement or dry intellectual validation. Let us surrender our hearts to the boundaries of the instructions, and experience the sweet, uncompromised safety of disciplic protection.

---

## **Chapter 5: Heart Check**

### **Am I Wearing the Spiritual Armor?**
Before stepping out into the world for your daily work, your family responsibilities, or your preaching service:

* Do you pray to represent your spiritual master submissively, or do you rush out unprotected, trying to carry the heavy load of doership on your own head?
* Do you chant your rounds with the humble awareness of a beggar, depending on the Guru's prayers, or has your Japa become a dry, mechanical ritual?

### **My Nightly Account**
Like Srila Gurudeva Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, who examined his diary every night to ask if his service was done exactly as desired:

* Before you go to sleep tonight, put away all devices, look into your heart, and ask yourself:
 1. *My dear Guru, did I serve your instructions with my full heart today, or did I let laziness and ego take over?*
 2. *In my dealings with other devotees today, did I speak with sweet, pure Bhakti, or was I critical, political, and unpalatable?*
