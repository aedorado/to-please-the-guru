# Chapter 20: Giving Beyond the Modes

If you want to know how to give charity, you must study the instructions of the *Bhagavad-gītā* [3]. Kṛṣṇa explains that everything in this material world is tinged by the three modes of nature, and our giving is no exception [3]. 

---

## **The Three Modes of Charity**

Let us try to understand these three modes of charity carefully [3].

*   **Charity in Ignorance:** This is charity given to an unqualified person, in an impure place, and at an improper time [3]. According to the Vedic literature, there is no recommendation for giving charity indiscriminately [3]. You cannot just throw your money anywhere and call it charity [3]. The suitable time to give charity is during a solar or lunar eclipse, at a holy place of pilgrimage, or at the end of the month to a qualified Brāhmaṇa or Vaiṣṇava [3]. The Vedas strictly condemn giving charity in the night—it is considered condemned charity because the result is not wonderful [3]. Giving money to support sinful activities like intoxication and gambling, or giving in an impure place like a cemetery, a hotel, or a bar, is in the mode of darkness and brings only suffering [3]. Similarly, if a person gives charity to a suitable person but without respect and without attention, that sort of charity is also said to be in the mode of ignorance [47].
*   **Charity in Passion:** This is charity performed with the expectation of some return, with a desire for fruitive results, or in a grudging mood where you later complain and repent: *“Why have I spent so much money in this way?”* [3, 61] Sometimes it is performed for elevation to the heavenly kingdom, or simply out of obligation at the request of a superior [3, 61, 62].
*   **Charity in Goodness:** This is charity given as a duty, without expectation of return, at the proper time and place, and to a worthy, qualified person engaged in spiritual activities [3, 75]. Indiscriminate charity is not recommended in the Vedic literature; spiritual perfection is always a consideration [76]. Therefore, charity is recommended to be given at a place of pilgrimage, on solar or lunar eclipses, or at the end of the month to a qualified Brāhmaṇa or a pure devotee [76, 77].

But beyond goodness, there is **transcendental charity.** [77] This is charity given directly to a pure devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa to facilitate and expand his mission [77]. When you beg a pure Vaiṣṇava with a humble mind to accept your offering for your own benefit, the result is 100% pure and takes you straight back to Goloka Vṛndāvana [55, 78]. You give this charity with begging: *“Please, kindly accept this for my benefit.”* [78] Because if a pure devotee refuses to take your charity, you cannot get purified [78]. With a humble mind, we must give charity to a pure devotee with begging and consider it our greatest fortune [78].

## **The Madman’s Cigarette**

To understand how Kṛṣṇa looks at our intentions rather than our external actions, let me share a practical history that happened to me personally [57, 58].

One day, I was passing in the street, and a madman was there [57]. Madman [57]. He smiled, and he was smoking [57]. When he saw me, he said, *“Oh my friend, can you take some cigarettes also?”* He offered me cigarettes [57].

Would he benefit? He is offering cigarettes to a devotee [58]. Will he benefit or not?

Will he benefit? Yes, he will benefit [58]! The action is in which mode? Ignorance [58]. But what is the intention? The intention is good [58]! Just the desire to give is suspicious [meaning: wonderful and worthy of notice] [59]! So he will benefit [59]. Even though it is a forbidden item, the desire to give something is very suspicious [59]. Kṛṣṇa will respond to our intention, not just our activities [86]. He sees what we are keeping behind; He doesn't see what we are giving Him [86].

## **The One-Rupee Receipt**

If there is motivation or pride behind our giving, it immediately becomes contaminated by passion and business mentalities [63, 64].

One day, somebody gave me a donation of exactly one rupee [81]. One rupee [81]! But do you know what he told me next? He said, *“Give me receipts.”* [81]

The receipt paper itself was more expensive than the one rupee he gave [81]! But he insisted, *“Give me receipts. I would show it to my wife that I am very charitable.”* [81]

Look at the cheating, business mentality [108]! This is charity in passion [61, 62]. They are doing business with Kṛṣṇa [64, 65]. They are using the Guru’s service strictly to purchase some material reputation and show off their prestigious position [121]. It is not pure; it is contaminated with desire [63].

## **The Stingy Lady and the Dry Palace**

In the *Brahma Bhairava* chapter, there is a historical narration of one lady who was very, very stingy [59]. Very stingy [59]. She never gave anything [59].

One day, a sadhu came by to beg from her [59]. She didn't want to give him anything, so she took a lump of dirt from the ground and put it on the begging bowl of the sadhu [59]. The sadhu left [59].

But look at the law of karma: because she had the desire to give *something*, in her next life she was promoted [59, 60]. She was born with a grand kingdom and a massive, beautiful palace [60]. But when she entered her palace, **there was no food inside!** [60] It was a grand palace, but there were no facilities, no water, and no food [60].

She was bewildered and inquired: *“I have chosen a nice place, but why are there no facilities?”* [60]

They told her: *“Because of the offense you committed. That's why you have a kingdom without facilities.”* [60]

Therefore, she was instructed to perform a wonderful sacrifice—she had to fast on the determined day of *Hari-vāsara* [the holy day of Lord Hari, Ekādaśī, which the transcript recorded as ‘karasa’ or ‘karas’] [60]. Once she fasted in the *Hari-vāsara*, everything became auspicious [60].

The desire to give something is important, yes [60]. But we must understand what is charity and how to give charity properly [60].

## **Paid Pujaris and the Corporate Mentality**

Whenever some personal interest or expectation of salary comes in, our devotional service is contaminated [63, 64].

I have seen devotees suffering [64]. They are suffering [64]. They are wearing saffron, they are doing Deity worship, but they are *paid* [64]. I am not speaking about outside people; I am speaking about inside our movement [64]. They are wearing saffron, they are worshipping Deities, but they are doing it for a salary [64].

Is that devotional service? No, it is not devotional service [64]! It is business [64]! They are doing business with Kṛṣṇa [65]!

I visited one temple, and I asked: *“What is the energy here? Why does it look like a company? The energy here looks like a corporate business.”* [65]

I was informed that most of the devotees there were paid, including the temple president [65]. That business energy was prominent, and it pervaded the whole temple area [65].

When you do something with a salary, with a pay, the mentality is completely different [109]. The moment salary comes inside, the business consciousness enters [109].

And do you know what happens? Some of these paid pujaris use this shocking word: **“Deity worship is boring.”** [65]

Krishna is boring [65]! It is true, they say like that [65]!

It is boring because there is no reciprocation between the Deities and the devotees [66]. Why there is no reciprocation? Because there is no love [66]! If there is no reciprocation, this is an abomination [66]. When one is offensive, he's making business with Kṛṣṇa, and Kṛṣṇa will turn His face away [66]. Therefore it is boring, it is fatigue, and it is a punishment [66].

A pujari must communicate with Kṛṣṇa because Deities are not wood [67]. They are not stones [67]. They are not marble [67]. Directly, the Supreme Kṛṣṇa is personally standing there [67]. So we should not serve Him with gloves [with formal, cold distance] [67]. In whatever way we can, we should do it with gratitude and love [68].

Let us be very clear: if a devotee is working in the temple because he doesn’t want to work outside, and due to some unavoidable family situations he receives some money from the temple for maintenance, that is different [86]. But that suggestion should not come from the devotee [87]. It must come from the temple management [87].

The temple management should see his utility and invite him: *“Please, I want you to do this, and we will support you because you have a family.”* [88] That is pure and auspicious [87]. But the moment the devotee start demanding and asking, *“Gurudev, this is too small for me now. I cannot take care of the house. Please increase my salary,”* that is business, and it is not helping us at all [30, 88].

## **"Sweet Heart" Prasādam**

Let us look at another area where we are introducing this business, mechanical consciousness: the way we distribute Kṛṣṇa’s prasādam [69].

In some of our temples, especially in India, we stand there and insult people [69, 70]! We shout at them: *“Come here! Sit!”* [69]

Is that charity? Insulting a person before you hand them prasādam [70]? And what is that prasādam we are giving them with so much shouting [70]? Not even a full prasādam of toffee—just a tiny scoop of *khichdi* [70]!

*Khichdi* is poor man’s food, food for beggars, and we give it in a very useless, degrading way [70]. We shout at the people who have come to Kṛṣṇa’s house [70].

That is not charity [70]! Charity should be given from the heart, from a **sweet heart**, so that the person feels deeply uplifted [71].

Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that the purpose of giving a gift is to develop love [71]. The physical object you give is not the real thing—the object is just the means [72]. The real thing is the heart’s desire to make the other person happy, which is transferred through the object [72]. When you help someone in a time of great need from a sweet heart, they can never forget you, and real love is developed [72, 73].

## **The Guru’s Assets and Yamarāja's Warning**

We must understand where we are and what we are doing [54].

Everything we possess in this material world is **stolen property**. [73] We are all thieves who have stolen everything from Kṛṣṇa [73]. By giving charity, we are not doing Kṛṣṇa any favor, and we are not doing the Guru any favor [73]. It is we who get purified [74]. Therefore, giving charity is compulsory for everyone to purify ourselves [74].

But if a person puts on the neck beads (*kaṇṭhī-mālā*) to pretend to be a devotee, and then takes or misuses Kṛṣṇa’s money and the Guru’s assets, **Yamarāja will personally remove the neck beads!** [56]

Yamarāja has told his servants: *“Those persons who are wearing the kaṇṭhī-mālā, don’t go near them. Never go to them.”* [56] But if someone puts on the neck beads and plays business games with Kṛṣṇa's and the Guru's assets, Yamarāja says: *“My servants will remove the neck beads, arrest him, and take him to hell where he will pay severely!”* [56]

For a minor offense, Kṛṣṇa may forgive you once [56]. But if you continue to cheat Him, Kṛṣṇa will personally use His **Sudarśana Cakra** to punish you [56]! Never play business games with the Guru’s assets [56].

## **Reactionary Charity**

When we give charity with some expectation—like giving grains to get to heaven—that is in the mode of passion, and it is partial [75].

In the beginning, we may have expectations [84]. We tell a child: *“Drink this bitter medicine, and I will give you a sweet.”* They drink it to get the sweet [85]. But when we become spiritual adults, we should know that we must take the medicine of bhakti without demanding any sweets [85].

Look at Dhruva Mahārāja [84]. In the beginning, he performed extreme austerities because he wanted a kingdom superior to his grandfather’s [84]. But when Kṛṣṇa appeared before him, he wept and said: *“My dear Lord, asking You for a kingdom was a great offense. I am very, very sorry. Please forgive me, and do not give me anything.”* [84] That is pure, non-calculating devotion [84].

## **Personal Giving and Association**

Today, we are living in a world where we think we are very advanced and very fast [79, 80]. We perform our charity through online transactions, QR codes, and bank transfers [79].

You can give through electronic business [80]. No problem, you can give like that [80]. But let us understand: **the feeling is not the same.** [80]

That is **impersonalism**. [80]

Personalism is different [80]. It is better when there is reciprocation and feelings [80]. When you come to the temple, you see the beautiful Deities, you take the dust of the holy place on your head, you associate with the devotees, you give your donation directly, and you take prasādam [80]. That association is what purifies us [81].

If you do everything alone through a screen, how will you get the purification [81]? We are missing so many things in the name of being very smart and very fast [81]. It is better if we come to the temple and give personally [81].

Let us throw away our dry, corporate calculations [3]. Give from a sweet heart, without expecting anything in return, and become a simple, non-calculating servant of the Guru [3, 71, 78].

Because after all, everything we possess in this world is stolen property [73]. We are all thieves who have stolen from Kṛṣṇa [73]. Our charity will not purify Kṛṣṇa, and our charity will not purify a pure devotee [74]. It is we who get purified [74]!
