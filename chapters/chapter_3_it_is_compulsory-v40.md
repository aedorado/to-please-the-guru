# Chapter 3: It Is Compulsory, Not Optional

Have you ever thought about why you are here? [120, 208] 

We are all looking for happiness, we are all looking for some peace, but we are looking in the wrong place [205, 210]. Look at a dog and a cat [145]. A dog sees something, but a cat will see and immediately close its eyes [145]. Which one is better—the dog or the cat [145]? The cat [145]! It means we should not be concerned with material things; we must be concerned only with Krishna [145]. Sincere practice means we are not concerned with what our material eyes are seeing, but with what our soul is hearing [145]. Just as we need our physical eyes all the time to see where we are going in this world, we need the Guru all the time to see the truth [119]. So having a Guru is compulsory [119]. 

Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and our great ācāryas tell us there is a formula [117, 118, 209]. And the very first step in that formula is not a matter of “if you like it” or “if you feel like it.” [119] 

It is compulsory [119]. It is not optional [119].

In the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*, Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī outlines sixty-four principles of devotional service [117, 118]. Sixty-four! [117] But do you know how he begins? [117, 118] He doesn’t begin with big, complicated rituals [118]. He begins right from the spiritual master [117, 118]. 

The first five items of those sixty-four are entirely about the Guru:

1.  ***Guru-padāśraya:*** Accepting the shelter of the lotus feet of a bona fide spiritual master [118].
2.  ***Kṛṣṇa-dīkṣādi:*** Receiving formal initiation from him [118].
3.  ***Śikṣaṇam:*** Being submissively instructed by him on how to discharge service [118].
4.  ***Obedience:*** Obeying his orders with absolute faith and devotion [118].
5.  ***Following the Ācāryas:*** Walking strictly in the footsteps of the great previous teachers [118].

Rūpa Gosvāmī says you *must* accept the shelter of a spiritual master [119]. It is a must! [119] If you are a human being, having a Guru is compulsory [119]. Except you are not a human being! If you are a human being, Guru is compulsory [119, 131]. 

The moment you accept initiation, your real journey back to Godhead begins [120]. It doesn’t end there—it only begins [120]!

## **The Sacred Vow of Initiation**

What is initiation? [120] 

Initiation is a **contract** [120]. 

When we get initiated, we accept three basic commitments:
*   You promise to keep the four regulative principles (no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, no illicit sex) [120, 151].
*   You promise to chant a minimum of sixteen rounds of the Hare Kṛṣṇa maha-mantra every day [120, 184].
*   You accept a fixed, dedicated service under his direction [120].

And once we accept that contract, the Guru goes to work [120, 121]. 

His business is to gradually, day by day, remove the thick, heavy curtain of *māyā* from your eyes so you can actually see Kṛṣṇa [120, 175]. 

Think of it like going to a corporate job [120]. When you arrive at the office, there is a register, isn’t it? [120] Your name is written there [120]. The company records when you resume work, how many hours you spend on the job, what you actually do, and when you close [120]. Your promotions, your bonuses, and your salary increases depend entirely on that daily record [121].

When you take shelter of a bona fide Guru, he is also keeping a register [120, 121]. He is monitoring you every day [121]. He is looking at your hours, your chanting, your service, and most importantly, **your consciousness** [120, 121]. 

Every day, the Guru is marking our progress, looking especially at our consciousness [121]. 

And what is he marking? He is checking to see how much of your material contamination is being removed [121]. If we are sincere, we don’t add new dirt [121]. We just keep reducing what is already there [121]. 

Our material ego, our pride, our lust, our envy—we keep letting the Guru cut them down [121, 200]. And one day, your contamination is reduced to **zero** [121]. That is the day you achieve perfection [121]. On that day, your life begins to shine like the brilliant midday sun [121]!

## **Sincere Effort to Receive Divine Mercy**

Some people sit back and say, *“Well, if Kṛṣṇa wants me to be a devotee, He will show me mercy. If my Guru wants to save me, he will just shower his grace on me. Why do I have to work so hard?”* [123, 124]

Laziness! You are not serious [124]! 

I warn you: **Mercy alone is partiality. Why not everybody? Why only you?** [123] We are all Kṛṣṇa's parts and parcels. His mercy is always available [123]. 

The truth is, **divine mercy is constantly, eternally available** [123, 125]. It is always there—not that it has to come, but it is already there, waiting for us [123]. But if you want to receive it, **you must straighten your hand to take it!** [123, 124]

If someone is offering you something, but you keep your hands tightly folded behind your back, how will you ever get it? [123] You have to straighten your hand [123]. 

Reaching out means putting in sincere, serious effort [122, 124]. It means doing something practical in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, taking the instructions on your head and executing them in your daily activities [124, 128]. 

But instead, we sit in our comfortable laziness and complain: *“Kṛṣṇa has never shown me mercy yet. If He shows me mercy, I will immediately become a pure devotee.”* [124] 

We wait for eternity, making excuses, while the treasure is lying right in front of us [124, 140].

## **The Beggar’s Gold**

Look at how we behave when mercy is offered to us. Let us look at a beautiful, striking history [125].

One day, Lord Śiva—the greatest of all Vaiṣṇavas—was traveling through the material world with his wife, Pārvatī [125]. As they were walking along, they passed a beggar sitting by the roadside [125]. 

Pārvatī looked at him and pleaded, *“Prabhu, please help him.”* [125]

Lord Śiva looked at him and turned his face away [125]. As they were coming back from their journey, they met the beggar again [125]. Pārvatī said, *“Prabhu, please help this man. Please.”* [125]

So Lord Śiva straightened his hand to the beggar [125]. He opened his hand, and dropped something precious right there [125]. 

The beggar looked at what was dropped in his hand and thought, *“What is this?”* [126] He did not value it, and simply gave it to somebody else [126]. That other person took the precious gift and instantly became wealthy [126]. 

Meanwhile, the beggar remained standing there, begging just as before [126]. 

Lord Śiva turned to Pārvatī and said, *“See. I gave it to him, but he gave it to somebody else. He is not right for it.”* [126]

We are exactly like that beggar [126]. We are getting so much, yet we still remain spiritually poor [126]. Kṛṣṇa has given us the Holy Name, *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, devotees, and the shelter of Guru [23, 118, 120, 151]. Yet we often fail to appreciate what we have been given [18, 126]. We remain poor because we don't know how to receive the mercy [123, 126].

Let us stop being roadside beggars [125, 126]. Let us straighten our hands, take the gold we have been given, and honor the contract of initiation with our very life and soul [119, 120, 123].
