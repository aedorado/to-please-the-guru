# Chapter 7: Purity is a Force

How do we intensify our sādhana? [111] 

Are you ready for it? Please, I know we have so many philosophies to speak [152]. We speak so many philosophies because we are very weak [152]. We are not strong—we are very weak! [152] That is why I am hesitating to speak, because I don't know how you will take it [152]. But because you asked me, I will try [152]. To intensify our sādhana means putting extra effort into our practice [152]. You want to put extra effort into our practice; that is how to intensify and upgrade our practice [152]. 

We often hide behind big, complex philosophies because we are actually very weak on the practical front [152]. We speak lofty theories to mask our internal lazy struggles [152]. But the fundamental principles of Kṛṣṇa consciousness are themselves a sufficient, perfect process to awaken pure love of Godhead [152]. Simply following the four regulative principles, hearing, chanting, and doing our services—this is a unique, perfect principle [152]. 

Look at the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* of Sri Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu [152]. Just those eight prayers alone! Only eight verses! [152] If we understand them carefully and apply them, we can become pure human beings [152]. The *Śikṣāṣṭaka* takes us from exactly where we are—covered in dirt—all the way to the highest stage of *prema*, pure love [152]. 

*Ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam*—cleansing the dust from the mirror of the heart [152]. That is the beginning [152]. But to get that cleansing, we have to be serious [152]. We have to stop making excuses [166].

## **Drinking Poison with a Smile**

There is a very prominent contamination we are introducing into our society today [164]. We are trying to run a spiritual movement using material abilities and academic qualifications [164]. 

But let me ask you a very direct question: **If you drink poison nicely, with a good smile, will you not die?** [164] 

If you drink it very politely, with first-class manners, and a beautiful smile on your face—will the poison say, *“Oh, this person is so nice, let me not kill him”*? No! [164] You will still die! [164] 

In the exact same way, this is a spiritual movement [164]. We cannot manage or expand this movement using material qualifications alone [164]. It is not sufficient [164]. This movement is based strictly on **purity** [164]. 

Śrīla Prabhupāda boldly declared: **Purity is the force!** [164] 

He did not say academic degree is the force [164]. He did not say money is the force [164]. Purity is the force, and purity means Kṛṣṇa consciousness in full, undivided practice [164]. 

It is the spirit that maintains the matter; matter can never maintain the spirit [164]. If we make our spiritual life very strong by good sādhana, then everything else—our management, our preaching, our families—will follow nicely and become easy [135]. But if we are spiritually sick, then everything else will be a struggle, a speculation, and a guesswork [133, 136].

## **Intensifying Our Practice on Special Days**

In his purports to the *Bhagavad-gītā* (9.14), Śrīla Prabhupāda mentions that there are certain days which are called **“determined days.”** [152] These are days like Ekādaśī and Janmāṣṭamī [152]. 

Why are they called determined? [152] 

Because they are serious [153]. They are serious because these specific days have an immense, unimaginable spiritual potency [153]. And to realize and capture this potency, we have to follow what is necessary: we must perform austerity, we must fast, and we must hear and chant more [153, 157].

But what do we do? We have manufactured so many clever philosophies to avoid the austerity! [153] 

We look at our calendar and we carefully, philosophically bypass the sacrifice [153]. Srila Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura writes in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata* that just two days—just two determined days in a month—are sufficient to send anyone back on the path to Godhead [153]. 

What are those days? The Ekādaśī days [153]. 

But instead of taking advantage of them, we eat more on Ekādaśī days than on ordinary days! [154] We make elaborate feasts of potato, sago, and nuts, stuffing our bellies until we can barely move [154]. The entire purpose of Ekādaśī is defeated! [154] We are defeated! [154] 

Where did Ekādaśī come from? [155] 

The *Padma Purāṇa* describes this history [155]. One day, Lord Kṛṣṇa went to visit Yamarāja, the lord of death [155]. While He was there, Kṛṣṇa heard a horrific, groaning cry of agony [155]. The sound was coming from the hellish inhabitants being tortured by Yamarāja’s assistants for their sins [155]. 

Kṛṣṇa became deeply concerned and full of sorrow [155]. He was thinking seriously: *“I am suffering like this, what should I do? I must find a way to help these poor conditioned souls get My mercy easily.”* [155] 

Thinking and thinking about this, Kṛṣṇa went to sleep to get a solution for the problem [156]. While He was sleeping, a powerful demon named Mura Asura came to fight Him [156]. Mura Asura came there and started talking: *“I will kill you because you are running away from fighting! You are sleeping, I will kill you!”* [156] 

As he was talking like that, a female personality came out from Kṛṣṇa’s body and killed the demon Mura [156]. Kṛṣṇa woke up and saw the lady standing there. *“Who did this?”* He asked [156]. She replied, *“It is me. The demon was trying to disturb you, so I killed the demon.”* [156] 

Kṛṣṇa was so pleased that He told her, *“Because today is the eleventh day of the month, your name is Ekādaśī [156]. Your duty is to chase away the Papa Purusha (the personification of sin) and give people a chance to get My mercy easily.”* [156] 

The Papa Purusha realized he had nowhere left to run, so he came back and begged Kṛṣṇa for shelter [156]. Kṛṣṇa told him, *“She will appear two times in a month [156]. So on those days, you can enter inside grains, and she will not disturb you there.”* [156] 

This is why eating grains on Ekādaśī is **extremely dangerous** [156]. It is so dangerous that Sri Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu recommended that a devotee should not eat grains on Ekādaśī—even in a dream! [156] 

On Ekādaśī, we must design our lives in such a way that we have more time to chant more rounds [156]. Sixteen rounds is the minimum prescribed standard—the “minimum property” Śrīla Prabhupāda gave us for daily maintenance [156]. But on Ekādaśī, we should take advantage and increase our chanting [156]. Don't chant only sixteen—sixteen is too small, especially on Ekādaśī! [156] We should go far beyond the minimum; we should double our rounds [156, 157]. 

And that requires reducing what we talk the whole day [157]. Reduce it, make sure it is within only Kṛṣṇa consciousness [157]. Let our speech be within Kṛṣṇa consciousness, not outside [157]. Especially on Ekādaśī, we should try to control our speech so that we don't talk anything against any devotee [157]. Never! [157] If you want to say something, let it be positive—a glorification, an appreciation [157]. Find a way to talk good about other devotees, and spend the day reading about the previous ācāryas and appreciating the hard work they did to establish a system that is saving our lives [157].

## **Engaging the Mind in Service**

Let me share a beautiful pastime of how my spiritual master, Srila Gurudeva, celebrated Janmāṣṭamī [157]. It is a lesson in how to occupy the mind so that Maya has no way to enter [157, 159]. 

When we were new in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, Srila Gurudeva—who was our Regional Secretary at the time—designed a very powerful meditation for Janmāṣṭamī [157, 159]. He literally **tricked our minds** so that we forgot about the physical struggle of fasting [159]. 

He gave us two mandatory assignments for Janmāṣṭamī:

1.  **The Chapter Narration:** Every devotee was assigned exactly one specific chapter from the *Kṛṣṇa* book [157]. We had to read it, condense it, and stand before all the senior devotees to narrate the entire chapter from memory! [157, 158]
2.  **The Personal Letter:** We had to sit down and write a deeply personal, confidential letter to Lord Kṛṣṇa [158]. 

Do you know what happened? [159] 

The entire temple was in an absolute state of spiritual absorption [159]. We were so busy thinking about our chapters and wondering, *“How will I write this letter to Kṛṣṇa? Will He reply? If He replies, how will I get the reply?”* that nobody even remembered the hunger or the fasting! [158, 159] 

Even the devotees who were cooking for the massive festival—before they could even touch a pot, they had to sit and read their assigned chapters [159]. While they were standing over the hot stoves, stirring the preparations, they were silently memorizing their pastimes [159]. 

The cooking was first-class because there was no gossip, no talking, and **no animosity**! [159] Everyone was simply consumed by the thoughts of Kṛṣṇa [159]. 

That is real Janmāṣṭamī [157]. If we plan our Kṛṣṇa consciousness like this, we will soon be there [159]. Very, very soon we will be there [159].

## **Chanting Sincerely and Audibly**

How should we chant our Japa? [160] 

Śrīla Prabhupāda, drawing from the commentaries of Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura, gives us the ultimate key: **Chant nicely, which means distinctly and loudly!** [160]

Why loud and distinct? [160] 

Because when you shout at someone, they are forced to give you their attention [160]. Chanting loudly and distinctly is literally **shouting at your own distracted mind to sit down and listen!** [160] It forces the ear to hear, and when the ear hears, the mind cannot wander off to Maya [160]. 

We must chant clearly while our body is still fit and healthy [160]. At the time of death, your bodily functions will be completely in disorder [160]. You will be bewildered and in pain [160]. You may not be able to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra very distinctly at that moment [160]. 

But Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura reveals a secret: If you have practiced chanting loudly and distinctly during your life, then at the time of death, Kṛṣṇa Himself will look at you and feel: **“This person has called My name clearly throughout their life. It is now My personal duty to give them all protection.”** [161] 

What a beautiful, comforting promise! [161] Kṛṣṇa takes it as His personal obligation to save you because you called Him clearly [161].

## **First Japa, Then Management**

Let us be very clear about our priorities: **Sādhana is our primary duty.** [67, 162] Services come *after* Sādhana [162]. 

If we want to be a good book distributor, we must chant our rounds first [162]. Otherwise, it is just ordinary selling of books, not a spiritual activity [162]. We must chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra distinctly and clearly, then book distribution becomes a spiritual activity [162]. It is the same with everything else we do in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [162]. One is not qualified to cook for Kṛṣṇa without chanting, and one should not do Deity worship without chanting [162]. Our consciousness has to be purified to do what is offerable to Kṛṣṇa [162].

But today, we are managing in Japa—it is wrong! [162] That is not what Śrīla Prabhupāda gave to us [162]. Managing in a class is wrong! [162] Prabhupāda gave us distinctly clear activities: a time for Japa, a time for classes, a time for book distribution, and a time for cooking [162]. 

If you do not chant your rounds properly *before* you step out to manage, you are going to manage the devotees for your own selfish benefit and prestige [162, 164]. A good leader manages people for *their* benefit [162]. If you chant sincerely first, you will receive the spiritual potency from your Kṛṣṇa consciousness to lead and manage the devotees purely for their spiritual benefit [162, 164]. A good devotee will be a good manager because his practice makes him good, so he will manage devotees for the benefit of devotees [162, 163].

Purity is our only strength [164]. Let us take our Sādhana seriously, shout at our distracted minds, and straighten our hands to receive the mercy [156, 160, 164].
