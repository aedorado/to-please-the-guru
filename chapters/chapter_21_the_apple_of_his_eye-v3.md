# Chapter 21: The Apple of His Eye

To satisfy Lord Kṛṣṇa is our only business [169]. With our services, with our activities, and in everything we do, our sole motivation must be the desire to please the Lord [169]. That is selflessness [169]. The moment the desire for satisfying ourselves enters, it becomes selfishness, and we can go on performing activities for millions of years without ever being successful in Kṛṣṇa consciousness [169]. 

How do we please Him? Narada Muni reveals that the Supreme Lord, Janārdana, is very satisfied with His devotee when the devotee greets other people with tolerance, mercy, friendship, and equality [170, 171]. 

In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (4.31.19), this divine formula is declared:

> *dayayā sarva-bhūteṣu*
> *santuṣṭyā yena kena vā*
> *sarvendriyopaśāntyā ca*
> *tuṣyaty āśu janārdanaḥ* [167, 168]

**Translation by Śrīla Prabhupāda:**  
**“By showing mercy to all living entities, being satisfied somehow or other, and controlling the senses from sense objects, one can very quickly satisfy the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Janārdana.”** [168]

---

## **The Piece of Apple**

What is mercy? What is compassion [171, 172]? 

If we look at our own history, we must ask: how did our supreme preceptor, Śrīla Prabhupāda, preach [171, 175]? He did not convince us through dry, academic philosophy [171]. He did not conquer our hearts by showing off his intellectual scholarship [171]. He convinced us by his personal, sweet behavior [171]. It was his mercy [171]. It was his compassion [171]. 

In the early days, a typical lecture by Śrīla Prabhupāda was very brief—only twenty minutes [171]! But at the end of the lecture, he would personally share a small piece of apple with everyone in the room [171]. 

That simple, sweet gesture—that piece of apple—is what made people surrender and become devotees [171]. It was his compassion, not the philosophical complexity, that entered their hearts [171]. The Lord is pleased when we greet others with that same spirit of mercy, friendship, and tolerance [171].

---

## **Character is Power**

To show mercy to all living entities means we must preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness [174, 185]. But we must understand what true preaching actually is [173, 175]. 

Preaching is not a drama [175]. It is not a stage show where you learn to act, come to the stage, and make a demonstration [175]. True preaching is the manifestation of Vaisnava character [173]. 

**“A preacher is one who has character. It is character that preaches, not philosophy. Knowledge does not preach; knowledge cannot transform somebody, but character can transform. Character preaches—that is a preacher. Therefore, knowledge is not power—character is power.”** [173, 174]

We talk endlessly about mercy, yet we struggle with basic, gentle Vaisnava dealings [172, 174]. Sometimes, Hare Kṛṣṇa devotees cannot even greet each other nicely when they meet in the temple [172]! 

Look at the tiny ants [172]. Have you ever seen an ant pass another ant on a wall without greeting [172]? When two ants meet, they stop and kiss each other before they pass [172]. But a Hare Kṛṣṇa devotee will often only greet you if they want something from you [172]! 

Why are we suffering in this movement? We suffer because we are duplicitous and still deeply attached to our dirty, sinful habits [172, 173]. We want to speak big philosophy to others, but we fail to apply tolerance, mercy, and friendship in our own daily interactions [171, 172, 173]. 

Śrīla Prabhupāda notes that it is the duty of an advanced devotee on the *madhyama-adhikārī* platform—the second stage of devotional perfection—to act in accordance with this verse [174]. A *kaniṣṭha-adhikārī* does not have the Vaisnava character to affect others because their hearts are still empty of purity [174]. A *madhyama-adhikārī* bhakta has character, and because they are free of duplicity, their only interest is to help and liberate all conditioned souls [174, 175].

---

## **Relieving His Anxiety**

If purity is the force that transforms hearts, how does a disciple please the spiritual master [175, 177]? 

Śrīla Prabhupāda once explained: **“The duty of a disciple is to relieve the spiritual master of his anxiety. A disciple should never be a liability or a drain on the Guru. But these days, we are liabilities. We are not disciples. The bona fide disciple's absolute duty is to relieve the spiritual master of his anxiety.”** [177]

How do we relieve his anxiety? We do it by supporting his preaching, facilitating his mission, and taking active risks to expand his service [177, 189]. 

Pure devotees make incredible, life-threatening sacrifices to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness [175]. Traveling from country to country is not a comfortable vacation; dealing with embassies, visas, and physical hardships is full of intense anxiety [175]. Yet they do it because their hearts bleed out of compassion for the conditioned souls who are suffering all over the world [175, 179]. 

Understanding what the spiritual master wants and executing it is what makes a true disciple [189]. Our supreme preceptor, Śrīla Prabhupāda, noted that all the great spiritual leaders had established their centers in the Holy Dham of Mayapur, and he expressed his own deep desire to have a special center there as well [189]. A genuine disciple does not sit back and wait; they take that desire of their spiritual master on their head and actively manifest it as a loving offering [189]. Kṛṣṇa is extremely pleased when He sees devotees dedicating their lives to supporting the mission of His pure representative [189].

---

## **The Divine Formula: Forget the Problem**

Many devotees are constantly overwhelmed by personal problems, health issues, bad karma, and material anxieties [179, 180]. They spend their days meditating on their sickness, or complaining about what they lack [180, 181]. 

**“If you continue to think of your problem, you will get problems continuously, because that is what you are meditating upon! When the problem becomes your deity, it will never go away, because you are always calling the problem to come. Forget about the problem, and the problem will forget you! This is a divine formula.”** [179, 180, 181]

Stop meditating on your bad health, your financial struggles, or your material anxieties [180, 181]. Instead, throw your mind and your intelligence into planning how to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness and how to help others become free of their anxieties [178, 181, 185]. 

To preach, we must first be satisfied ourselves in our own Kṛṣṇa consciousness [178]. We cannot distribute frustration to others; we must distribute the process of becoming free from anxiety [178]. 

When your mind is fully engaged in planning how to make Kṛṣṇa consciousness easy for others, you are instantly transported to Goloka [185, 186]. Your mind is absorbed in His service, and Kṛṣṇa Himself smiles with you [181, 186]. And when Kṛṣṇa is smiling, that joy immediately floods your own heart, and you automatically know that your service has been accepted [186, 187].

---

## **Expanding the Director's Business**

When you forget your own selfish anxieties and spend your life planning how to reclaim lost souls, you become **“the apple of Kṛṣṇa's eye.”** [179, 183] 

Kṛṣṇa can never forget a devotee who is deeply concerned with supporting His mission [183]. In fact, He declares that the highest opulence of His divine character is His eagerness to reclaim the conditioned souls [182]. 

**“When you support that mission, Kṛṣṇa becomes your personal supporter. Why? Because you are doing His duty! It is completely natural. If you are working very hard in an office, your director will like you and promote you, because he sees you are expanding his business. If you are expanding Kṛṣṇa’s business, your promotion back to Godhead is guaranteed.”** [183]

---

## **The Art of Association**

How do we maintain this standard of satisfaction when surrounded by the whimsical, disturbing behaviors of others in community life [190, 191]? 

The speaker warns that we should not allow the misbehavior of others to become our meditation [191]. If someone behaves foolishly, and we have the opportunity to advise them, we should do so affectionately [191]. If we cannot, we look for someone close to them to address it [191]. But if nothing works, we must immediately remove our mind, focus on our own early-morning Sādhana, and continue our service [191]. 

**“We should learn how to take all the good things from people and leave the bad things with them. That is the practice.”** [193]

Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī categorized the different grades of devotees so we would know exactly how to relate with everyone without committing offenses [192]. If we are strong in our early morning Sādhana, the material disturbances of others will simply bounce off our chests [193]. But if we are weak, we will be affected by everything [193]. 

Let us stop treating Kṛṣṇa consciousness as a cheap, mechanical show [138, 148]. Let us build Vaisnava character, relieve our spiritual master's anxieties, expand Kṛṣṇa's business, and become the apple of His eye [173, 177, 183].
